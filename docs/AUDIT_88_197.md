### The six most consequential FAILs

**1. A factual claim contradicted by the paper's own table and figure (items 124, 192; S5).**
`main.tex:534–536`: *"The excess grows with window width, as a quantity reflecting a real
temporal spread of evidence would, and this experiment does not separate that growth from a
widening of whatever else varies with the window."* Repeated in Limitations,
`main.tex:770–772`. The excess **does not grow with window width**. From `numbers.tex` and
`experiments/e12_integer_period/result.json`, printed in Table 2 and drawn as bars in Fig. 4:

| width | 5000 µs | 10000 µs | 14996 µs | 20000 µs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| excess | 212.6 | 195.2 | **183.6** | 246.8 |

Three of the four widths run *downward* and the fourth jumps. The sentence is false against
the numbers on the same page, and Fig. 4 shows the contradiction to any reader who looks at
the bars. The same error is in the experiment record, `e12_integer_period/README.md`
("Limits", 2nd bullet: *"The absolute excess grows with window width"*), which is where it
was imported from. This is the single highest-severity finding: it is the kind of error a
reviewer finds in thirty seconds by reading the figure the sentence points at.

**2. Table 2 reports the same condition twice with different numbers, unexplained
(items 105, 112, 130; G10).** Table 2's top block, row *"Night, 14996 µs"*, reads
1133 frames / sd 229.5 / null 93.4 / excess 208.8 / frac 0.9179 (`e11.../result.json`, arm
`ALL`). Table 2's bottom block, row *"14996 µs (1.4996 p)"*, reads 1134 / 206.6 / 93.0 /
**183.6** / 0.9012 (`e12.../result.json`, key `14996`). Same sequence, same nominal window
width, same (all) pixels — and a 25.2 µs (12 %) difference in the headline quantity plus a
one-frame difference in the sample. Neither the caption, the body, nor the supplement says the
two are computed differently (the top block uses each frame's own published `[a,b]`, the
bottom a fixed width recentred on mid-exposure; the frame counts prove they differ). A reader
cannot tell which number is the result. This breaks attribution of the headline.

**3. The random-box control is mis-reported in Table 2 and absent from the abstract
(items 112, 115, 126, 130; S5).** Two separate defects on the paper's most important control:

- Table 2 row 3, *"Night, random boxes"*, prints its frame count as `\sigmaEvtFramesNight`
  = **1133** (`main.tex:373`). The measurement used **1120** frames:
  `experiments/e09_per_object_evidence_time/zurich_city_09_a.json` gives
  `"n_frames_with_rand": 1120` beside `"frames_used": 1133`. There is no
  `\framesRandBoxes` macro; the row reuses the wrong one. A stated sample size that is not
  the sample size is the kind of error that costs a reviewer's trust in every other cell.
- The abstract (`main.tex:46–71`) reports the flicker exclusion, the matched random pixel
  exclusion and the integer-period arms, and is silent on the random-box arm. The body's
  correction — *"an excess of 310.5 µs --- larger than the annotated boxes' … Nothing here
  identifies the dispersion as specific to objecthood, and we make no such claim"*
  (`main.tex:445–455`) — is on p. 4 and appears in neither the abstract, the introduction,
  the contribution bullets nor the conclusion. Three of the four most-read locations carry
  the un-bounded reading.

**4. `σ_c = 0.4973` px is shipped from an estimator the repository has retracted, and E13's
two replacement facts are omitted (items 106, 115, 165, 192; S5).** `main.tex:653–654` and
`main.tex:772` report *"`σ_c = 0.4973` px from the third difference against `0.6053` px from
the second"*. Those two values come from the MAD variance at
`experiments/e08_label_noise/e08_label_noise.py:67–68`
(`(np.median(np.abs(z - np.median(z))) * 1.4826) ** 2`). `experiments/e13_difference_ladder/README.md:24–30`
retracts that route:

> *"The first run used a MAD-based variance and returned **bit-identical** sigmas for the full
> and half rate at k=2 and k=3 (0.6053 and 0.4973), which cannot happen with different sample
> counts. The cause is in the data, not the code: **DSEC-Det stores `x`, `y`, `w`, `h` as
> integers** … so box centres lie on a 0.5 px lattice, and a MAD on lattice-valued data locks
> onto discrete values."*

and at `:60–64`: *"it was tight by luck … **Any number taken from that first run should be
discarded.**"* Stated precisely: the *value* survives — E13's converged difference ladder
gives `σ_total → 0.4974` px, matching to four figures — but the *method* behind the number in
the paper is one the repository says not to use, its four-significant-figure precision is
unsupported (E13: *"worth about two decimal places, not three"*), and the two facts E13
explicitly labels **"worth stating in the paper"** (`e13/README.md:66–69`) appear nowhere in
`main.tex` or `supplement.tex`:

> *"The DSEC-Det boxes are integer-valued, so the annotation carries a **0.144 px quantization
> floor** that no method can go below. And the remaining annotation noise, **0.476 px**, is
> what a temporal estimate has to work against: at the median object speed measured in E08
> (40 px/s), it corresponds to **11.9 ms** of timing."*

That last figure is the paper's own §7.3 design argument stated more sharply than the paper
states it, and it is sitting unused in the repository. E13 and E03 are the only two experiment
directories with no trace in either document (verified by grepping `main.tex` and
`supplement.tex` for every identifier in both). E03's absence is correct — it is a
dataset-availability planning record. E13's is not.

**5. §7 is 2.5 of 8 body pages and its result table is six rows of `[unmeasured]`
(items 122, 123, 134, 160, 184, 187).** §7 (pp. 6–8) introduces roughly twenty symbols
(`k_P`, `c_P`, `w_P`, `c_G`, `w_G`, `τ_max`, `δ̂_i`, `λ_att`, `A_j`, `C_j`, `Δ(w)`,
`τ̂_naive`, the Frisch bracket, `AP^sync`, `AP^bias`, `Δ_clock`, `b̂_P`, `σ_τ` …), a
cross-fitting protocol, a Holm correction, a chord-term subtraction and a re-binning
discipline. Its output is Table 3, in which all six rows are `[unmeasured]`. One subsection
(§7.3, the label noise floor) is measured. Every reference paper's page-6-to-8 material is
result-carrying (FVD Tables 2–4; TB §4.2–4.5; BD §4.3–4.6).

**6. Figure 1 shows the premise, not the contribution (items 111, 167; and a legibility
defect in Fig. 3).** The only full-width figure, on p. 2, is the DSEC exposure survey —
correct in kind (evidence, "no model is run") but it shows the *cause's* distribution and
never the effect. The contribution's figure, Fig. 2, is column-width on p. 5. FVD's Figure 1
carries the two metric values in its caption and is the whole argument; TB's real Figure 1 is
its Table 1 on p. 3. Separately, **Fig. 3's right panel is not legible**: the four x-tick
labels collide, rendering as `night 100 Hz1 37 Hz1 00 Hz1 37 Hz`, and the `null p95` /
`null median` annotations overprint the dotted and dashed null lines at the right edge.
(Verified by rendering `figs/fig3_day_night.pdf` at 400 dpi; the same collision is present in
`main.pdf` p. 5.)

### Number provenance — checked, and where it is thin

Every load-bearing macro in `numbers.tex` was traced to the experiment named in its comment.
**41 of the 47 headline values are confirmed against a machine-written output file**, several
by independent recomputation from the raw DSEC files or by re-running the script (E00's
21 142 frames / 6866 pinned / p66 = 2999 recomputed from the 18 `e_*.txt` exposure files; E06's
390 118 boxes / 6693 tracks / 70 379 distinct `t` / 5.54 per value recomputed from all 60
`tracks.npy`; E08's `result.json` reproduced byte-identically by rerun; E09/E10/E11/E12 all
matched against their JSONs). Nothing in the paper was found to be invented.

Three provenance weaknesses, none of which changes a number but each of which a reviewer
demanding an artefact would hit:

1. **Six values live only in a hand-written README with no output file behind them**:
   `\excessMaskPix` (14 334) and `\lockedSensorPct` (4.67) — `e11/README.md:16` only, and
   `4.67 % = 14334/307200` checks out arithmetically; `\letTemporalHits` (6) and
   `\letAxisHits` (0) — `e07/README.md:16,29`, the second being a human judgement that
   `e07/README.md:38–42` partly undercuts by recording one returned paper as unread; the five
   E05 masking macros; and the E02/E04 macros. `\labelAcfWhite` (−0.740) is likewise
   README-only but was reproduced on rerun (−0.7399).
2. **Six experiment directories contain no script**: `e02`, `e04`, `e10`, `e11`, `e12`,
   `e13`. E11's mask construction ("Rayleigh `p ≥ 1e-3` at 100 Hz, mask built from a 6 s
   slice, every 4th event") cannot be regenerated from the repository, and E12's — the
   decisive control — cannot either, which is why the 14996 µs discrepancy of Summary FAIL 2
   cannot be resolved without re-implementing it.
3. **E09's positional and temporal-persistence controls have no output file at all.**
   `e09/README.md:93–147` reports a between-track/within-track sd split, a 20.56× ratio, a
   lag-1 of 0.471 and an 8×8/16×16/32×32 cell-mean table; `pos_run.log` contains only the same
   summary block as `night_run.log`. None of those numbers enters `numbers.tex` or the paper,
   so nothing published depends on them — but the directory reads as though it does.
