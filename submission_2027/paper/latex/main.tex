\title{Temporal-Support Identifiability\\
in Event-Camera Detection Evaluation Pipelines}
\author{Anonymous CVPR submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An event-camera detection benchmark scores each sample at one nominal timestamp, while a recurrent detector forms its prediction from a history whose length the benchmark never records. We formalize the missing requirement as \emph{temporal-support identifiability}: the variables a protocol records with a score must determine the temporal evidence behind every scored prediction. Because this is a uniqueness property, one controlled pair of runs with identical records and different supports refutes it, and we construct that pair on the released SSM-ViT evaluation. With frames, weights, labels, evaluator and evaluated timestamps fixed, moving only the chunk boundary of the streaming evaluation changes the recurrent history behind the same \ssmPairedN{} frames from 1--4 to 17--20 windows and changes the base checkpoint (S5-B) by \ssmPairedMapAllBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} mAP over all labeled boxes and by \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset, with a graded response at intermediate shifts and an exact zero at zero shift. The same intervention leaves RVT within \rvtBoundCarryMax{} mAP while its state is carried and moves it by \rvtBoundResetS--\rvtBoundResetB{} mAP once the state is reset, so the effect follows the reset policy and not the architecture. The constructive counterpart is Support-Conditioned AP, which scores every checkpoint from the same reset state at a declared history of $H$ windows. On five released Gen1 checkpoints scored on the same \lhNFrames{} frames, S5-B is fourth in the pooled score and first at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$, RVT-\texttt{b} overtakes it between $H=\lhCrossLo$ and $H=\lhCrossHi$, three pairwise gaps reverse sign between the pooled score and $H=1$ with both intervals excluding zero, and Kendall's $\tau$ against the pooled ordering is at most \lhTauHTwentyone{} for $H\le21$. Audits on Gen1 and DSEC show that input timing and label-acquisition timing are left implicit by the same timestamp. Five fields recorded with the labels make the support behind a score identifiable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Two recurrent detectors evaluated at the same nominal timestamp are not comparable in time unless the history each one actually used is also identified.

An event-camera detection benchmark defines a sample by a nominal timestamp. A prediction $\hat{s}$ is scored against the ground truth $s^{*}$ carried at query time $t$ through a function $m(\hat{s},s^{*})$ of position, shape and class. Beyond selecting the label at $t$, that function contains no variable for the temporal evidence behind either argument, so any temporal discrepancy enters the score disguised as displacement.

A recurrent detector does not read only the instant the benchmark names. It computes $\hat{s}$
from an input window and from state carried across earlier windows. The label it is
compared against comes from an exposure of finite width, an annotator or tracker working on the
exposed image, and a geometric transfer into another sensor's frame. The evidence on both sides
is an interval, while the protocol records an instant.

We use four terms. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant at which the protocol pairs a prediction with a label. The \emph{effective temporal support} is the past that can influence the prediction, including state carried by recurrence. The \emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired. The \emph{protocol record} is the set of variables a benchmark specifies and a paper reports with a score: frames, labels, weights, evaluator, input window, nominal timestamps and published acquisition metadata. A protocol is \emph{temporal-support identifiable} when its record determines the effective temporal support of every scored prediction uniquely (Sec.~\ref{sec:def}). A quantity that can be reconstructed from released code or index files, but is absent from the record, does not meet this condition: two evaluations that agree on the entire record can still differ in it, and nothing reported with the score tells them apart.

Prior work on timing in detection takes the benchmark's temporal index as given and changes what is measured against it: latency is folded into the score~\cite{li2020streaming,yang2022streamyolo,chu2026stare}, delay is scored separately~\cite{mao2019delay}, and temporal windows are redesigned~\cite{gehrig2023rvt,zubic2024ssm,sui2026astw}. None of these asks whether the index determines the evidence behind the number reported under it. That question has a sharp form. Identifiability is a uniqueness property, so it is refuted by a single pair of runs that agree on the whole record and differ in support, and it is restored by recording the variables that fix the support.

We build the refuting pair on the released SSM-ViT evaluation pipeline, using its base and small checkpoints (S5-B, S5-S). In that
implementation the nominally carried state does not enter the outputs across evaluation chunks. The recurrent
history available to a prediction is a deterministic function of chunk position, and ranges from
1 to \ssmEvalChunk{} windows inside one pooled score. Moving every chunk boundary
\ssmPairedShift{} windows later re-scores the same frames under a longer history at the same
evaluated timestamp, with frames, weights, labels and evaluator fixed
(\ssmPairedN{} labeled frames scored twice, cluster bootstrap over \outSeqsScanned{} sequences; $\pm$ denotes one cluster-bootstrap standard error).

Effective temporal support alone changes S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} mAP over all labeled
boxes and \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable
subset (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). The second checkpoint of the same released pipeline, S5-S, moves by
\ssmPairedMapAllSmall{} and \ssmPairedMapVelSmall{} points. The response is graded in the amount of history returned and is exactly zero at zero shift. The same intervention on RVT leaves it within \rvtBoundCarryMax{} points while its state is carried and moves it by \rvtBoundResetS{} and \rvtBoundResetB{} points once the state is reset at the boundary. The effect therefore belongs to the reset policy, a protocol variable, and not to one implementation: wherever a protocol does not record the support behind a score, the reported score is underdetermined. For scale, the frequency-generalization result that motivates the SSM-ViT design is a 3.31 mAP drop~\cite{zubic2024ssm}.

The pair has a constructive counterpart. Support-Conditioned AP scores a checkpoint from a reset state with the recurrent history fixed to a declared number of windows $H$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}). It needs no retraining and no new labels, only the released checkpoints. Applied to five released Gen1 checkpoints on \lhNFrames{} common frames, it orders them differently from the pooled score: S5-B is fourth in the pooled score and first at each of $H=1,5,10,21$, and for three pairs the pooled and $H=1$ gaps have intervals that exclude zero on opposite sides. RVT-\texttt{b} minus S5-B is \lhGapRbSbPool{} points in the pooled score, \lhGapRbSbHOne{} at $H=1$ and \lhGapRbSbHEighty{} at $H=80$, and the two checkpoints exchange rank between $H=\lhCrossLo$ and $H=\lhCrossHi$.

All measurements use released checkpoints, released code and public data: five checkpoints of two architecture families on the Gen1 validation split (\fhNAll{} labeled frames, \outSeqsScanned{} sequences) and \dsecSeqCount{} DSEC sequences (\dsecFrameCount{} frames).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Temporal-support identifiability}, an evaluation property with two operational criteria: a witness pair that refutes it and a record that restores it (Sec.~\ref{sec:def}).
\item A same-frame intervention that isolates recurrent support. Only the chunk boundary changes, and the S5-B score moves by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP overall and \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset, with a dose response, a zero-shift control, a permutation null, and an RVT control measured in both state regimes (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}).
\item \emph{Support-Conditioned AP}, a protocol that fixes the reset state and the history behind each score. On five released checkpoints the leader depends on $H$ (S5-B up to $H=21$, RVT-\texttt{b} from $H=40$) and three pairwise gaps reverse sign with intervals that exclude zero (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}).
\item A five-field record that a benchmark can ship with its labels, filled in for two released evaluations, and predictor-side and label-side audits on Gen1 and DSEC of what the timestamp leaves implicit (Secs.~\ref{sec:support} and~\ref{sec:sc}). The evaluation scripts, the boundary-shift driver and the per-frame access logs behind every table will be released.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Sensor and runtime latency.}
Work on event-camera timing takes the benchmark's temporal index as given and improves what is measured against it. Sensor latency is calibrated away~\cite{yang2024latency,liu2024nighttime}, compute latency is folded into average precision~\cite{li2020streaming,yang2022streamyolo,chu2026stare}, delay is scored separately~\cite{mao2019delay}, and temporal tolerance has been proposed~\cite{softed2024}. Each changes what the predictor does at a fixed benchmark index, or how late its answer may arrive. The object measured here is that index itself and the support a score reported under it pools: we test whether a released benchmark specification uniquely determines the temporal quantities behind its score. Streaming metrics move the instant at which a prediction is matched. Support-Conditioned AP holds that instant fixed and controls how much past the prediction was allowed to use, a variable those metrics leave free.

\paragraph{Temporal windows and event benchmarks.}
RVT and SSM-ViT redesign or parameterize temporal windows
\cite{gehrig2023rvt,zubic2024ssm}, and adaptive-window methods compare
alternative partitions \cite{sui2026astw}. Gen1, 1\,Mpx, DSEC-Det and Ev-3DOD use asynchronous measurements, rectified frames, or interpolated labels \cite{detournemire2020gen1,perot2020megapixel,gehrig2024lowlatency,cho2025ev3dod}, and the event count inside one labeled box can be too small to support detection~\cite{kugele2023howmany}. Event--RGB frequency mismatch and exposure duration have also been noted \cite{faod2024,sun2023refid,kim2022unknownexposure}. LEOD~\cite{wu2024leod} states that event detectors take only events triggered before the query time and builds a time-flip augmentation on that premise. It does not record where inside that window the evidence sits. Both released detector implementations leave a frame's position inside the evaluation chunk unspecified by the protocol. The recurrent state entering a chunk is the state saved at the end of the previous chunk, and is reset only at the first sample of a sequence. Under streaming sampling every timestep emits predictions, while otherwise only the last does (RVT \texttt{modules/detection.py}:232,\,243 and SSM-ViT :315,\,318). These works vary the window a detector reads. None reports the recurrent history behind a score, and none compares detectors at a matched history.

\paragraph{Benchmark reliability and evaluation analysis.}
Prior work studies detector calibration, stratified model evaluation, label-induced benchmark
instability, and event-vision evaluation protocols
\cite{oncalibration2024,stratification2024,northcutt2021labelerrors,ercan2023evreal}.
These analyses examine uncertainty or error in reported performance, whereas our focus is the
temporal support and acquisition semantics that a nominal detection timestamp leaves implicit.

\paragraph{Label time and attribution.}
Waymo and annotation studies estimate per-object capture or annotation offsets
\cite{waymolabelproto,sun2020waymo,nunez2026annotations}. Event datasets expose
per-box time fields, but their degrees of freedom are not necessarily specified.
LET-3D-AP and TIDE/DETAD/HOTA decompose or bucket spatial error while taking the
label clock as given \cite{hung2022let,bolya2020tide,hoiem2012diagnosing,alwassel2018detad,luiten2021hota}.

\section{Temporal-support identifiability}
\label{sec:def}

\paragraph{Setup.}
An evaluation run $e$ is everything executed to produce a score: the weights, the data, the evaluator, and the driver that feeds windows to the detector and manages its recurrent state. The protocol record $R(e)$ is the part of $e$ that the benchmark specifies and a paper reports. Let $i$ index the scored predictions and $r_i$ the window at which prediction $i$ is emitted. The effective temporal support $S_i(e)\subseteq\{0,\dots,r_i\}$ is the set of input windows whose content can change prediction $i$ with the rest of $e$ held fixed, and $m(e)$ is the reported score.

\paragraph{Definition.}
A protocol is \emph{temporal-support identifiable} if, for any two runs $e,e'$ it admits,
\begin{equation}
R(e)=R(e')\;\Longrightarrow\; S_i(e)=S_i(e')\quad\text{for every scored } i .
\label{eq:ident}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Criterion 1: a witness pair refutes it.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:ident} quantifies over all admitted runs, so one pair with $R(e)=R(e')$ and $S_i(e)\neq S_i(e')$ for some $i$ shows that the protocol is not identifiable. If in addition $m(e)\neq m(e')$, the score is not a function of the record, and a reader holding $R$ can neither reproduce $m$ nor say what temporal evidence it summarizes. The refutation is not a population claim. The number of frames and sequences enters only through the precision with which $m(e)-m(e')$ is estimated. Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} constructs such a pair and measures that difference.

\paragraph{Criterion 2: a record that restores it.}
Consider a causal detector driven window by window whose recurrent state is cleared at reset points, and let $b_i\le r_i$ be the last reset at or before $r_i$. Nothing before $b_i$ can reach prediction $i$, so $S_i\subseteq\{b_i,\dots,r_i\}$, an interval of $h_i=r_i-b_i+1$ windows. The support spans that whole interval when the oldest admitted window reaches the output, which a replacement probe on that window checks. A record that adds the reset policy, which fixes $b_i$, and the history length $h_i$ therefore determines $S_i$. Two points follow for practice. First, $b_i$ has to be measured on the running evaluation and not read from a configuration: the SSM-ViT release declares a carried state that never reaches its outputs (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}), so its realized resets are not its declared ones. Second, fixing $h_i=H$ for every prediction and every detector yields scores whose support is identical by construction. This is Support-Conditioned AP (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}).

\section{Primary evidence: the same-frame chunk-boundary intervention}
\label{sec:chunk}

The state the SSM-ViT release carries between evaluation chunks does not affect any of its
outputs. It enters as a multiplicative factor on the first coefficient of an associative scan
on which the outputs do not depend. Under the released streaming evaluation this bounds the
recurrent history available to a detection to the current \ssmEvalChunk{}-window chunk. That
history ranges from 1 window to \ssmEvalChunk{} with the detection's position in the chunk,
and the reported number averages over all \ssmEvalChunk{} positions. Chunk position is a
deterministic function of the release's own index files and is recorded nowhere in the
evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{The mechanism, measured.}
The property is read from the source and then checked on the running model. Zeroing the state that enters a chunk changes the emitted detection tensor of SSM-ViT by a relative $L_2$ of \ssmStateEffect{} at every position of a \ssmChunkMeas-window chunk. The same test on RVT-\texttt{t} gives \rvtStateEffectOne{} at the first position and \rvtStateEffect{} at the last. A model that ignored its history would return zero in both cases, so the control ablates the previous window inside the same chunk: SSM-ViT then changes by \ssmInchunkOne{}. It is recurrent within a chunk and loses its state only across chunks (Supplement Sec.~2). Repeated with a \raChunk-window chunk, the test changes the output of all three RVT checkpoints and of a third released detector (EvRT-DETR) and changes that of neither SSM-ViT checkpoint (Supplement Sec.~2).

\paragraph{The same-frame intervention.}
The intervention moves every chunk boundary \ssmPairedShift{} windows later and changes nothing
else: the same frames, the same weights, the same labels, the same evaluator, and the evaluated
timestamp of each frame unchanged. \ssmPairedN{} labeled frames are scored twice under this
shift, once with 1--4 windows of recurrent history and once with 17--20.
They are compared paired within frame with a cluster bootstrap over the \outSeqsScanned{}
sequences. The long-history arm gains
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP points for S5-B ($z=\ssmPairedMapVelBaseZ$) and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{} for S5-S ($z=\ssmPairedMapVelSmallZ$) on the velocity-evaluable subset,
and over every labeled box the gains are
\ssmPairedMapAllBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} and \ssmPairedMapAllSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapAllSmallSE{} points. Mean detection confidence, which uses
no labels, moves with it by \ssmPairedConfBase$\pm$\ssmPairedConfBaseSE{} and
\ssmPairedConfSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedConfSmallSE{}. Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos} (bottom) draws one such
pair. No quantity the protocol records distinguishes the two arms, so the pair is the witness of Criterion~1.

\paragraph{Graded response and what moves.}
The size of the boundary shift is a free parameter. Re-scoring the same \ssmDoseN{} frames after
shifting the boundary so that the starved block gains $+6$, $+11$ and $+16$ windows of history
changes the velocity-subset mAP of S5-B by \ssmDoseBaseSix$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseSixSE{},
\ssmDoseBaseEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} points, and that of S5-S by
\ssmDoseSmallSix$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallSixSE{}, \ssmDoseSmallEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{}. The zero-shift control, which re-runs the
release's own boundary placement through the same pipeline, returns \ssmDoseNull{}. The three rungs share their frames, so a frame-difficulty confound aligned with chunk position cannot produce the gradient. Confidence on detections matched to a
label rises by \ssmPairedConfTpBase{} and \ssmPairedConfTpSmall{}, and total output falls by
\ssmPairedDpfBase{} and \ssmPairedDpfSmall{} boxes per frame, so both terms of precision move: the starved arm emits more boxes and scores the correct ones lower.
Tab.~\ref{tab:evidence} lists all four amounts.

\paragraph{The observational contrast across chunk positions.}
The released evaluation already contains the contrast, without any intervention. Reconstructing chunk position over the \mapFrames{} labeled frames of the released evaluation and
scoring every labeled box, S5-B reaches \chunkShortBase{} mAP on the frames given 1--4
windows and \chunkFullBase{} on those given 17--\ssmEvalChunk{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos}, top; Tab.~\ref{tab:chunkmain}). This contrast compares different frames, so it is differenced
against the three RVT checkpoints, evaluated on the same frames with a state that crosses chunk
boundaries. That controls for frame-position effects shared with them. It leaves
\chunkDidBase$\pm$\chunkDidBaseSE{} points for S5-B ($z=\chunkDidBaseZ$) and
\chunkDidSmall$\pm$\chunkDidSmallSE{} for S5-S ($z=\chunkDidSmallZ$), against placebo
differences among the RVT checkpoints of at most \chunkPlaceboMax{} points
($z=\chunkPlaceboZ$). A permutation null reshuffles chunk position among the frames of each sequence, which preserves the scene, the sequence lengths, the label density and the block sizes and destroys only the correspondence between a frame and its history. Over \chunkPermB{} reshuffles the null has a standard deviation of about \chunkPermSd{} points; both SSM values lie outside every reshuffle ($p=\chunkPermP$, the smallest attainable), and every placebo stays within \chunkPermPlaceboZ{} standard deviations. A label-free statistic shows the same split: the mean confidence of S5-S rises from \chunkConfOne{} at 1 window to \chunkConfFull{} at 17, while that of RVT-\texttt{t} stays flat. The observational and interventional readings agree in sign and magnitude, and the paired intervention carries the claim
because it holds the frames fixed (Supplement Sec.~1).

That differencing treats the RVT checkpoints as a control because their state survives a chunk
boundary. The same-frame intervention applied to RVT measures that property. Driven on the SSM
chunk grid, RVT reproduces its stored per-window detections bit for bit at zero shift, and the
same \rvtBoundN{} frames are re-scored with every boundary moved \rvtBoundShift{} windows
later. With the released state carried across the boundary the paired change is
$\rvtBoundCarryS\pm\rvtBoundCarrySSE$ points for RVT-\texttt{s} ($z=\rvtBoundCarrySZ$) and
$\rvtBoundCarryB\pm\rvtBoundCarryBSE$ for RVT-\texttt{b} ($z=\rvtBoundCarryBZ$). Dropping

difference-in-differences relies on holds as a measurement. The reset arm shows that the shift reaches the model, so the null of the carried arm is a property of the released evaluation and not of an intervention that failed. One model, one set of weights and one set of frames give a zero or a \rvtBoundResetMin{}-point effect depending only on whether the state survives the boundary, a variable that no protocol record contains (Supplement Sec.~1).

\paragraph{Controls.}
Each alternative reading of the effect has its own null. A pipeline artifact is excluded by the zero-shift control (\ssmDoseNull{}) and by the bit-identical reproduction of the stored RVT detections. Frame difficulty is excluded by pairing within frame, by the dose response on one frame set, and by the permutation null. An evaluator artifact is excluded by the label-free confidence shift and by rescoring every detection dump with \texttt{pycocotools}, which agrees with our evaluator to within \portEvalGap{} points on all five checkpoints. An architecture-specific reading is excluded by the two RVT state regimes. Dependence between frames of a sequence is handled by resampling sequences, not frames, in every interval.

\paragraph{What the pooled score reports.}
One released number pools detections whose recurrent history differs by a factor of \ssmEvalChunk{}. Restricting evaluation to the frames that receive 17--\ssmEvalChunk{} windows gives an S5-B score \chunkGapBase{} points above the pooled released score, and \chunkGapSmall{} points for S5-S. Two systems compared under this protocol at the same nominal timestamps need not have been given the same temporal evidence, and the protocol record contains no variable by which a reader could tell.

\section{What else the timestamp leaves implicit}
\label{sec:support}

Recurrent history is one component of the support behind a score. Two audits measure the others on released artifacts, the input timing inside the newest window and the acquisition timing of the label. Each is a timing descriptor that the nominal timestamp does not encode. Their derivations, figures and tables are in
Supplement Secs.~5 and 14.

\paragraph{Protocol record.}
Call a quantity \emph{protocol-identifiable} when the protocol record of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} determines it uniquely. The input window and the published exposure interval are in the record. The effective predictor timing, the recurrent history pooled in a score, and the attribution of event timing within an exposure are not.

\paragraph{Predictor side (Gen1).}
The released RVT configuration~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} indexes each \rvtBins-bin, \rvtWindow\,ms input
window by its end, and places that end on a label time. Ablating each bin of the released RVT-\texttt{t} checkpoint over \rvtInfluenceSamples{} Gen1
validation samples puts the ablation-sensitivity
centroid at \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms, so the evidence of the newest window is centered about half a window before the instant the label asserts. A regression of detection-center residuals on label velocity,
which carries the temporal calibration of~\cite{qin2018temporal} to a box center and is fitted on
nominal-time-matched detections, gives a coefficient of
\ladderFullTau\,$\pm$\,\ladderFullTauSE\,ms, so the state the output reports is the one at the label time. One timestamp thus stands for two instants whose point estimates are \outInterval\,ms apart, the centroid of the evidence and the time of the state the output reports. All five released checkpoints show the same newest-window centroid to within a fraction of one bin. Over a 1000\,ms horizon the newest window accounts for only 22.7\,\% of the
summed window-ablation sensitivity. Most of the evidence behind a detection lies in the recurrent history, which is the component Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} manipulates (Supplement Secs.~5, 6 and 8).

\paragraph{Label side (DSEC).}
Across the \dsecSeqCount{} DSEC training sequences whose exposure files
resolve~\cite{gehrig2021dsec} (\dsecFrameCount{} frames), published exposure width varies by a factor of \dsecExpRatio{}
against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, and \dsecPinnedFrac\,\% of frames sit at the auto-exposure ceiling. A label on the frame clock therefore summarizes acquisition intervals that differ by two orders of magnitude. Inside one ceiling exposure the event rate moves by a factor of \ceilVarNight{} between its extremes, against \ceilVarDay{} in a daytime control, and its spectrum carries the \flickerLineHz\,Hz line of rectified mains lighting. Across all \dsecCeilingSeqs{} ceiling sequences and \sixBoxes{} labeled boxes, \sixLockedLoPct--\sixLockedHiPct\,\% of boxes per sequence carry that signature above an off-frequency control on the same events. Within-box dispersion of event time inside one exposure is
\sigmaEvtNight\,\micro s against an analytic null of \sigmaEvtNullNight\,\micro s (Fig.~\ref{fig:daynightmain}). Within-exposure event timing contains an illumination-locked component and cannot be
attributed to object timing without separating that component first (Supplement Secs.~14 and 16).
DSEC-Det ships more than one annotation version~\cite{beyondduality2026}, and Supplement Sec.~15
names the one measured here.

\section{Support-Conditioned AP}
\label{sec:sc}

Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} shows that one released protocol pools detections that received different histories. Criterion~2 of Sec.~\ref{sec:def} says what to fix. This section scores the \rankCkpts{} released Gen1 checkpoints at a declared history and compares the result with the pooled score.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
For a labeled frame scored at window $r$ and a history length $H$, the detector starts from a reset recurrent state at window $r-H+1$ and runs through window $r$, and the detections of window $r$ are scored with the labels, confidence threshold, NMS and evaluator of the released-protocol dumps. We call the resulting AP at fixed $H$ Support-Conditioned AP. $H$ counts the windows read up to and including the scored window; we write $H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ for this requested length. A frame with fewer than $H$ windows available receives $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=\min(H,r+1)$ windows instead. All checkpoints are scored on the same \lhNFrames{} frames. They are a stratified sample of the \lhNElig{} labeled frames that have at least 80 windows available, so that $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21,40,80\}$. The eligible frames lie in \lhNEligSeq{} of the \lhNSeqAll{} validation sequences. The sequence is the stratum: allocation is proportional to a sequence's eligible frames, every such sequence contributes at least one frame and at most \lhMaxPerSeq{}, and the draw used a fixed seed and was made before the runs at $H=40$ and $80$. The column \emph{pooled} is the released evaluation of each checkpoint, in which the history depends on the release and on the frame, restricted to the same \lhNFrames{} frames. The access log of every run shows that the number of distinct windows read equals $H_{\mathrm{forced}}$ and that the first window read is $r-H_{\mathrm{forced}}+1$ in every cell of every checkpoint (Supplement Sec.~1.4). The protocol reuses released weights and labels and adds no training.

\paragraph{What Support-Conditioned AP equalizes.}
The access log confirms that exactly $H$ windows are read. Replacing the oldest admitted window by zeros changes the output in \lhProbeHitLo{} to \lhProbeHitHi{} of \lhProbeN{} probed cells per checkpoint at $H=21$, 40 and 80, and in every other probed cell the window already holds no events or the frame has no detection (Supplement Sec.~1.4). The full admitted history therefore reaches the detection, and the support is the interval of Criterion~2. Every checkpoint starts from the same reset state. The protocol therefore equalizes the two things the pooled score leaves free, the reset policy and the history length. Under the released evaluations these differ by family: the RVT release carries its state across a sequence, while the SSM-ViT release scores each chunk from a reset state (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). This is why the pooled RVT scores sit above their $H=\ssmEvalChunk{}$ scores (RVT-\texttt{b}: \lhMapRbPool{} against \lhMapRbHTwentyone) and the pooled S5 scores sit below theirs (S5-B: \lhMapSbPool{} against \lhMapSbHTwentyone). The pooled column compares checkpoints that were given different support. Table~\ref{tab:sc} compares them at the same support, which is the comparison a timing benchmark is taken to make. The unit compared is the released checkpoint under one evaluation, the unit a leaderboard ranks.

\paragraph{The curve over $H$ as a result.}
Support-Conditioned AP reports a curve where the pooled protocol reports one number, and the curve carries information the number hides (Fig.~\ref{fig:schcurve}). From $H=1$ to $H=21$ the RVT checkpoints gain between \lhGainRvtLo{} and \lhGainRvtHi{} points and the S5 checkpoints between \lhGainSsmLo{} and \lhGainSsmHi{}, so at $H=1$ both S5 checkpoints rank above all three RVT checkpoints and the families converge as history grows. Beyond $H=21$ the RVT scores keep rising, by a further \lhLateRvtLo{} to \lhLateRvtHi{} points up to $H=80$, while the S5 scores fall, by \lhLateSsmB{} points for S5-B and \lhLateSsmS{} for S5-S. The S5-S score at $H=80$ (\lhMapSsHEighty) is below its score at $H=1$ (\lhMapSsHOne). The S5 scores at $H=40$ and 80 come from chunks longer than the \ssmEvalChunk{} windows of the released evaluation, and the Gen1 training configurations of both repositories set the sequence length to \ssmEvalChunk. We did not test whether this accounts for the decline. A reader choosing a detector for a short-latency budget and a reader choosing one for long sequences would pick differently from the same table, S5-B for $H\le21$ and RVT-\texttt{b} for $H\ge40$, and neither choice can be read from the pooled column.

\paragraph{Reporting recipe.}
The protocol reduces to five steps that apply to any recurrent detector with a released checkpoint. (i) Declare a set of history lengths; we use $H\in\{1,5,10,21,40,80\}$, where 21 is the chunk length of the released streaming evaluation and 40 and 80 exceed it. (ii) Restrict scoring to the frames that have the largest $H$ available, sample them if the largest $H$ is costly, and state the frame count and the sampling. (iii) For every frame and every $H$, run from a reset state over exactly $H$ windows and keep the detections of the last. (iv) Log the windows the loader returned and probe the oldest one, so that the declared support is the realized one. (v) Report the fixed-$H$ scores with paired sequence-bootstrap intervals beside the pooled score, together with the distribution of history lengths the pooled score contains.

\paragraph{What a benchmark should record.}
A nominal timestamp does not determine the effective temporal support or the label-acquisition support, and the intervention above shows a case in which the unrecorded support changes the score. We propose that a benchmark record, for each scored sample, five fields. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant at which the sample is scored. The \emph{input window} is what the detector reads at that timestamp. The \emph{state-reset policy} states where the recurrent state is cleared, including chunk boundaries and sequence starts. The \emph{effective history length} is the number of windows read, as the access log of the evaluation run records it. The \emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired. By Criterion~2, the reset policy and the history length determine the effective temporal support. Tab.~\ref{tab:fields} fills in these fields for the two releases examined here. A score is then reported at a declared history length, as in Tab.~\ref{tab:sc}. The record makes support checkable, and Support-Conditioned AP makes it equal, so that scores are compared at a history that both detectors received.

\section{Conclusion}

A benchmark timestamp does not determine the temporal evidence behind the score reported under it. With frames, weights, labels, evaluator and evaluated timestamps fixed, moving only the chunk boundary shifts S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP over all labeled validation boxes and by \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset. The chunk boundary changes the recurrent support behind a reported score while every recorded quantity stays fixed, which is the witness pair that refutes temporal-support identifiability. RVT shows the same effect as soon as its state is reset at the boundary and none while it is carried, so the effect belongs to the reset policy and not to one architecture.

Support-Conditioned AP removes this degree of freedom by fixing the reset state and the history behind every score. On five released Gen1 checkpoints, S5-B moves from fourth in the pooled score to first at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$, RVT-\texttt{b} overtakes it between $H=\lhCrossLo$ and $H=\lhCrossHi$, Kendall's $\tau$ between the pooled and fixed-$H$ orderings is at most \lhTauHTwentyone{} for $H\le21$, and three pairwise gaps reverse sign between the pooled score and $H=1$ with intervals that exclude zero on both sides.

Two scores at the same nominal timestamp are comparable in time only when the support behind them is the same. A benchmark can make this checkable by recording five fields for each scored sample (nominal timestamp, input window, state-reset policy, effective history length, label-acquisition support), and a paper can make it hold by reporting scores at a declared history length.
