\title{Temporal-Support Identifiability\\
in Event-Detection Evaluation Pipelines}
\author{Anonymous CVPR submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An event-detection benchmark identifies each scored sample by one nominal timestamp, but a recurrent detector forms its prediction from a longer history, and the benchmark does not record how long. We state the missing requirement as a property of the protocol, \emph{temporal-support identifiability}: the benchmark variables must uniquely determine the temporal evidence supporting a reported prediction. This makes effective temporal support part of the benchmark specification rather than a hidden property of one evaluation implementation. A released SSM-ViT evaluation pipeline provides a direct counterexample. At the same evaluated timestamp, with frames, weights, labels and evaluator fixed, moving only the chunk boundary changes the recurrent history from one to twenty-one windows within the pooled score. The same move changes S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP over all labeled boxes and by \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset. Because identifiability is a uniqueness property, one controlled pair in which identical recorded protocol variables yield different effective support and different scores is sufficient to show that the protocol under-specifies the score. Two supporting diagnostics reported in the supplement, an input-sensitivity measurement on the RVT predictor and an exposure analysis of DSEC label timing, bound the interpretation without being used as recurrent-support estimators. Over all labeled boxes the validation-split ordering of the \rankCkpts{} released checkpoints is itself reproduced in only \rankHoldBox\,\% of sequence-bootstrap replicates, so that ordering cannot certify a timing convention as immaterial. Support-Conditioned AP scores each checkpoint with the recurrent history fixed to $H$ windows and so equalizes the history across checkpoints. For five released Gen1 checkpoints, S5-B ranks third in the pooled score on the common frames and first at each of $H=1,5,10,21$, and Kendall's $\tau$ between the pooled ordering and the fixed-$H$ orderings lies between \fhTauHOne{} and \fhTauHTwentyone{}. This is measured for these five checkpoints under this evaluation only. Timing comparisons between recurrent detectors therefore require the support that produced each score to be stated and equalized, a condition an event-detection protocol can record with its labels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two recurrent detectors evaluated at the same nominal timestamp are not comparable in time unless the history each one actually used is also identified.

An event-detection benchmark defines a sample by a nominal timestamp. A prediction $\hat{s}$ is scored against the ground truth $s^{*}$ carried at query time $t$ through a function $\rho(\hat{s},s^{*})$ of position, shape and class. Beyond selecting the label at $t$, that function contains no variable for the temporal evidence behind either argument, so any temporal discrepancy enters the score disguised as displacement.

A recurrent detector does not read only the instant the benchmark names. It computes $\hat{s}$
from an input window and from state carried across earlier windows. The label it is
compared against comes from an exposure of finite width, an annotator or tracker working on the
exposed image, and a geometric transfer into another sensor's frame. The evidence on both sides
is an interval, while the protocol records an instant.

Three terms carry this throughout. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant
at which the protocol pairs a prediction with a label. The \emph{effective temporal support} is
the past that can influence the prediction, including state carried by recurrence. The
\emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired.
A nominal timestamp determines neither of the other two uniquely, and a temporal descriptor may
be reconstructable from released artifacts yet remain unreported or heterogeneous within one
pooled score. We call this protocol property temporal-support identifiability:
whether the released protocol and artifacts uniquely determine the temporal quantities a
reported score represents.

We test it with a paired intervention on the released SSM-ViT evaluation pipeline. In that
implementation the nominally carried state is inert across evaluation chunks. The recurrent
history available to a prediction is a deterministic function of chunk position, and ranges from
one to twenty-one windows inside one pooled score. Moving every chunk boundary
\ssmPairedShift{} windows later re-scores the same frames under a longer history at the same
evaluated timestamp, with frames, weights, labels and evaluator fixed
(\ssmPairedN{} paired boxes, cluster bootstrap over \outSeqsScanned{} sequences).

Effective temporal support alone changes S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP over all labeled
boxes and \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable
subset (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). A second checkpoint of the same released pipeline moves by
\ssmPairedMapAllSmall{} and \ssmPairedMapVelSmall{} points. Since temporal-support identifiability is a uniqueness property, this controlled violation is sufficient to show that the nominal timestamp does not uniquely specify the temporal evidence represented by that score. The consequence is at the level of the protocol rather than of one implementation: where the protocol does not record the support behind a score, the reported score is underdetermined. We measure that condition on the two protocols examined here and do not assert it of event-detection scores in general.

The counterexample has a corrected counterpart. Support-Conditioned AP scores a checkpoint with the recurrent history fixed to a stated number of windows $H$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}). Applied to five released Gen1 checkpoints on \fhNCommon{} common frames, it orders them differently from the pooled score: S5-B is third in the pooled score and first at each of $H=1,5,10,21$, and for three pairs the pooled and $H=1$ gaps have intervals that exclude zero on opposite sides. This describes these five checkpoints under this evaluation and is not a ranking of the architectures.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Temporal-support identifiability}: an evaluation property separating the nominal
timestamp a protocol records from the effective temporal support and label-acquisition support a
reported score represents.
\item A paired intervention that isolates recurrent support: the frames, weights, labels,
evaluator, and evaluated timestamp are fixed, while only the recurrent history changes.
\item Its measured consequence: support the protocol does not record moves a pooled
leaderboard-style score by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP overall and \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on
the velocity-evaluable subset. The same benchmark reproduces its validation-split ordering
over all labeled boxes in only \rankHoldBox\,\% of sequence-bootstrap replicates.
Predictor-side and label-side audits delimit the result.
\item A corrected protocol: Support-Conditioned AP, which fixes the recurrent history behind each score, applied to five released checkpoints, together with the fields a benchmark would need to record so that the support behind a score is identifiable.
\end{itemize}

Work on event-detection timing takes that index as given and improves what is measured against
it. Sensor latency is calibrated away~\cite{yang2024latency, liu2024nighttime}, compute latency
is folded into average precision~\cite{li2020streaming,yang2022streamyolo,chu2026stare}, and the
temporal window is redesigned~\cite{gehrig2023rvt,zubic2024ssm}. Each changes what the predictor
does at a fixed benchmark index, whereas the object measured here is that index itself and the
support a score reported under it pools. The two datasets examined here instantiate this protocol
condition. How often it holds elsewhere is not measured.

\paragraph{Supporting audits.} Two audits, summarized in Sec.~\ref{sec:support} and reported in
Supplement Secs.~15 and 16, delimit this result and are not used as recurrent-support estimators.
Ablating each input bin of the event branch examined here~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} on real validation
samples puts the ablation-sensitivity centroid at \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms, and the regression-based
temporal coefficient estimated on nominal-time-matched detections is
\ladderFullTau\,$\pm$\,\ladderFullTauSE\,ms and is compatible with zero. Both describe
predictor-side timing, and the nominal timestamp encodes neither. On the label side, across the
\dsecSeqCount{} DSEC training sequences whose exposure files resolve, published exposure width
varies by a factor of \dsecExpRatio{} against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, and
within-exposure event timing carries an illumination-locked component, so it is not attributable
to object timing without separating that component first.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Sensor and runtime latency.}
Prior work calibrates sensor latency
\cite{yang2024latency,liu2024nighttime}, incorporates runtime latency into
average precision \cite{li2020streaming,yang2022streamyolo,chu2026stare}, or
proposes temporal tolerance \cite{softed2024}. These methods address the time
from an event to an output and take the benchmark's temporal index as given.
We instead measure that index, testing whether a released benchmark
specification uniquely determines the temporal quantities behind its score.

\paragraph{Temporal windows and benchmark identifiability.}
RVT and SSM-ViT redesign or parameterize temporal windows
\cite{gehrig2023rvt,zubic2024ssm}, while adaptive-window methods compare
alternative partitions \cite{sui2026astw}. Event--RGB frequency mismatch and
exposure duration have also been noted \cite{faod2024,sun2023refid,kim2022unknownexposure}.
Both released implementations leave a frame's position inside the evaluation chunk
unspecified by the protocol rather than fixed by the model. The recurrent state entering a
chunk is the state saved at the end of the previous chunk, and is reset only at the first
sample of a sequence. Under streaming sampling every timestep emits predictions, while otherwise only the last does
(RVT \texttt{modules/detection.py}:232,\,243 and SSM-ViT :315,\,318).
LEOD~\cite{wu2024leod} states that event detectors take only events triggered before the
query time and builds a time-flip augmentation on that premise. It does not measure where inside that window the evidence sits.
This is a reading of the released code and text, not a measurement of those systems.
We read the released per-frame windows and exposures and separate predictor
support, output-time interpretation, and label-acquisition support.

\paragraph{Benchmark reliability and evaluation analysis.}
Prior work studies detector calibration, stratified model evaluation, label-induced benchmark
instability, and event-vision evaluation protocols
\cite{oncalibration2024,stratification2024,northcutt2021labelerrors,ercan2023evreal}.
These analyses examine uncertainty or error in reported performance, whereas our focus is the
temporal support and acquisition semantics that a nominal detection timestamp leaves implicit.

\paragraph{Label time and attribution.}
Waymo and annotation studies estimate per-object capture or annotation offsets
\cite{waymolabelproto,sun2020waymo,nunez2026annotations}. Event datasets expose
per-box time fields, but their degrees of freedom are not necessarily specified.
LET-3D-AP and TIDE/DETAD/HOTA decompose or bucket spatial error while taking the
label clock as given \cite{hung2022let,bolya2020tide,hoiem2012diagnosing,alwassel2018detad,luiten2021hota}.

\paragraph{Event benchmarks and exposure.}
Gen1, 1\,Mpx, DSEC-Det and Ev-3DOD use asynchronous measurements, rectified
frames, or interpolated labels \cite{detournemire2020gen1,perot2020megapixel,
gehrig2024lowlatency,cho2025ev3dod}. LEOD establishes causal input windows
\cite{wu2024leod}. The present work reads the released window and exposure
artifacts as protocol evidence and tests whether the resulting temporal
quantities are uniquely specified.

Shen~\cite{qin2018temporal} model a global camera--IMU time offset by shifting
feature observations with image-plane velocity and jointly optimizing the offset
with visual--inertial states and feature locations. Here the offset lies between
a predictor's clock and

folds wall-clock compute latency into average precision and re-anchors any

effect. This work instead asks which acquisition and label times a nominal benchmark
timestamp represents, using released detector and exposure artifacts.

LET-3D-AP~\cite{hung2022let} decomposes camera-only 3D localization error along
the line of sight and defines an average precision tolerant of the longitudinal
part. It also reports a camera-to-label synchronization gap as a per-dataset interval
that depends on horizontal object position. Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime} keeps the

bucket detection error atemporally, so a temporal offset falls inside their
localization bucket, and HOTA~\cite{luiten2021hota} takes the label clock as
exact.

\paragraph{Per-object time in labels.} Waymo's \texttt{camera\_synced\_box} solves
$t_{\mathrm{capture}}$ per object and stores the resulting box shift instead of the time~\cite{waymolabelproto,sun2020waymo}. Miro et
al.~\cite{nunez2026annotations} measure within-frame per-object annotation
offsets on Argoverse\,2 and TruckScenes and define a dispersion of the
\emph{spatial} error. Each computes a per-object time in order to remove it. The
event-vision label formats carry the field: the Prophesee dtype used by Gen1 and
the 1\,Mpx dataset, and the DSEC-Det format, both have a per-box \texttt{t}. In the releases examined
here, that field's per-object degree of freedom is not specified. Across the \ddSeqs{} released DSEC-Det
sequences, \ddBoxes{} boxes take \ddDistinctT{} distinct values of \texttt{t},
\ddBoxesPerT{} boxes per value, and those values are the \dsecFramePeriod\,ms
frame clock.

\paragraph{Event benchmarks and their exposures.}
Gen1~\cite{detournemire2020gen1} labels are drawn on gray-level images assembled
from asynchronous per-pixel measurements. On the 1\,Mpx
dataset~\cite{perot2020megapixel} the event count inside a labeled box at its own
timestamp is often too small to support detection~\cite{kugele2023howmany}.
DSEC-Det~\cite{gehrig2024lowlatency} labels by tracking~\cite{pang2021qdtrack} on
rectified frames and warps into the event view, and
Ev-3DOD~\cite{cho2025ev3dod} interpolates lower-rate annotations into high-rate
3D labels. That a frame's exposure is long relative to event timing has been
stated. Event-based deblurring~\cite{sun2023refid} argues that accumulating events from a single
frame timestamp loses information, and deblurring work names auto-exposure as why the
width is unknown~\cite{kim2022unknownexposure}. RENet~\cite{zhou2022renet}
uses a DSEC exposure interval as its event window while describing it as short.
The cited works do not read the released per-frame widths or measure what is
inside one. State space backbones~\cite{zubic2024ssm} substantially reduce sensitivity to
inference-time window duration, and
adaptive spatial-temporal windows~\cite{sui2026astw} retrain a detector under
each of nine partitioning strategies and report the score of each. Both treat the
window as a design variable to choose. The released window configuration is
treated as a measured property of the released detector and evaluation pipeline.
That the released window is causal is already in print:
LEOD~\cite{wu2024leod} states that event detectors take only events triggered
before the query time. It builds a time-flip augmentation on the consequence
that forward inference favors receding objects. It does not measure where inside
that window the evidence sits. Protocol
work supplies the standards these measurements are held
to~\cite{oncalibration2024,stratification2024,northcutt2021labelerrors,ercan2023evreal}:
a repaired protocol can reverse a field's conclusion, and a reported score is
an estimate with a variance. In this benchmark, the streaming intervention changes the
reported score even though the model and evaluated frames are held fixed.

\section{Primary evidence: the same-frame chunk-boundary intervention}
\label{sec:chunk}

The state the SSM-ViT release carries between evaluation chunks does not affect any of its
outputs. It enters as a multiplicative factor on the first coefficient of an associative scan
on which the outputs do not depend. Under the released streaming evaluation this bounds the
recurrent history available to a detection to the current \ssmEvalChunk{}-window chunk. That
history ranges from one window to \ssmEvalChunk{} with the detection's position in the chunk,
and the reported number averages over all \ssmEvalChunk{} positions. Chunk position is a
deterministic function of the release's own index files and is recorded nowhere in the
evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{The same-frame intervention.}
The intervention moves every chunk boundary \ssmPairedShift{} windows later and changes nothing
else: the same frames, the same weights, the same labels, the same evaluator, and the evaluated
timestamp of each frame unchanged. \ssmPairedN{} labeled boxes are scored twice under this
shift, once with one to four windows of recurrent history and once with seventeen to twenty.
They are compared paired within frame with a cluster bootstrap over the \outSeqsScanned{}
sequences. The long-history arm gains
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP points for S5-B and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{} for S5-S on the velocity-evaluable subset,
and over every labeled box the S5-B gain is
\ssmPairedMapAllBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} points. Mean detection confidence, which uses
no labels, moves with it by \ssmPairedConfBase$\pm$\ssmPairedConfBaseSE{} and
\ssmPairedConfSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedConfSmallSE{}. Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos} (bottom) draws one such
pair. No quantity the protocol records distinguishes the two arms.

\paragraph{Graded response and what moves.}
The size of the boundary shift is a free parameter. Re-scoring the same \ssmDoseN{} frames after
shifting the boundary so that the starved block gains $+6$, $+11$ and $+16$ windows of history
changes the velocity-subset mAP of S5-B by \ssmDoseBaseSix$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseSixSE{},
\ssmDoseBaseEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} points, and that of S5-S by
\ssmDoseSmallSix$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallSixSE{}, \ssmDoseSmallEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{}. The zero-shift control, which re-runs the
release's own boundary placement, returns \ssmDoseNull{}. Confidence on detections matched to a
label rises by \ssmPairedConfTpBase{} and \ssmPairedConfTpSmall{}, and total output falls by
\ssmPairedDpfBase{} and \ssmPairedDpfSmall{} boxes per frame, so both terms of precision move.
The curve is measured at four amounts on one dataset and one architecture family, and a
saturation point is not estimated (Supplement Sec.~13).

\paragraph{The observational contrast across chunk positions.}
Reconstructing chunk position over \mapFrames{} labeled frames of the released evaluation and
scoring every labeled box, S5-B reaches \chunkShortBase{} mAP on the frames given one to four
windows and \chunkFullBase{} on those given seventeen to \ssmEvalChunk{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos}, top). This contrast compares different frames, so it is differenced
against the three RVT checkpoints, evaluated on the same frames with a state that crosses chunk
boundaries. That controls for frame-position effects shared with them. It leaves
\chunkDidBase$\pm$\chunkDidBaseSE{} points for S5-B ($z=\chunkDidBaseZ$) and
\chunkDidSmall$\pm$\chunkDidSmallSE{} for S5-S ($z=\chunkDidSmallZ$), against placebo
differences among the RVT checkpoints of at most \chunkPlaceboMax{} points
($z=\chunkPlaceboZ$). Permuting chunk position within each sequence puts both SSM values at
least \chunkPermZSmall{} standard deviations outside their own null and every placebo inside
it. The two readings agree in sign and magnitude, and the paired intervention carries the claim
because it holds the frames fixed (Supplement Sec.~13).

\paragraph{The control, measured rather than assumed.}
That differencing treats the RVT checkpoints as a control because their state survives a chunk
boundary. The same-frame intervention applied to RVT measures that property. Driven on the SSM
chunk grid, RVT reproduces its stored per-window detections bit for bit at zero shift, and the
same \rvtBoundN{} frames are re-scored with every boundary moved \rvtBoundShift{} windows
later. With the released state carried across the boundary the paired change is
$\rvtBoundCarryS\pm\rvtBoundCarrySSE$ points for RVT-\texttt{s} ($z=\rvtBoundCarrySZ$) and
$\rvtBoundCarryB\pm\rvtBoundCarryBSE$ for RVT-\texttt{b} ($z=\rvtBoundCarryBZ$). Dropping
the state at every chunk start, the condition the SSM release is already in, moves the same
weights on the same frames by $\rvtBoundResetS\pm\rvtBoundResetSSE$ ($z=\rvtBoundResetSZ$)
and $\rvtBoundResetB\pm\rvtBoundResetBSE$ points ($z=\rvtBoundResetBZ$). Random-frame
nulls are centered within \rvtBoundPermMax{} points of zero. The boundary effect follows whether the
recurrent state survives the boundary, not the architecture, and the control the
difference-in-differences relies on holds as a measurement (Supplement Sec.~13).

\paragraph{What the pooled score reports.}
One released number therefore pools detections whose recurrent history differs by a factor of
\ssmEvalChunk{}. Restricting evaluation to the frames receiving 17--21 windows yields an S5-B
score \chunkGapBase{} points above the pooled released-evaluation score. Two systems compared
under this protocol at the same nominal timestamps need not have been given the same temporal
evidence, and the protocol records no variable by which a reader could tell.

\section{Supporting audits}
\label{sec:support}

Two audits delimit how the intervention of Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} is read. Each measures a
timing descriptor that the nominal timestamp does not encode, and neither estimates the recurrent
support that the intervention manipulates. Their derivations, figures and tables are in
Supplement Secs.~15 and 16.

\paragraph{Latent quantities.}
A benchmark releases one tuple per evaluated instant. The tuple holds a nominal timestamp, the
labels asserted at it, the predictions scored against them, and the score. Three temporal
quantities are latent in that tuple and are not recorded in it: the temporal support of the
predictor's input and carried state, the effective state time its output refers to, and the
acquisition support of the labels. Call such a quantity \emph{protocol-identifiable} when the
released protocol and artifacts determine it uniquely. Under this definition, input and exposure
intervals are protocol-identifiable, whereas effective predictor timing, recurrent context
represented by a pooled score, and within-exposure attribution are not.

\paragraph{Predictor side.}
The released RVT configuration~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} indexes each \rvtBins-bin, \rvtWindow\,ms input
window by its end, and places that end on a label time. The window's uniform-weight centroid
therefore lies \rvtCentroidMag\,ms before the instant at which the label asserts a state.
Ablating each bin of the released \texttt{rvt-t} checkpoint over \rvtInfluenceSamples{} Gen1
validation samples, with the incoming recurrent state held fixed, puts the ablation-sensitivity
centroid at \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms. A regression of detection-center residuals on label velocity,
which carries the temporal calibration of~\cite{qin2018temporal} to a box center and is fitted on
nominal-time-matched detections, gives a coefficient of
\ladderFullTau\,$\pm$\,\ladderFullTauSE\,ms. The two are distinct operational summaries. Their
point estimates are \outInterval\,ms apart, and the difference is not an estimate of
architectural latency. Over a 1000\,ms horizon the newest window accounts for 22.7\,\% of the
summed window-ablation sensitivity, so the newest-window centroid does not describe the full
recurrent support. Displacing ground-truth centers across the measured label-to-centroid interval
changes mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset by \mapCost{} points and does not reorder the
\rankCkpts{} released checkpoints, an effect at the benchmark's sampling-resolution floor. That
near invariance follows from Gen1's speed composition. It characterizes the metric's resolution
and does not show the absence of a temporal discrepancy (Supplement Secs.~2, 11--12, 14, 15).

\paragraph{Label side.}
Across the \dsecSeqCount{} DSEC training sequences whose exposure files
resolve~\cite{gehrig2021dsec}, published exposure width varies by a factor of \dsecExpRatio{}
against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, and \dsecPinnedFrac\,\% of frames in
\dsecCeilingSeqs{} sequences are pinned at an auto-exposure ceiling of \dsecExpMax\,\micro s. In
those sequences the event-rate spectrum carries the 100-Hz line and its harmonics, which the
daytime control lacks. Within-box dispersion of event time inside one exposure is
\sigmaEvtNight\,\micro s against an analytic null of \sigmaEvtNullNight\,\micro s. A frame-level
permutation null gives \permNull\,\micro s, and the excess remains after the clean-pixel control.
Random-position boxes show that the dispersion is not specific to annotated object regions.
Within-exposure event timing therefore contains an illumination-locked component, and it cannot be
attributed to object timing without separating that component first (Supplement Secs.~9 and 16).
DSEC-Det ships more than one annotation version~\cite{beyondduality2026}, and Supplement Sec.~8
names the one measured here.


\section{Support-conditioned AP}
\label{sec:sc}

Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} shows that one released protocol pools detections that received different histories. This section scores the \rankCkpts{} released Gen1 checkpoints at a stated history and compares the result with the pooled score. It follows the counterexample with the corresponding protocol.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
For a labeled frame scored at window $r$ and a history length $H$, the detector starts from a reset recurrent state at window $r-H+1$ and runs through window $r$, and the detections of window $r$ are scored with the labels, confidence threshold, NMS and evaluator of the released-protocol dumps. We call the resulting AP at fixed $H$ Support-Conditioned AP. $H$ is the number of recurrent windows allowed before scoring, $H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$. A frame with fewer than $H$ earlier windows receives $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=\min(H,r+1)$ windows instead. All checkpoints are scored on the same \fhNCommon{} frames, those with $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$. The other \fhNExcl{} of the \fhNAll{} labeled frames have fewer than 21 windows of history and are excluded. The common frames lie in \fhNSeq{} sequences. The column \emph{pooled} is the released evaluation of each checkpoint, in which the history depends on the release and on the frame, restricted to the same common frames. The score of the released evaluation on all \fhNAll{} frames is listed separately. The access log of every run shows that the number of distinct windows read equals $H_{\mathrm{forced}}$ and that the first window read is $r-H_{\mathrm{forced}}+1$ in every cell of every checkpoint (Supplement Sec.~13).

The RVT release carries its state beyond 21 windows, which the fixed-$H$ protocol does not.

\paragraph{What the comparison supports.}
$H$ is the history allowed before scoring, not a measurement of how many windows influenced the detections. The access log shows which windows were read. In an oldest-window probe, replacing the oldest admitted window changed the output in \fhProbeHit{} of \fhProbeN{} probed cells for four checkpoints and in \fhProbeHitT{} of \fhProbeN{} for RVT-\texttt{t}. Every $H$ starts the RVT checkpoints from a reset state, which the RVT release does not do at evaluation: it carries its state and clears it only at the first sample of a sequence. A short $H$ therefore combines a shorter history with a reset the checkpoint did not see in this form, and the experiment does not separate the two. Supplement Sec.~13 reports that RVT-\texttt{s} and RVT-\texttt{b} score \rvtBoundResetS{} and \rvtBoundResetB{} points higher on the velocity-evaluable subset when a reset block is given seventeen to twenty windows instead of one to four. The SSM-ViT release already evaluates each chunk from a reset state (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). The ordering change is therefore a result for these five checkpoints under this evaluation. It does not rank the architectures, and it does not show that either family is better in general.

\paragraph{What a benchmark should record.}
The three quantities of Sec.~\ref{sec:support} are not recoverable from a nominal timestamp, and the intervention above shows a case in which the missing one changes the score. We propose that a benchmark record, for each scored sample, five fields. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant at which the sample is scored. The \emph{input window} is what the detector reads at that timestamp. The \emph{state-reset policy} states where the recurrent state is cleared, including chunk boundaries and sequence starts. The \emph{effective history length} is the number of windows read, as the access log of the evaluation run records it. The \emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired. Together, the reset policy and the history length determine the effective temporal support. A score is then reported at a declared history length, as in Table~\ref{tab:sc}, together with the distribution of history lengths that the pooled score contains. These fields do not equalize the support across checkpoints. They record it, so that scores can be compared at a history that both detectors received.

\section{Scope and limitations}

Each supporting audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:support}, Supplement Secs.~15 and 16) is conditional on its
stated instrument: local window-ablation sensitivity, nominal-time association under the reported
regression specification, and a local constant-velocity displacement surrogate. The primary chunk-boundary intervention is established on two released SSM-ViT checkpoints under their released streaming evaluator, the second testing that the violation is not confined to one weight set. Gen1 and DSEC are separate timing audits, not premises of that counterexample.

Support-Conditioned AP (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}) covers five released Gen1 checkpoints on the \fhNCommon{} frames that have at least 21 windows of history, at $H\le 21$, with a reset state at every $H$ and intervals from \fhB{} sequence-bootstrap draws. It does not test $H>21$, and it does not measure how many windows influenced a detection. The orderings it reports hold for these checkpoints under this evaluation, and no claim about the two architecture families in general follows from them.

\section{Conclusion}

At the same evaluated timestamp, with frames, weights, labels and evaluator fixed, moving only the chunk boundary shifts S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP over all labeled validation boxes and \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset. One pooled score in the released SSM-ViT pipeline combines predictions carrying one to twenty-one windows of recurrent history. The chunk boundary is an unreported degree of freedom of the protocol. It changes the recurrent support behind the reported score while every recorded quantity stays fixed, which is exactly a failure of temporal-support identifiability.

The result does not single out a canonical temporal-support statistic, nor attribute the shift to one stage of the pipeline. The predictor-side and label-side audits stay descriptors of timing rather than estimates of recurrent support.

Support-Conditioned AP is the corrected counterpart of the counterexample: it fixes the recurrent history behind each score. For the five released Gen1 checkpoints on the \fhNCommon{} common frames, S5-B ranks third in the pooled score and first at each of $H=1,5,10,21$, the Kendall $\tau$ between the pooled and fixed-$H$ orderings lies between \fhTauHOne{} and \fhTauHTwentyone{}, and for three pairs the pooled and $H=1$ gaps have intervals that exclude zero on opposite sides. Part of the short-$H$ deficit of the RVT checkpoints may come from the reset that every $H$ imposes on them, and the experiment does not separate it from the shorter history. These statements hold for these checkpoints under this evaluation.

Where a protocol does not record the support behind a score, the reported score is underdetermined. Two scores at the same nominal timestamp are not performance at the same effective time when the recurrent support behind them differs. Comparisons across recurrent architectures should therefore report or control the three latent temporal quantities alongside the score. Concretely, a benchmark can record the nominal timestamp, the input window, the state-reset policy, the effective history length and the label-acquisition support of each scored sample, and report the score at a declared history length.
