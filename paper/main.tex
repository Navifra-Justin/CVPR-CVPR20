\begin{abstract}
A detection benchmark compares a prediction with a label at one query time and
scores their difference as displacement. That timestamp does not uniquely specify
the temporal support of either side of the comparison. Event-detection benchmarks
collapse three quantities into it: a predictor's evidence support, its effective
output time, and the exposure-conditioned support of the labels. This paper
separates the three and measures what released artifacts identify of each. The released configuration of
the detector examined here ends its input window at the label instant, and
occluding each bin of the released \texttt{rvt-t} checkpoint's input puts that
window's influence centroid at \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms. The detector is recurrent,
so that window is not all of its evidence: occluding whole past windows leaves the
newest carrying \histShareTwenty\,\% of the influence, with a tail the measured
horizon does not resolve.
Its detections, regressed on the label velocity as a vector alongside
that velocity rotated a quarter turn as a placebo no temporal offset can reach,
put the output time at $\ladderFullTau\,\pm\,\ladderFullTauSE$\,ms: consistent with the
label instant, which its training objective encourages, and \outCentroidSigmas{}
standard errors from the window's own centroid. This establishes the
separation, not an equality with the label timestamp. That offset
spans \satLo{} to \satHi\,ms across specifications with geometric controls, never
reaching the $+\rvtCentroidMagMeas$\,ms the centroid would require.
Despite that measured separation, displacing the ground truth across the
interval moves mAP by \mapCost{} points: an unchanged score is not evidence that a
prediction and its evidence are temporally aligned, because the score stays flat
while the two instants it conflates are measurably apart. At the ninetieth
percentile of label speed the interval is \outPxNinety\,px of displacement,
the annotation dispersion of the labels themselves, and \outPxNinetynine\,px at the
ninety-ninth, so the weak response is a property of the metric, not an absence of
displacement. On the label side no
per-object label time is inferred: the \dsecCeilingSeqs{} DSEC sequences pinned at
the auto-exposure ceiling carry the harmonic series of a \flickerLineHz\,Hz mains
flicker, undocumented in the release, and what follows is an attribution limit.
A per-object temporal statistic measured inside one of those exposures cannot be
attributed to object timing without separating the illumination-driven structure
first.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An object detection benchmark evaluates a prediction $\hat{s}$ against a ground
truth $s^{*}$ through a function $\rho(\hat{s},s^{*})$ of position, shape and
class, indexed by a query time $t$. The query time selects which label the
prediction is compared against. The metric itself has no argument in which a time
could appear, so a temporal discrepancy is scored as displacement. Neither side
of the comparison is a state at an instant. A predictor computes $\hat{s}$ from a
window of sensor data. A label is produced by an exposure, an annotator or
tracker working on the exposed image, and a geometric transfer into another
sensor's frame.

The predictor side of that mismatch is fixed by a released configuration and is
measurable from released artifacts. The event branch of the detector examined
here~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} consumes a window of \rvtWindow\,ms divided into
\rvtBins{} bins, and the preprocessing script of its own repository builds the
window boundaries by counting backwards from label timestamps, so each window
ends at the label it is scored against (Sec.~\ref{sec:window}). Under uniform
weighting the evidence in such a window is centered \rvtCentroidMag\,ms before
the instant the label asserts. Whether the trained network weights its bins
uniformly is a separate question, and occluding each bin in turn on real
validation samples answers it: the influence-weighted centroid is
\rvtCentroidMeas\,ms (Sec.~\ref{sec:influence}). A network whose newest window sits
there and whose output is scored at the label instant extrapolates over that
interval.

The training objective already places the output at the label instant, and the
measurement of Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime} is consistent with it, at
\ladderFullTau\,$\pm$\,\ladderFullTauSE\,ms, which is a failure to reject and not
a demonstration that they coincide. The objective does not fix the interval
that measurement leaves open, \outInterval\,ms between that window's centroid
and the measured output time. The velocity that measurement regresses on is finite differenced from the same
labels the residual is measured against, and a forward difference shares their
noise, returning an offset from a detector that has none. The estimator is therefore calibrated on synthetic tracks before it is used,
and carries a placebo term on which no lag can load.

The second measurement concerns the same class of undeclared timing property on
the label side. DSEC
publishes each frame's own exposure interval, and across the \dsecSeqCount{}
training sequences whose exposure files resolve, the width varies by a factor of
\dsecExpRatio{} against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, with
\dsecPinnedFrac\,\% of frames pinned at an auto-exposure ceiling of
\dsecExpMax\,\micro s in \dsecCeilingSeqs{} sequences (Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure}).
Those \dsecCeilingSeqs{} carry the harmonic series of a \flickerLineHz\,Hz mains
flicker, with nothing at the odd multiples of \mainsHz\,Hz, and between
\sixLockedLoPct\,\% and \sixLockedHiPct\,\% of labeled boxes carry more power at
the fundamental than at an off-frequency arm on the same events
(Sec.~\ref{sec:flicker}). A \flickerPeriodUs\,\micro s period inside a
\dsecExpMax\,\micro s exposure displaces event times with no motion involved, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:cost} reports what that leaves of a per-object statistic.

The single timestamp the metric is indexed by does not specify the temporal
support of either side of the comparison. Three quantities are collapsed into it,
and this work separates
and measures each on released artifacts: the evidence support of a released
recurrent event detector, whose newest window is centered
\rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms before the label instant and carries
\histShareTwenty\,\% of its influence, the rest reaching past the second
measured (Secs.~\ref{sec:window}--\ref{sec:influence}); the output time of the
same checkpoint, identified by a vector regression with a placebo term and a
sign-flip control on the velocity estimator, which places it at the label instant
and \outInterval\,ms from that window
(Secs.~\ref{sec:outputtime}--\ref{sec:extrapolation}); and the
exposure-conditioned support of DSEC's labels, where the harmonic series of a
\flickerLineHz\,Hz mains flicker in \dsecCeilingSeqs{} ceiling-exposure
sequences bounds what a per-object temporal statistic measured inside one
published exposure window can be attributed to (Sec.~\ref{sec:flicker}).

The consequence claimed is a failure of identifiability under the metric, not a
ranking change. Displacing the ground truth across the measured interval moves
mAP by \mapCost{} points and reorders none of the \rankCkpts{} released
checkpoints, while the same interval is \outPxNinety\,px of displacement at the
ninetieth percentile of label speed, the size of the labels' own annotation
dispersion, and \outPxNinetynine\,px at the ninety-ninth
(Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime}, Tab.~\ref{tab:strata}). A score that stays within
\mapCost{} points over shifts that large cannot certify that a prediction's
effective time and the temporal location of its evidence agree, and
Tab.~\ref{tab:strata} locates the insensitivity in the speed composition of the
benchmark rather than in an absence of displacement.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Recovering a time offset from image-plane displacement.} Qin and
Shen~\cite{qin2018temporal} calibrate a clock offset online by modeling a feature
observation as $z(t_d)=[u,v]^{\top}+t_d V$, with $V$ differenced from consecutive
observations. That is the estimator of Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime}, published in
visual--inertial odometry, and the same work identifies the offset by making one
scalar reconcile two channels. Temporal calibration removes the offset between
two \emph{sensor} clocks by pooling over features, so the spread of per-feature
estimates is its error bar. Here the offset lies between a predictor's clock and
a \emph{benchmark's} label clock. Streaming perception~\cite{li2020streaming}
folds wall-clock compute latency into average precision and re-anchors any
detector from box coordinates alone; later work adapts to the measured runtime
delay~\cite{yang2022streamyolo}, a continuous-stream framework scores the latest
available output against high-rate ground truth and reports reordered
rankings~\cite{chu2026stare}, and temporal tolerance has been argued for
time-series event detection~\cite{softed2024}. Each of those latencies is the
time from an event to an output and falls as the device gets faster. Sensor
latency, the illumination-dependent delay between a change and the timestamp the
sensor assigns it, is measured and corrected at
microseconds~\cite{yang2024latency}, and nighttime event imaging corrects an
illumination-dependent trailing by learning a per-event timestamp
calibration~\cite{liu2024nighttime}; both target the sensor, while here the
released exposure metadata and event stream bound what a per-object temporal
statistic computed inside one published exposure can be attributed to. Inside
the citation graph of LET-3D-AP~\cite{hung2022let}, a latency-aware average
precision scores 3D detection at the instant a prediction becomes available on
a named device~\cite{rethink3d2025}, again compute latency; the interval
measured in Sec.~\ref{sec:extrapolation} is fixed by a data representation. The
event--RGB frequency mismatch is named in~\cite{faod2024}, which scores with
unmodified average precision.

\paragraph{Decomposing localization error along a privileged axis.}
LET-3D-AP~\cite{hung2022let} decomposes camera-only 3D localization error along
the line of sight and defines an average precision tolerant of the longitudinal
part, and reports a camera-to-label synchronization gap as a per-dataset interval
that depends on horizontal object position. Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime} keeps the
idea of a privileged axis but replaces the sensor's line of sight with the
object's own velocity vector, and estimates a time rather than defining a
tolerant score.
TIDE~\cite{bolya2020tide,hoiem2012diagnosing} and DETAD~\cite{alwassel2018detad}
bucket detection error atemporally, so a temporal offset falls inside their
localization bucket, HOTA~\cite{luiten2021hota} takes the label clock as exact,
and the response of average precision to isotropic box
perturbation~\cite{apsensitivity2022} supplies the isotropic null the supplement
uses.

\paragraph{Per-object time in labels.} Waymo's \texttt{camera\_synced\_box} solves
$t_{\mathrm{capture}}$ per object and stores the resulting box shift rather than
the time~\cite{waymolabelproto,sun2020waymo}. Nu\~{n}ez et
al.~\cite{nunez2026annotations} measure within-frame per-object annotation
offsets on Argoverse\,2 and TruckScenes and define a dispersion of the
\emph{spatial} error. Each computes a per-object time in order to remove it. The
event-vision label formats carry the field: the Prophesee dtype used by Gen1, the
1\,Mpx dataset and DSEC-Det has a per-box \texttt{t}. What no release fills is
that field's per-object degree of freedom. Across the \ddSeqs{} released DSEC-Det
sequences, \ddBoxes{} boxes take \ddDistinctT{} distinct values of \texttt{t},
\ddBoxesPerT{} boxes per value, and those values are the \dsecFramePeriod\,ms
frame clock.

\paragraph{Event benchmarks and their exposures.}
Gen1~\cite{detournemire2020gen1} labels are drawn on gray-level images assembled
from asynchronous per-pixel measurements. On the 1\,Mpx
dataset~\cite{perot2020megapixel} the event count inside a labeled box at its own
timestamp is often too small to support detection~\cite{kugele2023howmany}.
DSEC-Det~\cite{gehrig2024lowlatency} labels by tracking~\cite{pang2021qdtrack} on
rectified frames and warps into the event view, and
Ev-3DOD~\cite{cho2025ev3dod} interpolates lower-rate annotations into high-rate
3D labels. That a frame's exposure is long relative to event timing has been
stated: REFID~\cite{sun2023refid} argues that accumulating events from a single
frame timestamp loses information, deblurring work names auto-exposure as why the
width is unknown~\cite{kim2022unknownexposure}, and RENet~\cite{zhou2022renet}
uses a DSEC exposure interval as its event window while describing it as short;
none reads the released per-frame widths or measures what is inside one. State space backbones~\cite{zubic2024ssm} removed by architecture the
dependence of an event model's score on inference-time window duration, and
adaptive spatial-temporal windows~\cite{sui2026astw} retrain a detector under
each of nine partitioning strategies and report the score of each. Both treat the
window as a design variable to choose. Here it is a released fact to locate.
That the released window is causal is already in print:
LEOD~\cite{wu2024leod} states that event detectors take only events triggered
before the query time, and builds a time-flip augmentation on the consequence
that forward inference favors receding objects; where inside that window the
evidence sits is not measured there. Protocol
work supplies the standards these measurements are held
to~\cite{oncalibration2024,stratification2024,northcutt2021labelerrors,ercan2023evreal}:
a repaired protocol can reverse a field's conclusion, a reported score is an
estimate with a variance, and a label audit is judged by whether what it finds
moves benchmark results.

\section{The interval between evidence and label}
\label{sec:predictor}

\subsection{Definition}

Let a predictor $P$ emit a state at query time $t$ with normalized temporal
support kernel $k_{P}(u)$, the share of the emitted state's information coming
from $t+u$, of centroid $c_{P}$. A benchmark scoring at a nominal instant treats
that centroid as zero. Of the benchmarks and released detectors whose
documentation we read, Gen1, 1\,Mpx, DSEC-Det, DSEC-3DOD, DSEC-Flow, MVSEC, the
RVT release and the SSM-ViT release~\cite{zubic2024ssm}, none states or checks
it.

The \emph{effective timestamp} $\tau_{P}$ is not $c_{P}$. For a predictor linear
in position measurements at bin centers $c_{k}$ with weights $a_{k}$ summing to
one, the conditional expectation of the output under constant velocity is the
true position at $\tau_{P}=\sum_{k}a_{k}c_{k}$. Non-negative weights give
$\tau_{P}=c_{P}$, while a predictor that uses older evidence to compensate its
own lag has negative weights and moves $\tau_{P}$ toward zero without moving
$c_{P}$. Sections~\ref{sec:window} and~\ref{sec:influence} measure the newest window's
contribution to $c_{P}$,
Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime} measures $\tau_{P}$, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:extrapolation} reports the interval between them.

\subsection{The window and the instant it is indexed by}
\label{sec:window}

Two released files fix the input side. The dataset configuration
\texttt{config/dataset/gen1.yaml} of the detector's own
repository~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} names the representation
\texttt{stacked\_histogram\_dt=50\_nbins=10}, a window of \rvtWindow\,ms divided
into \rvtBins{} bins of \rvtBinWidth\,ms. Line \rvtSourceLine{} of
\texttt{scripts/genx/preprocess\_dataset.py} constructs the array
\texttt{ev\_repr\_timestamps\_us\_end} by counting backwards from a label
timestamp in steps of the window duration, and extends it between consecutive
label timestamps. Each representation is therefore indexed by the end of its
window, and those ends are placed on label times. A window attached to a label at
time $t$ holds the events of $[t-\rvtWindow\,\mathrm{ms},\,t]$, whose uniform
weighting centroid falls \rvtCentroidMag\,ms before the instant $t$ at which the
label asserts a state. Both statements are read from the released source, and
the \rvtCentroidMag\,ms figure is a reference point rather than a property of
the trained network.

\subsection{The measured influence profile}
\label{sec:influence}

$\tau_{P}$ is defined on influence weights, which are measurable for a released
checkpoint. We take \rvtInfluenceSamples{} samples of the released Gen1
validation split with the released \texttt{rvt-t} checkpoint, warm the recurrent
state for \rvtWarmSteps{} steps, and occlude each bin in turn, reading a bin's
influence as the relative $L_{2}$ change its occlusion produces in the emitted
detection tensor. The recurrent state given to the occluded pass is the state the
unoccluded one received, and the state it returns is discarded, so the profile is
a property of the current window and not of a perturbed history.

The profile varies across bins and is balanced between halves
(Fig.~\ref{fig:predictor}a): the coefficient of variation across the \rvtBins{}
bins is \rvtInfluenceCV{}, the heaviest bin carries \rvtInfluenceMaxMin$\times$
the lightest, and the five older bins hold \rvtHalfOlder\,\% of the influence
against \rvtHalfNewer\,\% for the five newer. The influence-weighted centroid is
therefore \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms against the uniform-weight value of
\rvtCentroidUnif\,ms, a difference of \rvtCentroidDiff\,ms or
\rvtCentroidDiffPct\,\% of one bin width. For this configuration the centroid of
the input is the uniform-weight centroid to within a fraction of one bin, so the
configuration fact of Sec.~\ref{sec:window} is a measured property of the
trained network.
This is the current window's contribution to $k_{P}$, taken with the recurrent
state held at the value entering the step, and not $k_{P}$ itself. Zeroing a bin
also pushes the input off the data manifold, so the fill is varied and the
instrument replaced.
Setting a bin to its dataset mean gives \occMean\,ms and taking it from an
unrelated sample \occSwap\,ms; a gradient sensitivity, which occludes nothing at
all, gives \occGrad\,ms. The four centroids span \occSpread\,ms against a
bootstrap standard error of \occBootSE\,ms on the zero-fill centroid, so the spread is
the choice of instrument.

\paragraph{The rest of the kernel.}
The detector is recurrent, so $k_{P}$ does not end at the window. Restoring the state saved before the step at
$t-50L$, occluding that whole window and letting the state propagate to $t$
measures each past window's influence over \histSamples{} samples. The newest
carries \histShareTwenty\,\% of the total over a \histHorizonB\,ms horizon, half
is reached only by \histHalfLag\,ms, and zeroing the state changes the output by
\histResetChange{} of its norm against \histCurrentChange{} for removing the
newest window entirely. The tail does not decay to zero within that second, so
the influence-weighted centroid follows the horizon instead of converging:
\histCentroidTen\,ms over \histHorizonA\,ms of history and
\histCentroidTwenty\,ms over \histHorizonB\,ms. Filling a past window from an
unrelated sample rather than with zeros deepens the plateau, the decay across the
second falling from \histDecayZero{} to \histDecaySwap{}. This predictor's
support therefore has no centroid the measured horizon identifies.

\paragraph{Across the released checkpoints and a second architecture.}
RVT releases three Gen1 checkpoints differing only in embedding width, attention
head dimension and FPN depth. SSM-ViT~\cite{zubic2024ssm} releases two more in
which each stage's ConvLSTM is replaced by an S5 state space layer, at \ssmParamsS{}
and \ssmParamsB{} M parameters against RVT's \capParamsS{} and \capParamsB{}; it
distributes RVT's own preprocessed Gen1 and inherits its window construction. On the same sequences, and
driving each the way its own release drives it, all \archCkpts{} checkpoints put the
newest window's centroid between \archHi{} and \archLo\,ms, none further than
\archUnifMax\,ms, or \archUnifPct\,\% of one bin, from the uniform-weight value.
The state the SSM release carries between evaluation chunks reaches none of its
outputs, which bounds that model's support at the \ssmEvalChunk{}-window chunk its
evaluation steps through; the supplement measures it. The support behind that
window is not fixed in the same way either: over \capSamples{} samples of the
three RVT capacities, the newest window's share of a \capHorizon\,ms horizon
falls from \capShareT\,\% to \capShareB\,\% and the support centroid moves
from \capSuppT{} to \capSuppB\,ms.

\subsection{The output time}
\label{sec:outputtime}

The influence profile is a statement about the network's input; a network that
weights its window evenly can still emit a state describing a later instant,
and that quantity is measured from the same checkpoint's own detections.

A detector reporting the state an object occupied $\tau$ earlier displaces its
prediction by $-\tau v^{*}$, where $v^{*}$ is the label velocity, so
$\tau=-\tau_{P}$ on the axis of Sec.~\ref{sec:predictor}. That is a statement
about a vector, and it is estimated as one. With $\hat{c}$ the
predicted center, $c^{*}$ the label center, $\hat{u}=v^{*}/\lVert v^{*}\rVert$
and $\mathrm{R}$ a quarter turn,

\begin{equation}
\hat{c}-c^{*}=b-\tau\,v^{*}+\rho\,\mathrm{R}v^{*}+\gamma\sqrt{A}\,\hat{u}+\varepsilon
\label{eq:outreg}
\end{equation}

\noindent is fitted by stacking both components of every match into one least
squares problem, with standard errors clustered by sequence. It is the temporal
calibration of~\cite{qin2018temporal} carried to a box center, taken as a slope
rather than as the per-object ratio $-\langle
\hat{c}-c^{*},\hat{u}\rangle/\lVert v^{*}\rVert$, whose variance diverges as its
denominator approaches zero. Fitted on these rows its mean is
$\ratioMean\,\pm\,\ratioMeanSE$\,ms, on the very hypothesis Sec.~\ref{sec:extrapolation}
excludes, with an interquartile range of \ratioIQR\,ms. Written this way $\tau$ is identified by
construction under the model: no magnitude of the residual is taken, and $\tau$
does not load on a direction estimated from the same noisy velocity.

$\mathrm{R}v^{*}$ is the label velocity rotated a quarter turn: it has the same
magnitude and measurement noise as $v^{*}$, and no temporal offset can load on
it, so $\rho$ is a placebo whose null distribution the estimator supplies for
itself.
$\sqrt{A}\,\hat{u}$ absorbs a displacement along travel proportional to box side,
which is needed because that residual grows with box size and box size correlates
with label speed at \outSpeedSizeCorr{} on this split.

\paragraph{The finite difference used for the velocity.}
$v^{*}$ is finite differenced from labels, and the choice of difference changes
the answer. A forward difference $(c^{*}_{k+1}-c^{*}_{k})/\Delta$ carries the
label noise $\delta_{k}$ of the current center with weight $-1/\Delta$, and the
residual $\hat{c}-c^{*}_{k}$ carries the same $\delta_{k}$ with weight $-1$;
their covariance is $\operatorname{Var}(\delta)/\Delta$ when $\tau$ is exactly
zero. A backward difference carries $\delta_{k}$ with the opposite sign and
produces the opposite artifact. The centered difference
$(c^{*}_{k+1}-c^{*}_{k-1})/(\Delta_{f}+\Delta_{b})$ does not use $c^{*}_{k}$ and
is free of it. Fitting all three is therefore a control on the estimator
itself, calibrated before use. On \synTracks{} synthetic tracks whose detector
has no lag whatever, with label centers corrupted at the dispersion the
supplement's difference ladder measures on Gen1 (\labelSigmaGen\,px), the forward
estimator returns \synFwd\,ms and the backward one \synBack\,ms against a closed
form of \synPred\,ms, they sum to \synSum\,ms, and removing the label noise
returns all three to \synZero\,ms. The same simulation recovers an injected lag
at \synRecover{} of its size, the attenuation an errors-in-variables treatment
predicts at \synLambda{} for this noise.

\paragraph{The measurement.}
The released checkpoint is run over the Gen1 validation split with its recurrent
state carried across steps, and its detections at each labeled frame are matched
to the ground truth at an overlap of \outIoUfloor{} or above. Requiring both
label neighbors, so that a centered velocity exists, and discarding the matches
whose two neighbors coincide on the integer label lattice, leaves \outMatches{}
of \outRowsTotal{} over \outSeqs{} sequences at a median overlap of \outIoUmedian{}. Gen1's \texttt{track\_id} is a per-frame index rather
than a persistent identifier, so association is rebuilt by same-class overlap
between adjacent label times. No speed floor is imposed.

Table~\ref{tab:outtime} is a specification ladder, and the specification was
chosen by the placebo rather than by $\tau$. Under Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} with only
the box-side term, $\tau=\ladderMinTau\,\pm\,\ladderMinTauSE$\,ms, but $\rho$ is
\ladderMinPl\,ms at \ladderMinPlSigmas{} standard errors, which indicates a term
omitted from Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} that $\mathrm{R}v^{*}$ is a proxy for. A split by
the sign of the horizontal velocity locates it: $\rho$ changes sign across that
split, at \plRight\,ms and \plLeft\,ms, which a rotational term would not do.
Most label motion here is horizontal, so $\mathrm{R}v^{*}$ maps a horizontal speed
onto a vertical residual, where a bias differing between oncoming and
same-direction traffic lands with nothing rotational present. Entering
box height and image row along $\hat{y}$ and box width and image column along
$\hat{x}$, and then a constant vector per sequence, returns the placebo to
\ladderFullPl\,ms at \ladderFullPlSigmas{} standard errors. That is the
specification reported, and the criterion that selected it is a coefficient on a
regressor orthogonal to $v^{*}$.

Under it, $\tau=\ladderFullTau\,\pm\,\ladderFullTauSE$\,ms, which is
\outTauSigmas{} standard errors from the label instant and \outCentroidSigmas{}
standard errors from the influence centroid of Sec.~\ref{sec:influence}; a
cluster bootstrap over sequences puts the \bootCI\,\% interval at
[\outBootLo,\,\outBootHi]\,ms. The controls do not absorb a lag and the axis
pairing does not make the answer: an injected offset is recovered at
\injRecovery{} of its size under the full control set, entering all four
geometric terms on both axes gives $\satTau\,\pm\,\satTauSE$\,ms, and across
every specification carrying geometric controls $\tau$ spans \satLo{} to
\satHi\,ms.

\subsection{The interval between evidence and output}
\label{sec:extrapolation}

The two measurements are of one architecture at one configuration. Its newest input
window is centered \rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms before the label time
(Sec.~\ref{sec:influence}) and its measured output time is consistent with that label time
(Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime}). The measurement does establish the separation: the
output is incompatible with that window's centroid at \outCentroidSigmas{}
standard errors, and \outInterval\,$\pm$\,\outIntervalSE\,ms is the point
estimate of their distance under the reported specification rather than a latency
of this architecture. On the convention of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} the centroid
hypothesis is $\tau=+\rvtCentroidMagMeas$\,ms, and no specification carrying
geometric controls reaches it: $\tau$ spans \satLo{} to \satHi\,ms across them. The rest of the support lies further back, so
\rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms is the smallest interval consistent with the measurement
rather than its value. The standard error quoted is the output-time term's; the
influence
centroid contributes \occBootSE\,ms of sampling error and \occSpread\,ms of
dependence on the instrument (Sec.~\ref{sec:influence}), and the specification
spread above is wider than either.

That outcome is the one the training objective enforces: the detector is trained
against the ground truth at the label time on a window ending there, so a measured
offset near zero reports the objective. The objective does not enforce the
interval, and the instant the output describes and the centroid of the evidence
used to produce it are different quantities that one benchmark timestamp stands
for. At the ninetieth percentile of label speed on this split, \outSpeedNinety\,px/s, the
\rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms is \outPxNinety\,px of displacement, comparable to the
annotation dispersion of the labels themselves (\labelSigmaGenAnn\,px), and at the
ninety-ninth it is \outPxNinetynine\,px. That window's centroid and the output time
are both measurable from released artifacts alone.

\paragraph{The interval on the benchmark's own metric.}
The benchmark can be asked in its own units: displacing the ground truth of
every labeled frame by $\delta v^{*}$ scores the released predictions against the state
each object occupied $\delta$ later and leaves the detector untouched; a detection
carries no velocity of its own. Over \mapFrames{} frames and \mapGT{} boxes,
\mapGTvelPct\,\% of which carry a centered velocity and the rest are ignored, mAP
against $\delta$ is smooth with its maximum at \mapArgmax\,ms
(Fig.~\ref{fig:predictor}b). On the axis of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} that is
$\tau=\mapArgmaxTau$\,ms, on a top flat to \mapCost{} points across the
interval. A bootstrap over frames puts that argmax between \mapArgLo{} and
\mapArgHi\,ms in \bootCI\,\% of \mapArgBoot{} resamples, and in \mapArgNever{}
of them does it reach that window's centroid, so it identifies no value.

The flatness of that curve is the second result. The whole sweep moves
mAP by \mapSweepSpan{} points and displacing the ground truth to that window's
centroid costs \mapCost{} points, \mapCostPct\,\% of \mapZero{}: the separation measured above is real and this metric does not
register it. Nor does it on any of the \rankCkpts{} released checkpoints: displacing
each to the instant the metric prefers for it leaves their ordering unchanged in
\rankStrata{} speed strata, and cannot change it, since within a stratum the
largest gain from alignment is \rankRatio\,\% of the smallest gap between adjacent
models (supplement). A displacement costs overlap in
proportion to $\tau\lVert v^{*}\rVert$ against the box side, and the
median label speed here is \mapSpeedMedian\,px/s on a \mapSideMedian\,px box, so
\rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms is \mapPxMedian\,px; Fig.~\ref{fig:sweepreal} shows both
placements on one validation frame near the top of the speed range.
Table~\ref{tab:strata} locates the
flatness in that composition: \stShareSlow\,\% of the moving boxes travel below
\mapCutLo\,px/s, where the displacement is \stPxSlow\,px, and the sweep deepens
to \mapSpanHigh{} points between \mapCutMid{} and \mapCutHi\,px/s, where it is
\stPxHigh\,px, past the labels' own annotation dispersion of

\section{A mains signature in DSEC's ceiling-exposure sequences}
\label{sec:flicker}

\subsection{Exposure and labels}
\label{sec:labelclock}

The predictor side of the mismatch is fixed by a window; the label side is fixed
by an exposure. DSEC publishes per-frame exposure metadata, and the files resolve
for
\dsecSeqCount{} train sequences. The width varies by a factor of
\dsecExpRatio{} against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, and within
one recording by up to a factor of \dsecWithinSeqRatio{}. The auto-exposure
ceiling of \dsecExpMax\,\micro s holds \dsecPinnedFrac\,\% of frames, in
\dsecCeilingSeqs{} of the \dsecSeqCount{} sequences, and the distribution is not
continuous over that range (Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure}).

The released DSEC-Det labels comprise \ddBoxes{} boxes on \ddTracks{} tracks
across \ddSeqs{} sequences, every label sits on the frame clock, and the median
spacing between consecutive label times is \ddSpacingMedian\,\micro s. Over
\ddTriples{} triples of consecutive labels on a track,
\ddInterpBelowThresh\,\% of middle box centers fall within a hundredth of a
pixel of their neighbors' linear interpolant, where interpolated labels would
place that fraction near unity, so no construction term from interpolation
arises within this archive. DSEC-Det ships more than one annotation
version~\cite{beyondduality2026}; the supplement names the one measured here.

The label center carries noise measurable on the labels themselves: on a
uniform grid the variance of the $k$-th difference of a track's box center is
$\binom{2k}{k}\sigma_{c}^{2}$ plus a motion term that halving the sampling rate
multiplies by $4^{k}$. Over \ntracksNoise{} DSEC-Det tracks the two routes agree
from $k=4$ upward and extrapolate to $\sigma_{c}=\sigmaCtotal$\,px, of which the
\sigmaClattice\,px integer lattice the release stores box centers on contributes
\sigmaCquant\,px. Median box-center image speed is \labelSpeedMedian\,px/s, with
\labelSpeedPninetynine\,px/s at the ninety-ninth percentile.

\subsection{The line, and the per-pixel test}
\label{sec:line}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\figasset{figs/fig7_ceiling_vs_day.pdf}{\columnwidth}
\caption{One 350\,\micro s slice of the ceiling-exposure sequence

\label{fig:flickerreal}
\end{figure}

Swiss mains is \mainsHz\,Hz, so lamp intensity flickers at \flickerLineHz\,Hz;
an event camera responds to log-intensity change, so both edges of that
oscillation produce events and the arrival rate carries the harmonic series
(Fig.~\ref{fig:flickerreal}). Sweeping \harmGridLo--\harmGridHi\,Hz on each whole recording, with no
band fixed in advance and each frequency divided by the median of its own
neighbourhood, returns that series in every ceiling sequence: the ceiling median
is \harmOne{} at \flickerLineHz\,Hz, \harmTwo{} at \harmSecondHz\,Hz and
\harmThree{} at \harmThirdHz\,Hz, and the second harmonic is the strongest line
in \harmSecondStrongest{} of the \dsecCeilingSeqs{}, never falling below
\harmTwoMin{}. At the odd
multiples of \mainsHz\,Hz there is nothing: \harmFifty{}, \harmOneFifty{} and
\harmTwoFifty{} at \mainsHz{}, \harmOddOneHz{} and \harmOddTwoHz\,Hz. That absence is what a rectified
supply predicts and what a periodic source of any other origin need not obey. The
daytime sequence carries the fundamental at \harmDayOne{} and the second harmonic
at \harmDayTwo{}, against \harmTwoMin{} for the weakest ceiling sequence.

Per pixel, the Rayleigh statistic $Z=R^{2}/N$, for $R$ the resultant length of the
event phases at the test frequency, is $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ under independent uniform
phases, so its null has median \flickerZnull{}, ninety-fifth percentile
\flickerNullPninetyfive{} and a rate of \flickerNullRatePct\,\% at $p<10^{-3}$.
Independence is checked: over
\flickerLivePixNight{} pixels of the ceiling sequence with at least
\flickerMinEvents{} events, the same estimator on the same events at
\flickerCtrlHz\,Hz returns \flickerCtrlNightZ{} with a $p<10^{-3}$ rate of
\flickerCtrlNightRatePct\,\%, and on \flickerLivePixDay{} daytime pixels at
\flickerLineHz\,Hz it returns \flickerCtrlDayZ{} at
\flickerFracLockedDayPct\,\%, so per pixel the reference distribution is not
exceeded off the line or away from the lamps. At \flickerLineHz\,Hz on the
ceiling sequence the median $Z$ is \flickerZmedian{} and
\flickerFracLockedPct\,\% of those live pixels reach $p<10^{-3}$. DSEC's own
description attributes high night event rates near street lamps to flashing
lights~\cite{gehrig2021dsec}; neither that nor dim-light
noise~\cite{graca2021noise} is quantified per pixel in the release.

\subsection{All six ceiling sequences, per labeled box}

The per-pixel test above is one sequence against one daytime control; the same
statistic taken per labeled box, over the events inside that box during that
frame's own exposure window, extends to every sequence that reaches the
ceiling, and the supplement tabulates all \dsecCeilingSeqs{} of them over
\sixBoxes{} boxes. The median per-box Rayleigh statistic at \flickerLineHz\,Hz
runs from \sixZC{} to \sixZD{} with a median over sequences of \sixZmedian{},
against \sixZctrl{} for the same estimator at \flickerCtrlHz\,Hz on the same
events.

The analytic null does not transfer from a pixel to a box: over
\nullOffFreqs{} off frequencies the pooled median is \nullOffMed{},
\nullInflation{} times the analytic one, since events inside one box during a
\dsecExpMax\,\micro s exposure arrive in bursts, so no per-box $p$-value is
quoted, and \flickerCtrlHz\,Hz is the most favorable of those frequencies, the
line standing at \localRatioSweep{} times their pooled median. A ratio needs no
null: dividing $Z$ at the line by the median of $Z(f)$ over each box's own
neighbouring frequencies gives \localLine{} over \localBoxes{} boxes against
\localSham{} at \flickerCtrlHz\,Hz, and \localAbovePct\,\% of boxes exceed that
arm's ninety-ninth percentile. One window is transform-limited to
\localTransformLimit\,Hz, so a box establishes that the modulation reaches it and
not which harmonic carries it; the identification is the sweep of
Sec.~\ref{sec:line}.

\subsection{A per-object temporal statistic under that signature}
\label{sec:cost}

That signature bounds any per-object temporal statistic inside one published
exposure window, and the bound is measurable. Let a labeled frame have published
exposure $[a,b]$ of width $w=b-a$ and mid-exposure $m=(a+b)/2$, and let box $i$
contain the events falling inside it with $t\in[a,b]$. The \emph{event centroid}
of the box, $\bar{t}_{i}=\frac{1}{N_{i}}\sum_{k\in i}t_{k}-m$, is the mean event
time referred to the instant the dataset indexes the frame with; were the
evidence inside each box uniform over $[a,b]$, $\bar{t}_{i}$ would have variance
$w^{2}/12N_{i}$, so the excess over that null is
$\sigma_{\mathrm{evt,excess}}^{2}=\mathrm{Var}_{i}(\bar{t}_{i})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}w^{2}/12N_{i}$.
The subtrahend holds for arrivals independent and uniform on $[a,b]$, which
these are not: reassigning a frame's box events among its boxes at random,
which leaves everything common to the frame intact and destroys only box
identity, gives a null of \permNull\,\micro s, \permRatio{} times the analytic
one rather than above it, and an excess of \permExcess\,\micro s; the
supplement gives that construction. A box enters with at least
\minEvents{} events and a frame with at least \minObjects{} qualifying objects,
which \sigmaEvtFramesNight{} of \sigmaEvtFramesLabelled{} labeled frames of
\texttt{zurich\_city\_09\_a} do. The within-frame standard deviation of
$\bar{t}_{i}$ is \sigmaEvtNight\,\micro s against an analytic null of
\sigmaEvtNullNight\,\micro s, the excess is \sigmaEvtExcessNight\,\micro s, and
the standard deviation exceeds that frame's own null on
\sigmaEvtFracNightPct\,\% of frames (one such window rendered in the
supplement). On \texttt{interlaken\_00\_c}, where the exposure
is \sigmaEvtExpDay\,\micro s and the \minEvents{}-event floor leaves
\sigmaEvtFramesDay{} usable frames, the standard deviation is
\sigmaEvtDay\,\micro s against a null of \sigmaEvtNullDay\,\micro s and the
excess is zero.

The supplement tabulates what that dispersion can be attributed to. Boxes of the
same shapes at random positions give \excessRandBoxes\,\micro s, above the
annotated boxes', so it is a property of where evidence falls during the
exposure and not of objecthood; removing the \excessMaskPix{} pixels that reach
$p<10^{-3}$ leaves it at \excessClean\,\micro s: a weakly modulated population
remains in the clean arm. Without any mask, the excess rises
monotonically with each box's own modulation depth $m=2|C|$ at
\flickerLineHz\,Hz, from \excessModQone{} to \excessModQfour\,\micro s across
quartiles of $m$ over \modBoxes{} boxes, against \excessShamQone{} to
\excessShamQfour\,\micro s for the same stratification at \flickerCtrlHz\,Hz, so
part of the trend belongs to stratifying on any amplitude statistic, and no
unmodulated stratum exists to separate the two: $m$ has a tenth percentile of
\modPten{} against a floor of \modFloor{}. The dispersion is therefore a
property of the event stream inside the published window that this data does
not resolve into scene and illumination components; until they are separated, a
per-object temporal statistic measured inside one of these exposures cannot be
attributed to object timing.

That dispersion bounds the precision to which one scalar timestamp denotes an
instant: \sigmaEvtExcessNight\,\micro s, whatever its source, displaces a box
center by \pxAtMedian\,px at the median object and \pxAtPninetynine\,px at the
ninety-ninth percentile, \pxFracMedian{}--\pxFracPninetynine\,\% of
$\sigma_{c}$, and the frame-common offsets are smaller, \offsetAllEvents{} and
\offsetInBoxes\,\micro s from the mid-exposure instant over all events and the
labeled subset. Against the dispersion, the largest of the
three label-side terms, the newest window's influence centroid of
Sec.~\ref{sec:influence}, measured on a different dataset, is larger by a factor
of \termRatio{}, and it is the one a declared timestamp would remove.

\section{Limitations}

A per-object temporal claim on these sequences is bounded rather than
identified: the one spatial statistic chosen for its immunity to a spatially
uniform source does not survive its own background control, an equal-area
annulus of each box's surroundings aligning at least as well as the object in
\egoRingBetter{} of them, an alignment that is ego-motion, which this data does
not separate from object timing. The narrow-exposure contrast is one sequence, so
width and illumination move together in it; a denoiser ablation is not run.

On the predictor side, everything is one dataset and \archCkpts{} checkpoints
of two architectures sharing a repository, and occlusion reports a sensitivity
that equals a linear weight only for a predictor linear in its bins. The support measurement weights windows by the norm of a difference and neither
fill is the training distribution, so its shares are a ranking and its centroid is
horizon-dependent. The output-time measurement is a null bounded by
its precision: it excludes an offset as large as the newest window's centroid at
\outCentroidSigmas{} standard errors, says nothing about one of a few
milliseconds, and assumes constant velocity over the label
neighbourhood; the specification was chosen by a coefficient
orthogonal to $v^{*}$, though the step returning it to null also moved $\tau$.

\section{Conclusion}

A detector's newest input window is centered \rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms before the
instant it is scored at, within \archUnifMax\,ms across \archCkpts{}
checkpoints of two architectures; most of its influence lies further back; and its
output time is bounded away from that window at \outCentroidSigmas{} standard
errors under geometric controls, a separation average precision registers as
\mapCost{} points and no reordering; and DSEC's
\dsecCeilingSeqs{} ceiling exposures bound what a per-object statistic inside
one can be attributed to.
