## One-sentence thesis

Every event / event–RGB benchmark scores a prediction against a ground truth that lives on a *different clock
with a different temporal support*, and every metric in use is a scalar over a single nominal instant, so a
prediction that is at the right place along the object's path at the wrong time is booked as spatial error;
we **measure that time**, in milliseconds, for released checkpoints, show that it is (i) large, (ii) predictable
from each predictor's *measured* temporal support, and (iii) **not a single number per output** — different
objects in one prediction tensor live at different instants — and we show that the flagship inter-frame ground
truths against which the field measures low latency are, in the DSEC-Det case exactly and in the DSEC-3DOD case
to a measured degree, their own interpolation prior.

**What this thesis no longer promises.** It does not promise that "the ranking is not the ranking mAP reports."
R10 is right that the primary flip candidate (RVT-B vs S5-ViT-B, published gap ≈ 0.5 mAP) is inside a
sequence-level bootstrap, and R3 is right that a disagreement between `AP` and a deliberately permissive
`AP^⊥` is the null hypothesis rather than a finding. The ranking claim survives only in the one form that is
legitimate (§4.4, `AP^sync`) and only as a **supporting** result with a paired bootstrap and an ordering
probability attached.

## The assumption we kill

**Named assumption — the *Instantaneous-State Assumption* (ISA):**

> A benchmark tuple `(prediction ŝ, ground truth s*, query time t)` is evaluated as `ρ(ŝ, s*)` under the
> assumption that both `ŝ` and `s*` are the state of the world *at the single instant t*.

Formally, let a predictor `P` emit a state from a data window with a normalized temporal support kernel `k_P`
(how much of the emitted state's information comes from each past instant), with centroid `c_P = ∫u·k_P(u)du`
and width `w_P = 2√(∫(u−c_P)²k_P)`. Let the ground-truth process `G` have its own kernel `k_G`. ISA is the
claim `c_P = c_G = 0` and `w_P = w_G = 0`.

**Of the six benchmarks we examined, none states, enforces or checks this; in all six it is false; and the
violation is of order 25–50 ms.** (The scoping matters and R8 was right to demand it — the previous draft
wrote a universal quantifier and then scoped it in the same sentence.)

### Why it is universal on the benchmarks we checked (with sources)

Two corollaries the field has not stated:

1. **The high-rate ground truth of the two flagship "low-latency" event benchmarks is seeded by a linear
   motion model and then, in one case, refined.** DSEC-Det inter-frame labels are, by the authors' own
   sentence, pure linear interpolation. DSEC-3DOD's 100 FPS labels are linear interpolation seeded, refined
   against VFI-synthesised sensor data by human experts. E0 measures how much survives the refinement.
   (R8 was right that the v1 sentence — "*is* a linear motion model" — stated the answer before the
   measurement.)
2. **Representations with a constant *event count* have a temporal support whose width is a function of scene
   activity.** Faster motion produces more events and hence a shorter window — **but only up to the rate
   ceiling.** R1 is right and the correction makes the confound worse, not weaker: refractory dead time and
   bus arbitration cap the event rate, so `w_P(v)` is **non-monotone** in speed and turns around past the
   ceiling (ESVA, CVPR 2026, derives and measures the rise-then-fall curve). No metric in the field can see
   `w_P` at all, let alone its non-monotonicity.

### What is verified about the RVT window, and what is not

`experiments/e02` verified from the released source, not from a paper's description of it, that in
`uzh-rpg/RVT` both `config/dataset/gen1.yaml` and `gen4.yaml` set
`ev_repr_name: 'stacked_histogram_dt=50_nbins=10'`, and that `preprocess_dataset.py:405` builds
`ev_repr_timestamps_us_end` by counting **backwards from label timestamps**. So the network sees events over
`[t−50 ms, t]` and is scored on the state at `t`.

**The input representation's uniform-weight information centroid is therefore `t − 25 ms`. That is arithmetic
about the input tensor. Whether the trained network weights its ten bins uniformly is unmeasured** — this is
stated explicitly in `experiments/e02` and we were wrong in v1 to call the resulting output-latency figure "a
prediction, not a hypothesis." R3, R6, R7, R8 and R9 all caught the same sentence and they are all right.

The repair is not softer language. It is **E1b (§8)**, which measures the network's actual per-bin influence
profile by bin occlusion on a released checkpoint, converting `t − 25 ms` from an assumption about the input
into a measured property of the model, and turning C3 from an arithmetic restatement into a real prediction
with a measured predictor.

## The reformulation

### 1. The unit of error changes

Current unit: **pixels** (or dB, or IoU). Proposed unit: an ordered pair **(pixels ⟂ to motion, milliseconds
∥ to motion)**, plus a **dispersion** in milliseconds. A pixel is not a time; a benchmark whose only unit is
the pixel cannot report a time, which is why the field has no number for its own dominant fast-motion failure.

### 2. The geometric identifiability argument (unchanged, and it survives every objection)

A *timing* error is a **one-parameter** perturbation: a single scalar `δ` must simultaneously explain the box
translation (`δ·v*`), the box scale change (`δ·ṡ*`), the aspect change, and — in 3D — the yaw change. A
*spatial* error is unconstrained (2 or 7 DoF). The temporal hypothesis is therefore **over-identified**: we fit
one `δ` per object and check whether it explains all channels. R2 named this the strongest piece of mathematics
in the round; R5's collision check leaves it untouched, because LET-3D-AP has one channel and therefore no
falsification test.

### 3. The decomposition, with the estimator corrected

```
e      = ĉ − c*(t)                                  ∈ ℝ²
e_∥    = ⟨e, û⟩                                     (px, signed)
e_⟂    = e − e_∥ û                                  (px, vector)
δ̂_i    = e_∥,i / ‖v*_i(t)‖                          (ms)          [translation channel, per object]
ε_s    = log( ŝ / s*(t) )
δ̂_s    = ε_s / ( d log s*/dt )                      (ms)          [scale channel]
κ      = |δ̂ − δ̂_s| / (σ_δ̂ + σ_δ̂_s)                  (consistency, O(1) under H_temporal)
```

**Errors-in-variables (the repair R10 and R3 demanded).** The v1 estimator was weighted least squares of
`e_∥` on `‖v*‖` through the origin. `‖v*‖` is not observed; it is finite-differenced from labels, so the
regression is errors-in-variables and the naive slope is attenuated toward zero by
`λ_att = Var(v)/(Var(v)+σ_η²)`. Two distinct problems, with different sizes on different datasets:

- **Classical dilution** (noise in the divisor). For a central difference over ±Δ with per-frame box-centre
  noise `σ_c`, `σ_η ≈ σ_c/(√2·Δ)`. On DSEC-Det (Δ = 50 ms, track IDs, `σ_c` to be measured in week 3 from
  track residuals; if `σ_c ≈ 1 px` then `σ_η ≈ 0.014 px/ms`) this is small relative to the spread of image
  speeds, and the attenuation is of order a percent. **This is why DSEC-Det, not Gen1, is now the primary
  testbed for `τ̂`.** All three numbers in that sentence are measured before the estimate is reported.
- **Smoothing bias** (the divisor is the wrong quantity). On Gen1 (labels at up to 4 Hz, Δ = 250 ms), a
  central difference returns the *mean* velocity over 500 ms, not `v*(t)`. This is the dominant EIV problem on
  Gen1 and it is **not** classical dilution; it is correlated with acceleration and it is not fixed by Deming
  regression. R10's diagnosis is right in direction; on the dataset the paper now leads with, the mechanism is
  different and smaller, and we say both things.

**What we run, and what we report:**

1. `τ̂_naive` — WLS through the origin, as v1. Reported as a **lower bound in magnitude**.
2. `τ̂_Deming` — Deming regression with variance ratio `λ = σ_ε²/σ_η²`, with `σ_η` propagated from a measured
   `σ_c` and `σ_ε` from the residual.
3. `τ̂_IV` — instrumental variables. **Instrument: the velocity estimated from a disjoint earlier label pair
   `(t−2Δ, t−Δ)`.** Measurement noise is independent across disjoint pairs while true speed is strongly
   autocorrelated, which is exactly the condition an IV needs. This is the cleanest of the three and it costs
   nothing.
4. `λ_att` reported explicitly, per dataset, in the main paper.
5. Calibration: the kinematic rig (§8, E4) injects a known `τ` with *exact* velocity, then re-estimates it
   from finite-differenced velocity at each dataset's label rate, and reports the recovered slope. **Control
   C4 now points at the denominator, which is what R10 asked for, not only at the injected offset.**

**Effect on the effect size, stated in advance:** attenuation biases `|τ̂|` toward zero, so every correction
we apply makes the reported latency **larger**, not smaller. That is an uncomfortable direction — a reviewer
is entitled to suspect a correction that inflates the headline — which is why we pre-register that the
**headline number is `τ̂_IV`**, that `τ̂_naive` is printed beside it in every table, and that if the two
differ by more than a factor of 1.5 we report the discrepancy as a finding about the labels rather than
choosing the larger.

Per-method latency and speed-independent bias:

```
(τ̂_P, b) = argmin Σ_i w_i ( e_∥,i − τ‖v*_i‖ − b )²      [with the EIV correction above]
```

`b` is genuine, speed-independent spatial bias (calibration, parallax, event–RGB extrinsics). Reporting
`(τ̂, b)` separates "your model is late" from "your warp is wrong" — currently both land in TIDE's `loc`.

### 4. The metric family, rebuilt so nothing rests on a permissive score

This is the largest change in the revision and it answers R3 directly.

**4.1 The relaxation budget is now measured, not read off a config.**

```
τ_max := ( w_G + min(w_P^decl, w_P^meas) ) / 2
```

R6 is right that `w_P^decl = 50 ms` is the support of the *tensor*, not of the *prediction*: RVT carries LSTM
state across the sequence, S5-ViT carries SSM state, DAGr carries graph-structured memory, so the actual
support is unbounded with an unknown decay. E1c measures it (truncate recurrent state at `k` steps, measure
`τ̂(k)` and `dτ̂/dk`; `w_P^meas` is the `k` at which `τ̂` stabilises within its CI, converted to ms).

We take the **minimum** of declared and measured. `w_P^meas` will almost certainly be *wider*, which would buy
a larger relaxation budget and hence a more flattering permissive score. Taking the minimum guarantees the
paper never benefits from its own correction. Both numbers are printed.

R2 is also right that on Gen1 `w_G` is "the ATIS integration width", which is per-pixel and intensity-dependent
and never published as a number — so on Gen1 `τ_max` **is** a free parameter under another name, and we say
so in the table caption. On DSEC-Det `w_G = 50 ms` is the interpolation half-width stated by the authors, and
`τ_max` is genuinely derived. **This is a second reason DSEC-Det is the primary testbed.**

**4.2 Headline numbers (a measurement, not a score).**

| symbol | what it is | why it cannot be self-serving |
|---|---|---|
| `τ̂` (ms) | EIV-corrected per-method latency, sequence-bootstrap CI | it is not a score; no method "wins" on it, and a large `\|τ̂\|` is neither good nor bad without the downstream claim |
| `b` (px) | speed-independent spatial bias | same |
| `σ_τ` (ms) | within-output dispersion, against a permutation null | same |
| `w_P^meas` (ms) | measured temporal support | same |
| `AP` | standard, unchanged | it is the field's metric |

**4.3 Diagnostics (reported in one decomposition table, never in the abstract, never as a ranking
instrument).**

```
AP^⟂      : before IoU matching, translate each prediction along the GT tangent by the amount minimising
            the distance, clipped to |δ| ≤ τ_max.                                    DIAGNOSTIC ONLY
AP^⟂L     : the LET-3D-APL affinity-weighted form — a matched TP is credited by (1 − |δ|/τ_max) rather
            than fully — so the permissive metric cannot simply dominate.            SENSITIVITY CHECK
AP^iso    : identical to AP^⟂ but the translation direction is free (disc of radius ‖v*‖τ_max).  CONTROL
R         : (AP^⟂ − AP)/(AP^iso − AP), against a SIMULATED null.                     ANISOTROPY
```

R3's charge — that `AP^⟂` forgives exactly the error we want forgiven, forgives *more* for later methods, and
that pre-registration cannot fix a definition — is **correct and unanswerable, and we do not answer it. We
comply with it.** `AP^⊥` is no longer a headline number, is no longer a ranking instrument, and does not
appear in the abstract. It survives only as the numerator of `R`, whose entire job is to be able to come back
vacuous, and beside `AP^⟂L`, whose job is to show that the diagnostic conclusion does not depend on the
un-penalised form. R2 is also right that `R ≈ 0.5` is derivable only for the per-pair `E[cos²θ]`, not for a
ratio of AP differences; we simulate the AP-ratio null on the actual score distribution (a re-score, therefore
free) and report `E[cos²θ]` as the *primary* anisotropy statistic because that one has a derivable null.

**4.4 The one legitimate ranking instrument: `AP^sync`.**

```
AP^sync   : re-anchor every prediction by ĉ ← ĉ − τ̂_P · v̂_pred, where v̂_pred is estimated from the
            METHOD'S OWN consecutive outputs — never from ground truth — then score on the UNMODIFIED
            STANDARD METRIC.
Δ_clock   = AP^sync − AP        # reported WITH ITS SIGN
```

This is the instrument the ranking claim now uses, and the distinction from `AP^⟂` is the whole argument.
`AP^sync` is **not permissive**. It is standard `AP` applied to a prediction file that has been post-processed
using nothing but the method's own outputs. It **can go down** — R2 is right that v1's "by construction
`AP ≤ AP^sync ≤ AP^⟂ ≤ AP^iso`" is false for the first link, and the correction is load-bearing rather than
cosmetic: only `AP ≤ AP^⟂ ≤ AP^iso` holds by construction, and `Δ_clock < 0` is informative, not a bug.

Because the post-processing is legal, cheap, and GT-free, **anyone can apply it to their own detector and get
the same benefit.** That is exactly the shape R3 says is the only legitimate flip (C10, On Calibration of
Object Detectors, ECCV 2024: fix a broken protocol, re-measure the same quantity, watch cheap post-hoc methods
overtake train-time SOTA). A change of order between `AP` and `AP^sync` is a statement that one method was
being credited for a clock error that a five-line post-processing step removes. A change of order between `AP`
and `AP^⟂` is two different metrics disagreeing. We now claim only the first.

**4.5 Statistics — the procedure, in full (R3's objection 9).**

R3 swept the window and found **no CVPR/ICCV/ECCV/NeurIPS paper that puts confidence intervals on a
leaderboard ranking at all.** There is therefore no precedent to hide behind and the procedure must be stated
in the main paper rather than assumed. We cite C12 (*A Framework for Efficient Model Evaluation through
Stratification, Sampling, and Estimation*, ECCV 2024) as the nearest in-venue methodological template.

- **Unit of resampling: the sequence.** Detections inside one recording share scene, tracks, illumination and
  ego-motion, so the exchangeable unit is the recording, not the detection and not the frame. We report the
  number of sequences as the effective `n` for every CI. Where `n_seq < 20` we additionally report a **block
  bootstrap over 10 s temporal blocks**, which raises the effective `n` while preserving within-block
  dependence, and we print both intervals.
- **Procedure.** `B = 10 000` resamples with replacement over sequences. Within each resample, the *full*
  COCO-style AP is recomputed from the dumped prediction files for **both** methods on **the same resampled
  set** — the bootstrap is **paired**, which is essential: the paired difference has far lower variance than
  the difference of two independent bootstraps, and an unpaired procedure would make every flip claim
  unfalsifiable in the conservative direction. `τ̂` is re-estimated inside each resample (the EIV fit is refit,
  so the CI covers estimator uncertainty as well as sampling uncertainty).
- **Reported quantity.** For every pair: the 95 % percentile CI of `ΔAP` and of `ΔAP^sync`, **and the ordering
  probability** `P(ΔAP^(b) > 0)` for each metric.
- **When a flip may be claimed.** Only if `P(order under AP) ≥ 0.95` **and** `P(order under AP^sync) ≤ 0.05`.
  A pair whose `ΔAP` is inside the bootstrap — which R10 shows is the case for the v1 primary candidate at
  ≈ 0.5 published mAP — cannot produce a flip claim under this rule, by design.
- **Multiplicity.** The list of tested pairs is **pre-registered in the supplement before any prediction file
  is scored**, and is small (≤ 12 pairs, chosen for a mechanistic reason, not for expected yield).
  **Holm–Bonferroni** across the tested pairs. R3's arithmetic — ~100 orderable pairs at α = 0.05 yields ~5
  spurious flips — is the reason the pair list is capped rather than maximised, which reverses v1's Death-1
  mitigation (a) ("maximise the pool"). Maximising the pool was the wrong instinct and we retract it.

**4.6 `σ_τ`, absorbed from Team 04 — and the null it never had.**

`δ̂_i` is already per-object. So within-output dispersion is free:

```
σ_τ²      = Var_i( δ̂_i )  over objects in one prediction tensor, after removing the per-method constant τ̂_P
σ_τ,excess² = σ_τ² − E_i[ σ_δ̂,i² ]        # subtract the estimator's own per-object variance
```

The null: `δ̂_i` is noisy, so `σ_τ > 0` trivially, and R3's criticism of Team 04 — that `σ_τ`, the declared
kill shot, *has no null model* — is fatal as Team 04 posed it. Ours: a **permutation null**. Re-draw the
object set of each "frame" by sampling objects from *different* frames within the same sequence and speed
stratum, and recompute the dispersion. If within-frame dispersion is not detectably smaller than this
across-frame dispersion, there is no frame-level clock coherence, `σ_τ` carries nothing, and we drop it (K5).
This is the mirror of control C2 and it costs one CPU pass.

**Why we can run this where Team 04 could not.** Team 04's `σ_τ` needed intra-exposure ground truth at
240 Hz from FE108/FE240hz; `experiments/e03` establishes that the full FE108/FE240hz release with the 240 Hz
Vicon ground truth is **application-gated** (only an STNet-hosted preprocessed val/test split is open), and
R4 measured the alternate portal `fe108.dluticcd.com` returning HTTP 416 with a 0-byte body and refusing TLS.
Our `σ_τ` needs only per-object matched pairs on tracked labels, which DSEC-Det supplies for free with track
IDs at 20 Hz. **The quantity survives the dataset death that killed the idea that proposed it.**

`σ_τ` also does not require acceleration in the ground truth, which matters: R3's cross-cutting finding is
that a linearly-interpolated track has **zero acceleration by construction**, so acceleration-driven
measurements (Team 04's content dependence, Team 07's `Δt ≈ aT²/24v̄`, Team 02's `ν`) do not survive on
DSEC-Det at all, while a first-order bias measurement does. `σ_τ` is dispersion of a first-order quantity
*across objects*, not across time: different objects have different speeds and different textures, and the
interpolated GT gives each its own constant velocity. It survives.

**What we cut to make room** (the standing instruction is that narrowing scope is fine and weakening the
central claim is not): the ESIM/v2e simulator and the rendered three-GT release (§5), the event-VFI PSNR
strand, and the 1 Mpx / `gen4` arm. Itemised in §8.

### 5. The protocol: TS-Split, and the reporting contract

**TS-Split.** Report every metric stratified by deciles of `M = ‖v*‖·τ_max/d`, `d = √(wh)`, and report the
top-`M` decile as a headline slice (`AP@HiM`). R3's audit is that this is the only stratification in the round
that is structurally correct, because `M` is computable from ground truth and declared specifications *before
any model is run* — it cannot be a post-hoc fast-motion subset. We keep it unchanged and we now say why in the
paper.

**The three-GT release, in the form that survives.** v1 proposed rendering `GT_inst` / `GT_exp` / `GT_lin` in
a simulator. That is cut (R4: v2e is **not** on PyPI, its SuperSloMo checkpoint is Google-Drive hosted and is
"the single biggest silent failure mode in this literature"; esim-py is not on PyPI either; MultiFlow's
`train.tar` is 1.63 TB and `val.tar` 325 GB and must be deleted from any plan on 990 GB of disk). What
replaces it is **free and real**: DSEC-Det already ships both ground truths. The 20 Hz anchor labels are
`GT_anchor`; the released inter-frame labels are `GT_lin`; and the difference is exactly the interpolation
prior. Scoring the same predictions against both, and publishing the spread, tells a future author how much
of their inter-frame score is agreement with the annotation prior — with no simulator, no rendering, and no
dependency that can go dark. This is E0's deliverable and it is strictly more credible than a rendered
version, because a reviewer's first question about a simulated three-GT release is whether the simulator's own
exposure model is the thing being measured.

**Reporting requirement we propose for the community.** A submission must declare `(c_P, w_P)` — the centroid
and width of its temporal support — the way it currently declares FLOPs and latency, and must state whether
`w_P` is a constant or a random variable driven by scene activity. Two methods with different `w_P` are not
comparable under a single mAP number, and today nothing forces anyone to say what `w_P` is. R10's
cross-cutting attack on the whole round — *"is the effect you are measuring larger than the effect of the
event-window choice you made yourself?"* — is a question only this formalism can even pose, and the honest
answer for event detection is **yes, it is the window**, at 50 ms against a 1.5 ms daytime exposure
(`experiments/e01`). That is this paper.

### 6. The one "method" we allow ourselves — which is also the product

**Temporal re-anchoring (≈ 5 lines, training-free, no GT at inference):** estimate each track's velocity from
the method's *own* consecutive outputs, shift every box by `−τ̂_P·v̂_pred`. If the decomposition measures
something real, this must recover most of `Δ_clock` **on the unmodified standard metric**. If it does not, our
metric is measuring an artifact and we say so.

R7 makes the correct observation that this is also the paper's entire practical product: if
`τ̂(RVT) ≈ −25 ms`, then an RVT box on a vehicle at 50 km/h describes the world **34.7 cm ago** and at
130 km/h **90.3 cm ago**, and the fix is to stamp the output at `t + τ̂` and let the existing motion model
cancel it. We propose no architecture, no loss, and no training. §7 and §11 state plainly what that is and is
not worth.

## What existing methods cannot express

1. **A scalar over one nominal instant has no coordinate for "when".** mAP, EPE, PSNR and 3D AP are functions
   `ρ(ŝ, s*)`. There is no argument slot in which a timing offset could appear, so it is projected onto the
   only slot available — position — and reported as localisation error. TIDE's taxonomy (cls/loc/both/dup/bg/
   miss) is entirely *atemporal*: our whole phenomenon lives inside `loc`.
2. **No label format can state its own support.** `(x, y, w, h, t, class)` asserts a point in time. Nothing in
   DSEC-Det, GEN1, 1 Mpx, EventVOT or DSEC-3DOD records the exposure, the interpolation kernel, or the
   detector latency that produced `t`. A benchmark cannot report an uncertainty it has no field for.
3. **A benchmark cannot express that one prediction tensor carries several timestamps.** This is `σ_τ` and it
   is the point at which the reporting-contract answer runs out: a declared scalar `(c_P, w_P)` cancels a
   constant, and cannot cancel a dispersion.
4. **Constant-count representations have a speed-dependent, non-monotone support width.** Their `w_P` is a
   random variable driven by scene activity, rising with speed and then turning over at the rate ceiling.
   There is no field, plot or metric anywhere in event vision that reports it, and mAP averages over it.
5. **"Spatially the best, temporally the worst" is not expressible on any current event leaderboard.** Nor is
   its converse, which is the more dangerous case: a method that is spatially mediocre but happens to have a
   support centroid matching the annotator's, and is rewarded for it.
6. **Where the seed's phenomenon becomes a measurement.** "Frames and events do not share the same temporal
   support" is currently expressible only as a *modelling* remark. In our units it becomes a number:
   `τ̂_event` vs `τ̂_frame` vs `τ̂_fused`, in milliseconds, from released checkpoints — which is exactly what
   BFlow's `E` vs `E+I` pair lets us compute (§8, E2).

*(v1's item on event-VFI PSNR preferring blur is deleted. It was, per R6, the sharpest event-VFI observation
in the round and it is also unquantified without the reference camera's duty cycle, and its data — BS-ERGB —
is dark per `experiments/e03`. An unquantified claim on a dead dataset is not worth a bullet.)*

air-traffic surveillance and trajectory prediction, and claimed as novelty "recognising that event–RGB
benchmarks are the setting where the borrowed device is decisive." That claim was **wrong**, and R5 found the

## Experimental plan

**Everything is inference-only on released checkpoints. We train nothing, and §8.6 defends that decision
against the reviewers who asked us to train.** Every number derives from a dumped prediction file re-scored on
CPU, exactly as TIDE does.

**Machine reality (measured, `experiments/`):** one RTX 5090 (GPU index 1) shared, **budget every job under
7 GB**, 990 GB disk, Docker only, `torch 2.7.1+cu128` on `sm_120` confirmed in `cvpr19-gpu-g1:torch2.7.1-cu128`.
Measured sustained bandwidth 4.56 MB/s from `download.ifi.uzh.ch`, 6.88 MB/s from HuggingFace.

**The toolchain risk R4 called fatal is closed, and R4's review should be read with the correction applied.**
`experiments/e04` records that the released `rvt-t.ckpt` loads into a model built by hand from the repo's own
configs under `torch 2.7.1+cu128` with **zero missing and zero unexpected keys** and runs forward
(`params=4.41M`, `in_res [256,320]`, `partition [4,5]`), with **`pytorch-lightning` never installed**. RVT
contains no `.cu`/`.cpp` source, so the `cu118` pin is a dependency pin, not a compilation requirement. Cost:
one working session, not the 4–6 days R4 budgeted. **What remains unverified is whether the loaded model

### E0 — Label forensics (zero GPU, days 1–5, the paper's floor)

Inputs: DSEC-Detection **Object Detection Labels (4.7 MB)** and DSEC-3DOD labels (from the 40.2 GB HF zip,
`mickeykang/DSEC-3DOD`, ~1.6 h at measured HF rate; if range-reading the zip central directory works we pull
label members only, otherwise we take the whole 40 GB, which is cheap).

1. **DSEC-Det: an identity check, not a statistic.** The authors state the inter-frame labels *are* linear
   interpolation. We verify that the released files reproduce, to the precision of the label file, the linear
   interpolation of their 20 Hz anchors. If it holds, the sentence is: *"inter-frame mAP on DSEC-Det is, to
   the precision of the released labels, a measurement of agreement with a constant-velocity prior."* This is
   cheaper, sharper and more falsifiable than v1's "median IoU > 0.9" and it is a one-line result either way.
2. **DSEC-3DOD: the real measurement.** These labels were interpolated **and then expert-refined**, so the
   deviation is nonzero. Report the full distribution of IoU(released blind-time box, pure linear oracle) —
   i.e. **how much information the released labels carry beyond the prior that seeded them.**
3. **The causal test.** A constant-velocity extrapolator seeded at anchors `(t−Δ, t)` with **no sensor data at
   all**, scored under each benchmark's own published inter-frame protocol, compared against the published
   event-method numbers. Report the fraction of the published inter-frame advantage the sensor-free baseline
   recovers.
4. **The prior-agreement ceiling.** Score the anchor-seeded linear oracle against the released high-rate GT
   and publish it as *"the score a method gets for having the right motion model and no sensor."* Any
   published inter-frame number below that ceiling is, in part, a measure of failing to match the prior.

**What E0 cannot say, stated in the paper.** It cannot say these benchmarks are worthless. The anchors are
real annotations, and agreement with a constant-velocity prior over a 50 ms interval is not obviously wrong
for rigid vehicles. The claim is about what an inter-frame number *measures*, not about whether the dataset
is good.

### E1 — Detection on Gen1: reproduction, `τ̂`, `σ_τ`, and the support axis

**Checkpoints (URLs and byte sizes verified live by HTTP HEAD, and independently re-verified by R4):**
RVT-{B,S,T} `gen1/rvt-{b,s,t}.ckpt` at `download.ifi.uzh.ch/rpg/RVT/checkpoints/` (222 937 503 B for gen1-B);
S5-ViT-{B,S} `gen1_{base,small}.ckpt` at `download.ifi.uzh.ch/rpg/CVPR24_Zubic/` (218 814 784 B). Data:
`gen1.tar` **98 628 976 640 B**, live, **no Prophesee registration needed for the preprocessed form** (6.0 h
to download).

**E1a — the reproduction gate (week 2, ~7.5 GPU-h).** Reproduce each checkpoint's published Gen1 mAP to
within ±0.3 using the authors' own validation path, under the ported stack. **Publish the reproduction table.
Any checkpoint that fails is dropped, not fixed.** This gate, not the download, is the critical path — R4 is
right that at 4.56 MB/s the whole ~400 GB is under two days of wall clock and v1's *"the critical path is
download bandwidth, not compute"* was wrong. Dump per-detection predictions (score, box, query timestamp) in
the same pass; no `τ`-sweep re-inference is needed because `δ̂` estimates the offset at continuous resolution
from a single pass.

**E1b — the per-bin influence profile, and the free `w_P` axis (week 4, ~10 GPU-h).** This is the repair for
the C3 collapse (R1, R2, R7) and for the "prediction, not hypothesis" overclaim (R3, R6, R7, R8, R9), and it
requires no training and no new preprocessing.

The stored representation is a stacked histogram, 10 bins × 5 ms over `[t−50 ms, t]`, 2 polarities → the 20
input channels that `experiments/e04` observed. Three levers follow, all free:

- **Leading-bin masking.** Zero the first `j` bins → an input supported on `[t − 5(10−j) ms, t]`, i.e.
  `w_P ∈ {5, 10, …, 50} ms` at a fixed query time. We run `j ∈ {0,2,4,6,8,9}` on RVT-T, RVT-B and S5-ViT-B.
- **Per-bin occlusion** (occlude bin `k` alone, `k = 1…10`) → the **influence weight** `ω_k`, measured as the
  induced shift of the predicted box centre along the motion tangent. The influence centroid `Σω_k c_k` is
  the network's *measured* information centroid, which is what `t − 25 ms` was standing in for.
- **Representation concatenation / re-binning** (sum adjacent 5 ms bins across two consecutive stored windows
  into 10 bins of 10 ms over 100 ms) → `w_P ∈ {100, 150} ms`. Conditional on the stored representation being
  additive in bins, which a stacked histogram is; verified in week 1 on one file before this lever is used.
- **Query-step subsampling** → inference frequency 20 → 10 → 6.7 Hz, which is the downward half of the
  S5-ViT inference-frequency sweep R1 recommends. (The *upward* half is not free: the tarball stores
  representations only every 50 ms, so querying at 100 Hz would need the raw, form-gated Gen1.)

Together these give `w_P` from **5 ms to 150 ms — a 30× range — on a single checkpoint with zero training**,
which is the predictor variance R1 and R2 said the design had eliminated.

*Controls, because masked input is off-distribution.* (i) Report mAP at every mask so the reader sees the
damage. (ii) Mask *trailing* bins symmetrically: if the `τ̂` shift tracks the centroid of the retained mass
with slope ≈ 1 in **both** directions while mAP degrades comparably, the effect is support, not OOD damage.
(iii) A mean-imputation arm (replace masked bins with the dataset-mean bin content instead of zeros) separates
"information removed" from "zeros are out of distribution". If the mean-imputation and zero-masking curves
diverge, we report the OOD confound and restrict the axis to the cross-family points from E2/E3.

**E1c — measured recurrent support (week 4, ~5.6 GPU-h).** R6's objection is correct and is folded in as a
first-class experiment rather than a footnote: RVT is recurrent (LSTM state across the sequence), S5-ViT
carries SSM state, so the support of the *prediction* is not the width of the *tensor*. Truncate the recurrent
state every `k` steps at inference, `k ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32,∞}`, on RVT-T and S5-ViT-S over a fixed 25 % Gen1 val
subset. Report `τ̂(k)`, `mAP(k)` and `dτ̂/dk`. Define `w_P^meas` as the `k` at which `τ̂` stabilises within its
CI, in ms (each sequence step is 50 ms). Then §4.1's `τ_max` uses `min(w_P^decl, w_P^meas)` so a wider
measured support never buys a more flattering permissive score.

**E1d — `τ̂`, `b`, `σ_τ` (CPU).** Build GT tracks by Hungarian IoU association across label frames;
finite-difference for `v*` and `d log s*/dt`; discard pairs with `‖v*‖·τ_max < 2 px` and **report the retained
fraction and the speed distribution of the discarded set**, since R10 correctly notes that this filter
truncates in a speed-correlated way and is a second bias in the same direction as attenuation. Compute
`τ̂_naive`, `τ̂_Deming`, `τ̂_IV`, `λ_att`, `b`, `σ_τ`, `σ_τ,excess` and the permutation null, plus the
decomposition table `(AP, AP^sync, AP^⟂, AP^⟂L, AP^iso, R, E[cos²θ], Δ_clock, Δ_TS)` overall and per `M`-decile.

**VRAM.** Gen1 is 240×304; `experiments/e04` built RVT-T at `in_res [256,320]`. Eval batch 4 keeps RVT-B under
7 GB with margin; if the shared card is busier than the brief's ~9 GB estimate we drop to batch 1 and pay in
wall clock, not in feasibility.

### E2 — The single-variable support manipulation: BFlow `E` vs `E+I` on DSEC-Flow (~2 GPU-h)

`uzh-rpg/bflow` releases, for the *same* architecture and training recipe, an **event-only** `E_LU4_BD2.ckpt`
(129 MB per R4's HEAD) and an **event+image** `E_I_LU4_BD2.ckpt` (161 MB). The image branch's temporal support
is ~100× wider than the event branch's. This is the cleanest single-variable manipulation of `w_P` in
existence and it was released by someone else. Data: `bflow/DSEC/train.tar` 149.6 GB (9.1 h).

Also **E-RAFT** MVSEC 20 Hz vs 45 Hz checkpoints (64 MB each): the same architecture trained against ground
truth at two different rates, on MVSEC, whose GT is fully public. ~1 GPU-h.

**Prediction:** `|τ̂_{E+I}| > |τ̂_E|`, and the effective-interval statement `Δt_eff = Δt(1 + δ̂/Δt)` differs
between them — *"the event+image model estimates displacement over an effective interval of X ms rather than
the nominal 100 ms."* **We report `EPE_⟂` as a decomposition and never as a verdict.** R3 is right that for
optical flow the along-flow component *is* most of the signal, so deleting it and declaring `E+I` worse would
be a comparison on a metric that removed the quantity the image branch most helps with.

**Contamination caveat, corrected.** DSEC-Flow's test GT is withheld, so we use the authors' own train/val
split. v1 asserted the contamination is conservative. R3 is right that this is asserted, not argued, and that
it is conservative for the *magnitude* claim and **not** for the ranking claim: training on the sequences
whose latency you then measure lets the model absorb the interpolation prior, shrinking `e_∥` toward zero.
Consequence: **`τ̂` magnitudes from E2 are reported as lower bounds, and no ranking claim is made on
contaminated data at all.**

### E3 — Event + RGB detection: DAGr on DSEC-Detection (~5 GPU-h) — and the primary `τ̂` testbed

`uzh-rpg/dagr` ships `dagr_s_50.pth` (333 MB) and `run_test_interframe.py`, which already writes inter-frame
detections to disk — precisely the artefact our re-scorer consumes. Data: DSEC-Detection New Sequences
81.9 GB + Remapped Images 34.1 GB + Labels 4.7 MB (60 sequences, 70 379 frames, 390 118 boxes, **with track
IDs**).

**DSEC-Det is now the paper's primary `τ̂` and `σ_τ` testbed**, for three reasons that the reviews forced:
20 Hz labels with track IDs give the smallest EIV problem of any dataset here (§4.3); `w_G = 50 ms` is stated
by the dataset's authors, so `τ_max` is genuinely derived rather than a free parameter under another name
(unlike Gen1's unpublished ATIS integration width); and the per-object matched pairs `σ_τ` needs are free.

Report `τ̂` as a function of offset within the 50 ms inter-frame interval — the prediction is a **sawtooth**

### 8.6 On training: we still train nothing, and here is why the reviewers who asked us to are wrong

R2's required fix #1 is *"retrain 3–4 RVT variants at different `dt` to give C3 a non-degenerate predictor
… ~8 GPU-h."* We are not doing it, and the reason is arithmetic, not preference.

1. **The data does not exist without a gate.** The preprocessed tarballs that make this plan possible are
   fixed at `stacked_histogram_dt=50_nbins=10`. Producing representations at other `dt` requires re-running
   `preprocess_dataset.py` over the **raw** Prophesee GEN1, and `experiments/e03` establishes that only the
   *preprocessed* form bypasses registration; the raw dataset is behind a request form with unknown turnaround.
   A plan whose central control depends on a form is not a plan.
2. **The cost is off by an order of magnitude.** RVT's own published training budget is ~2 days on an A100 for
   Gen1 and ~3 days on two A100s for 1 Mpx. R4's dependency audit puts it plainly: *"there is no world in
   which 9 GB of a shared 5090 trains a competitive recurrent event detector before November."* Three
   retrainings is 60–120 GPU-h on this machine, not 8 — three times the entire budget of this paper, for one
   control.
3. **The axis is available for free and the manipulation is cleaner.** E1b's bin masking is a *within-subject*
   manipulation of `w_P` on a fixed checkpoint over a 30× range. A retrained set is a *between-subject* design
   confounded by three retrainings' worth of optimisation noise. The free version is the better experiment,
   not merely the cheaper one.

**The "we train nothing" selling point therefore survives intact, and we say so plainly rather than hedging.**
The tension the reviewers identified is real and it is resolved in the paper's favour by a fact about the data
distribution, not by a preference. If a reviewer insists on a genuinely retrained `w_P` axis in rebuttal, the
honest answer is that it costs the gated raw dataset plus roughly three times this paper's compute budget, and
we will say that instead of promising it.

### 8.8 Defence against "you just invented a metric your own method wins"

The strongest form remains: **we do not propose a method.** Beyond that, and beyond the LET-3D-AP precedent
that a major benchmark adopted this exact device as an official metric, five controls:

- **C1 — Isotropic null (`AP^iso`, `R`, `E[cos²θ]`), with a *simulated* chance value.** Promoted from control
  to **week-2 gate**. If `R` and `E[cos²θ]` are inside the null, the relaxation is loosened IoU and we report
  the metric as vacuous and stop (K1).
- **C2 — Coherence.** A timing error is coherent across objects; spatial noise is not. Test (i) that the
  per-frame mean of `δ̂` has variance far below the independent-noise prediction, (ii) that the EIV regression
  `e_∥ ~ τ‖v*‖` has a near-zero intercept. A straight line through the origin whose slope is a time is not
  something a mis-specified metric produces. `σ_τ`'s permutation null is the mirror of this test.
- **C3 — Architecture prediction, made discriminative.** v1's C3 was arithmetic on a config recovered once,
  which R3 correctly called confirmatory rather than discriminative and R8 correctly noted could pass for the
  wrong reason (a dataset offset landing in the same range) and fail for the wrong reason (non-uniform bin
  weighting). C3 is now the **regression of measured `τ̂` on measured `w_P` across ≥ 12 configurations**
  (Panel C), with a slope, a CI, and a pre-registered interval. R3's standard — *"a slope-1 line of
  measured-vs-declared latency across five architectures is evidence; recovering −25 ms once is arithmetic"* —
  is the standard we now meet.
- **C4 — Injection–recovery, pointed at the denominator.** Inject a known offset and verify unit-slope
  recovery; then re-estimate through finite-differenced velocity at each real label rate and report the
  attenuation factor that the real estimates are corrected by.
- **C5 — The falsification method.** Training-free re-anchoring must recover most of `Δ_clock` **on the
  unmodified standard metric**, and `Δ_clock` is reported with its sign. If it does not, we report that our
  metric failed its own test.

## Risks

### Death 1 — The support–latency slope is null

*Why it could happen:* `τ̂` is dominated by the annotation pipeline (GoPro sync, QDTrack latency, ATIS
integration, homography warp), so it does not move when `w_P` moves.

*Why this is now survivable where the v1 ranking flip was not:* v1's headline was a flip that could simply
fail to occur, leaving nothing. Panel C **cannot come back empty**: a slope means `τ̂` is a property of the
predictor; a null slope means it is a property of the dataset, which is the statement *"every published number
on this benchmark is scored against labels offset by X ms"* — smaller, but a real finding, and it is Death 3's
publishable branch stated in advance rather than discovered in a rebuttal. What we retract from v1 is the claim
that this is as good as the alternative; it is not, and the abstract would have to change (K4).

### Death 2 — The decomposition is not identifiable on real data

*Why:* velocities finite-differenced from 4 Hz (Gen1) or 20 Hz (DSEC-Det) labels are noisy and smoothed;
at low speeds `e_∥/‖v*‖` explodes; association fails in crowded scenes; the tangent is ill-defined for
near-stationary or rotating objects.

*Mitigations:* the EIV treatment (§4.3) with three estimators and a reported attenuation factor; the
`‖v*‖·τ_max > 2 px` filter **with its retained fraction and speed-distribution published**, since the filter is
itself a speed-correlated truncation; the scale channel as an independent estimator with its power reported;
DSEC-Det (20 Hz, track IDs) as primary rather than Gen1; and E4 calibration where `v*` is exact.

*Fallback:* the paper narrows to E0's label forensics plus the reporting contract.

### Death 3 — "You are measuring the labels, not the models"

Still the sharpest attack, and v1's answer was structurally unavailable: the discriminator required variance
in `w_P` across the method set, and RVT-{T,S,B} and S5-ViT-{B,S} all consume the identical preprocessed
tarballs at `dt=50 ms nbins=10`, so `w_P` had **zero variance by construction** — the same design decision
that guaranteed a fair comparison eliminated the control. R1, R2 and R7 found this independently and they were
all correct.

*The repair:* four sources of genuine `w_P` variance, none requiring training (E1b's three free levers on a
single checkpoint spanning 5–150 ms; E1c's measured recurrent support; E2's BFlow `E`/`E+I` and E-RAFT
MVSEC 20/45 Hz; E3's DAGr sawtooth). Plus the explicit variance decomposition across (dataset, method): the
dataset-level offset is the common component of `τ̂`, the method-level offset is the residual, and both are
reported with CIs.

### Death 4 — The "so what" is too small

R7's audit is the most serious challenge to the paper's *importance*, and most of it is correct:
25 ms is 34.7 cm at 50 km/h, which is **half** the 50 ms sampling interval the same system already accepts
from a 20 Hz camera; a constant bias with a known sign is cancelled exactly and for free by a constant-velocity
Kalman step provided the timestamp is declared; and across seven accepted deployed event-vision results
(Nature 2024; T-RO 2026 velocimetry; CoRL 2024 obstacle avoidance; IROS 2024 RAMP-VO; CVPR 2025 Ev-3DOD;
T-RO 2026 de-snowing; T-RO 2026 radiance fields) **none reports a benefit that came from getting temporal
support right**, and the two with hard closed-loop numbers use no frame at all.

*What we concede:* the bias half is a **reporting** problem, not a research problem, and we say that sentence
in the paper. The paper's product for the bias half is a declared number and a reporting contract, not a
downstream accuracy gain, and we do not claim one.

*What we do not concede, and where the paper's importance actually sits:* R7's own hint is that the interesting
quantity is **variance, not bias**, and three regimes in this plan produce a temporal error that is not a
declarable constant.
1. **`σ_τ` (§4.6).** Different objects in one output tensor have different effective timestamps. A scalar
   declared timestamp cancels a constant and cannot cancel a dispersion. This is the one place the
   reporting-contract answer runs out, and it is measurable on DSEC-Det for free.
2. **Activity-driven support.** For constant-count and event-rate-adaptive representations, `w_P` is a random
   variable driven by scene activity, rising with speed and then turning over at the rate ceiling (R1's
   correction). A support centroid that varies within a sequence is not a declarable scalar. E1b, stratified
   by events-in-window decile, measures whether the influence centroid moves between the bottom and top decile.
3. **The sawtooth.** DAGr's `τ̂(t)` rises across the 50 ms inter-frame interval and resets. Honest caveat:
   this *is* still declarable — as a function rather than a scalar — so it strengthens the reporting contract
   rather than defeating it, and we say so rather than counting it as a third escape.

*Net position, stated in the abstract:* this is a **benchmark-integrity and reporting** contribution, which is
the ground R7 says ideas 08, 06 and 05 stand on more firmly than the ideas promising downstream task gains.
The strongest claim we make about deployment is R7's own: nobody stamps their detector's output at `t + τ̂`,
not because it is hard, but because the number has never been published.

### Death 5 (procedural) — Prior art we have not found

The device's ancestor is now identified and cited (LET-3D-AP). R5's independent sweep — including the full
CVPR 2026 event listing, 38 papers — found no second collision and found `all:"ground truth" AND
all:"interpolation" AND all:"event camera" AND all:"benchmark"` → 0, leaving E0 untouched.

*Mitigation:* the residual sweep (2026 late listings, MOT/flow metric literature) runs in week 1 as K7, and
E0 — independent of the decomposition's novelty — runs in parallel.

*Next idea if the decomposition is taken:* pivot to **"the ground truth is a model"** — measure, for every
major event benchmark, how much of a published score is explained by agreement with the label-generating
process rather than with the world, and release a per-dataset prior-agreement ceiling. E0 is already that
paper's core experiment, which is why it runs first.
