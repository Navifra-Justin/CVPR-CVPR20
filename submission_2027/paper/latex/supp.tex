\section{Recurrent support under the released streaming evaluation}
\label{sup:support}

Sec.~\ref{sup:state} shows that the carried state of the SSM release is inert. Under
its own streaming evaluation the recurrent history behind a detection is bounded by
the current chunk of \ssmEvalChunk{} windows. That bound is not constant across detections:
a frame at the first position of a chunk carries one window, and a frame at the last carries
\ssmEvalChunk{}. The reported Gen1 number averages over all \ssmEvalChunk{} positions, and
neither the metric, the protocol, nor the published table names the variable. It is recoverable from the release, yet absent from the protocol record.

The release derives its chunk starts
from the index files it ships. The first chunk begins at
\begin{center}\footnotesize\ttfamily
max(objframe\_idx\_2\_repr\_idx[0]\\ $-$ sequence\_length $+$ 1, 0)
\end{center}
and later chunks step by \texttt{sequence\_length}. The position of every labeled frame
therefore follows from those files alone. Reconstructing it over the \mapFrames{} labeled frames reproduces, position by
position, the positions the instrumented dumper records at run time.

Comparing the frames at positions $0$--$3$ with those at positions $16$--$20$ confounds
support with scene content, since these are different frames. The three RVT checkpoints
control for that. They see the identical frames in the identical order, but their ConvLSTM
state is carried across chunks. Position in the chunk is not a support variable for them,
and any difference they show is scene content. Differencing removes it. Sec.~\ref{sup:rvtbound}
measures that insensitivity on the same checkpoints instead of assuming it. Tab.~\ref{tab:chunk}
scores every labeled box, the population the released numbers use, and bootstraps over the
\outSeqsScanned{} sequences.

A permutation null tests whether the reconstructed correspondence itself can account for the effect.
Chunk position is
reshuffled among the frames of each sequence. That preserves the scene, the sequence
lengths, the label density and the sizes of both blocks, and destroys only the
correspondence between a frame and the history the protocol gave it. Over \chunkPermB{}
reshuffles the difference-in-differences is centered on zero with a standard deviation of about
\chunkPermSd{} points. The observed values, \chunkDidBase{} and \chunkDidSmall{} points, lie far outside it ($p=\chunkPermP$, the smallest value
\chunkPermB{} reshuffles can return), while every placebo stays within
\chunkPermPlaceboZ{} standard deviations. The effect disappears when the correspondence between a frame and
its reconstructed history is permuted.

A second instrument uses neither the ground truth nor the evaluator. Mean detection
confidence is a label-free statistic, and it is flat in chunk position when the available
support does not vary with that position. For S5-S it rises from
\chunkConfOne{} at 1 window to \chunkConfFull{} at 17, \chunkConfPct\,\%, while
RVT-\texttt{t} stays flat across the same positions. The shift therefore appears upstream
of the matching rule.

The comparison can also be made against the reported figure itself. Under the released
evaluation, the pooled S5-B and S5-S scores lie \chunkGapBase{} and \chunkGapSmall{} points below the scores of
their respective full-support frames.

\subsection{The same frame under both chunk positions}
\label{sup:paired}

Every estimate above compares different frames and therefore rests on a parallel-trends
assumption. Absent the support difference, frames that fall early and late in a chunk are
assumed to differ only by a model-independent offset. The placebos make that
assumption testable, but they do not remove it.

The release's chunk starts follow from the index files, so moving them is a one-line change
to the protocol and not to the model. Shifting every start \ssmPairedShift{} windows later
sends the frame the release gave position $p$ to position $(p-\ssmPairedShift)\bmod
\ssmEvalChunk{}$, which carries the starved block, positions $0$--$3$, onto $16$--$19$.
Those frames are then scored twice. The first arm gives 1--4 windows of recurrent
history, as the release scores them, and the second 17--20. Both use the same
weights, the same ground truth and the same evaluator. \ssmPairedN{} labeled frames satisfy both, and the
comparison is within model and within frame. Shifting \emph{earlier} does not work: the
release's own first start is already clamped at zero for most sequences, so most frames
would keep the position they had.

\subsection{The same frames under four boundary positions}
\label{sup:dose}

The comparison above moves the boundary by one amount, so it establishes that the starved
block is recoverable without fixing how much history the recovery needs. Because the
rotation $(p-S)\bmod\ssmEvalChunk{}$ is modular, the amount is a free parameter. At
$S\in\{15,10,5\}$ the starved block, positions $0$--$3$, lands on $6$--$9$, $11$--$14$ and
$16$--$19$, which is $+6$, $+11$ and $+16$ further windows of recurrent history. The three
amounts select the same \ssmDoseN{} labeled frames, so the rungs are one population under
three doses rather than three separate comparisons. The code asserts set
equality of the selected frames before recording the curve. $S=0$ re-runs the release's own
boundary placement through the identical pipeline and serves as the negative control.

Both checkpoints increase monotonically in the history gained, and the increments fall as
the history grows: on S5-B the first $+6$ windows recover \ssmDoseBaseSix{} of the

is graded in the quantity the intervention manipulates, which a frame-difficulty confound
aligned with chunk position would not produce. That confound is fixed once the frames
are fixed, and these rungs share their frames. And the response is closer to saturation at
$+16$ than at $+6$, so the deficit is concentrated in the first windows after a boundary.
The curve is measured at four amounts on one dataset and one architecture family, and the
saturation point is therefore not estimated beyond the ordering of these rungs.

\subsection{The same intervention on RVT, in two state regimes}
\label{sup:rvtbound}

Main Sec.~4 differences the observational chunk-position contrast against the
RVT checkpoints. That differencing is valid only if an RVT boundary is not a reset, which is
a property of the released implementation: \texttt{modules/detection.py} keeps the validation
state in \texttt{mode\_2\_rnn\_states} and clears it only on \texttt{is\_first\_sample}. The
property is measured here rather than taken from the source.

RVT's per-window calls are grouped onto the same \ssmEvalChunk{}-window grid the SSM release
uses, so a chunk position can be recorded for each frame and a boundary shift applied. With
no shift and the state carried, the grouping is inert by construction, and
a check on the stored artifacts confirms it. Every one of the
\rvtBoundDetsS{} RVT-\texttt{s} and \rvtBoundDetsB{} RVT-\texttt{b} detections is bit-identical to the
stored per-window dump. The ground truth matches, and the recorded positions equal the
release placement frame for frame. Without that identity a null below would be indistinguishable
from a driver that dropped the treatment. Two regimes are then compared on the \rvtBoundN{}
frames the shift carries from positions $0$--$3$ to $16$--$19$, under the same selection rule
and the same estimand as Tab.~\ref{tab:dose}. The first is \emph{carry}, the released
behavior. The second is \emph{reset},
with the recurrent state dropped at every chunk start, which places RVT in the condition the
SSM release is already in.

\subsection{Scoring at a fixed history}
\label{sup:fixedh}

Main Sec.~6 scores the five released Gen1 checkpoints with the recurrent history fixed to $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$ windows. This subsection gives the construction, the checks that were run on it, and the ordering table behind main Tab.~3.

\paragraph{Construction.}
For every labeled frame at window index $r$ and every $H$, the checkpoint is run on windows $r-H+1,\dots,r$ only, from a fresh recurrent state, and the detections of window $r$ are kept. An RVT checkpoint is called once per window with its state carried inside the $H$-window run and started empty at the first of them, as in the release's own per-window call. An SSM-ViT checkpoint receives the $H$ windows as one chunk of length $H$ with a fresh state and the detection head applied at the last position, as in the release's chunked call. Nothing before window $r-H+1$ reaches the model. The nominal history is $H$, and a frame with fewer than $H$ earlier windows receives $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=\min(H,r+1)$. Ground truth, frame admission and label linking are those of the released-protocol dumps, and the confidence floor and NMS overlap are the ones of Sec.~\ref{sup:ordering}. All five checkpoints are scored on the \fhNCommon{} common frames, those with $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ at every $H$, out of \fhNAll{} labeled frames, in \fhNSeq{} sequences. Intervals are 95\,\% percentile intervals over \fhB{} bootstrap draws that resample sequences, with one draw shared by every checkpoint and column, so every difference below is paired.

\paragraph{Checks.}
Each run records which windows the loader returned for each (frame, $H$) cell, independently of the formula above. For all five checkpoints the number of distinct windows read equals $H_{\mathrm{forced}}$ in every cell, and the first window read is $r-H_{\mathrm{forced}}+1$ in every cell, so no earlier window reached the model. The weight hash of each checkpoint is unchanged by the run, one ground-truth array serves every $H$, and the evaluated frame set is the same at every $H$. An oldest-window probe replaces the oldest admitted window and records whether the output changes. It changed the output in \fhProbeHit{} of \fhProbeN{} probed cells for RVT-\texttt{s}, RVT-\texttt{b}, S5-S and S5-B, and in \fhProbeHitT{} of \fhProbeN{} for RVT-\texttt{t}. The mAP of the table is computed by a vectorized evaluator that reproduces the reference scorer up to the order of equal float32 confidences. Over all models and columns the largest difference between the two is $\fhFastDiff\times10^{-6}$ points.

S5-B is first at every fixed $H$, and RVT-\texttt{b} minus S5-B shrinks from \fhgap{Rb}{Sb}{HOne} at $H=1$ to \fhgap{Rb}{Sb}{HTwentyone} at $H=21$ (main Tab.~4). The pooled score of each RVT checkpoint is above all four of its fixed-$H$ scores, whereas that of each S5 checkpoint lies between its $H=1$ and $H=21$ scores. The reset-regime measurement of Sec.~\ref{sup:rvtbound} (\rvtBoundResetS{} and \rvtBoundResetB{} points) shows that the RVT checkpoints respond to the history length under that state.

\section{The carried state of the SSM release}
\label{sup:state}


Sec.~\ref{sec:influence} measures five released checkpoints of two architectures. The second is
SSM-ViT (Zubic et al., CVPR 2024), a fork of the RVT repository in which each stage's
ConvLSTM is
replaced by an S5 layer. It distributes RVT's own preprocessed Gen1 and builds its window
boundaries with the same statement of the same script, so the window convention of
Sec.~\ref{sec:window} holds there unchanged.

Its recurrent state does not. Let $s$ denote the carried state entering the first
step of a chunk. That release injects it as
$\bar{\Lambda}_{0}\leftarrow\bar{\Lambda}_{0}\odot s$ before an associative scan whose
binary operator is $(a_{i},b_{i})\circ(a_{j},b_{j})=(a_{j}a_{i},\,a_{j}b_{i}+b_{j})$. The
scan's outputs are the $b$ components, and $b_{p}$ is built from $a_{1},\dots,a_{p}$ and
$b_{0},\dots,b_{p}$, $\bar{\Lambda}_{0}$ enters only the $a$ component of the first element
and therefore no output. The carried state is inert.

We verify this code-level property empirically. Driving each model the way its own release
drives it and zeroing the state entering a chunk of \ssmChunkMeas{} windows, the relative
$L_{2}$ change in the emitted detection tensor is \ssmStateEffect{} at every position for
SSM-ViT. For RVT-\texttt{t} it is \rvtStateEffectOne{} at position one, falling to
\rvtStateEffect{} at the last position. A model that ignored its history altogether would return zero to
both arms, so the control ablates the window one position earlier inside the same chunk.
SSM-ViT answers \ssmInchunkOne{} there, falling to \ssmInchunkLast{}. It is recurrent
within a chunk, and only across chunks is the state lost.

\section{Timing differences against the sampling uncertainty of the score}
\label{sup:timing}

Sec.~\ref{sec:extrapolation} reports that displacing the ground truth across the measured interval moves
mAP by \mapCost{} points and reorders no checkpoint. On a fixed split both are
deterministic functions of the stored detections, so neither is interpretable without the
uncertainty that attaches to reading them as statements about the sequence population the
split samples. Frames inside a sequence are
dependent, so the resampling unit is the sequence. A cluster bootstrap over the
\outSeqsScanned{} validation sequences was run for all \rankCkpts{} checkpoints at each
displacement, resampling sequences with replacement and rescoring from the stored
detections.

Two standard errors must be distinguished. A claim made \emph{within} one paper compares
that paper's own score at two displacements on one set of detections. The relevant
quantity is the paired $\mathrm{SE}(\Delta\mathrm{mAP})$, which is \seDmapLo--\seDmapHi{}
points across the five checkpoints. A claim that compares \emph{across} papers compares two
absolute numbers, and the relevant quantity is $\mathrm{SE}(\mathrm{mAP})$, which is
\seMapLo--\seMapHi{} points on the velocity-evaluable subset, fifty times larger.

Two quantities that are easy to conflate are therefore reported separately, because they
are referred to different baselines. The first is the displacement \emph{from the maximum
of the sweep} at which the loss reaches two standard errors: \resPairedLo--\resPairedHi\,ms
paired and \resAbsLo--\resAbsHi\,ms in the single-score sense. The second is the effect of
the measured interval itself, referred to zero displacement, which is
\effTauLo--\effTauHi{} points across the \rankCkpts{} checkpoints against a paired
two-standard-error band of \twoSeDmapLo--\twoSeDmapHi{} points. The second clears its band
for \effTauResolved{} of \rankCkpts{} checkpoints. The measured interval therefore sits at
the paired sampling-resolution floor under sequence resampling. The identifiability conclusion
rests on where the evidence lies, not on that comparison. The
\mapCost{}-point figure measures the sensitivity of the pooled benchmark to the interval,
which follows from the benchmark's speed composition (Tab.~\ref{tab:strata}) and places no bound on
the interval itself.

The same computation gives the timing difference a \emph{leaderboard} could resolve. Let
$D^{\star}$ be the smallest common displacement at which an adjacent pair in the
validation-split ordering exchanges places. Over all velocity-evaluable boxes $D^{\star}=\dstarAll\,$ms,
\dstarAllTau$\times$ the measured interval, and in the \mapCutMid--\mapCutHi\,px/s stratum
$D^{\star}=\dstarMid\,$ms, or \dstarMidTau$\times$. A benchmark whose ordering only responds
to timing displacements of this size cannot be used to certify that a timing convention is
immaterial.

That ordering is itself unstable. The reference ordering here is the one the \rankCkpts{}
checkpoints take on this validation split at the nominal label time. It is not the ordering of
the published Gen1 tables, which are reported on the test split
(Sec.~\ref{sup:port}). Resampling the same \outSeqsScanned{} sequences and re-ranking
within each replicate, that validation-split ordering is reproduced in \rankHoldMov\,\% of
replicates on the velocity-evaluable subset and \rankHoldBox\,\% over every labeled box, the population
the released evaluation scores. Its tightest
adjacent pair is separated by \rankGapMin{} points against a paired standard error of
\rankGapMinSE{} and exchanges places in a fraction \rankFlipMax{} of replicates. Two
readings are consistent with the same evidence: that the correction changes no ranking on
this split, and that the ranking is unstable under sequence resampling. The measured
sampling uncertainty separates them in favor of the second.

\paragraph{Normalized image speed and timing resolution.} The natural reading of
Tab.~\ref{tab:strata} is that a benchmark of faster objects would resolve the interval. What an
IoU-thresholded metric registers is displacement relative to box size, so the scale-free
quantity is $r = |v| / \sqrt{wh}$, box-widths of motion per second, which is invariant to
sensor resolution and therefore comparable across benchmarks. A single fixed summary of $r$,
such as its median, does not explain the observed resolution across strata. Scaling $\delta$ by
each stratum's median $r$ increases the dispersion of the $5\,\%$-loss timescale from
\collapseCVraw{} to \collapseCVmed{}, and no fixed quantile improves on the unscaled result.

mAP thresholds each box instead of averaging over them. Under the single-threshold
caricature, in which a box loses its match once displaced past $d^{\star}$ box-widths and is
untouched before, the fraction of boxes lost at displacement $\delta$ is
$P(r > d^{\star}/\delta)$. The displacement at which a loss level $p$ is reached is
$D(p) = d^{\star} / Q_{1-p}(r)$. The scale-setter is the quantile \emph{matched to the loss
level being read off}, not the median or any fixed quantile. Scaled that way the strata
do collapse, to a coefficient of variation of \collapseCV{} at the $5\,\%$ level over three
strata and \rankCkpts{} checkpoints, with $d^{\star} = \collapseD \pm \collapseDsd$
box-widths. That constant also follows from the matching geometry. Displacing a square box by a
fraction $d$ of its width gives $\mathrm{IoU} = (1-d)/(1+d)$, so $d^{\star}$ is the
displacement that breaks a match at $\mathrm{IoU} = \collapseIoU$. That threshold lies inside the
$0.50$:$0.05$:$0.95$ range this mAP averages over and near its top, where the first few
percent of loss arise. The model degrades as the caricature should, to \collapseCVten{}
at $10\,\%$ loss and \collapseCVqtr{} at $25\,\%$. The $\geq\mapCutHi\,$px/s stratum is the
outlier at every loss level, with $d^{\star}$ approximately $1.8\times$ larger than the common
value. Its lower mAP$(0)$ may leave less high-IoU headroom for displacement, but no downstream
claim depends on this interpretation.

Under this caricature, timing resolution is governed by the upper tail of $r$. Stratification
increases the relevant quantile but also increases the standard error of $\Delta$mAP as
$1/\sqrt{n}$. The 25--50 px/s and $\geq 50$ px/s strata yield net resolution factors of
0.42$\times$ and 0.27$\times$, respectively. Neither resolves the interval better than the
full velocity-evaluable population.

On the DSEC-Det labels, associated by the \texttt{track\_id} the release ships
, not by IoU linking, $r$ has median \rDsecTrain\,s$^{-1}$ against Gen1's
\rGenAll\,s$^{-1}$. On the quantity that actually governs resolution the gap is smaller:
$Q_{95}(r)=5.59$, compared with 0.70 on Gen1, a 7.97$\times$ difference. This
label-composition contrast shows that DSEC-Det has a substantially heavier upper tail of
normalized image speed than Gen1. It supports the narrower conclusion that Gen1's timing
insensitivity should not be generalized to event-based detection benchmarks with different
motion and box-size distributions.

\section{Predictor-side temporal descriptors}
\label{sec:predictor}

\subsection{Identifiability and the timing estimands}

Main Sec.~5 defines the term protocol-identifiable for quantities the protocol record determines. This subsection fixes the notation for the two predictor-side
descriptors.

Let a predictor $P$ emit a state at query time $t$. A signed effective-weight
model uses bin centers $c_k$ and weights $a_k$ with $\sum_k a_k=1$, giving the
effective timestamp offset $\tau_P=\sum_k a_kc_k$ under constant velocity. The
empirical ablation profile instead assigns non-negative sensitivities $s_k\geq0$
to bins and has centroid $c_s=\sum_k s_kc_k/\sum_k s_k$. These are distinct
quantities: zero-ablation sensitivity depends on both the predictor and the
input, and is not in general a decomposition of temporal weights. Sections~\ref{sec:window}--\ref{sec:influence} measure this
operational input-sensitivity profile over the newest window. Section~\ref{sec:outputtime}
estimates the regression coefficient $\tau$ under Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg}. Interpreting $-\tau$ as an
effective state-time offset requires the absence of residual velocity-proportional
spatial bias.
Section~\ref{sec:extrapolation} compares $-c_s$ with the regression coefficient
$\tau$, equivalently $c_s$ with the regression-implied timestamp $\tau_P=-\tau$.

\subsection{The input window and its indexing instant}
\label{sec:window}

Two released files fix the input side. The dataset configuration
\texttt{config/dataset/gen1.yaml} of the detector's own
repository~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} names the representation
\texttt{stacked\_histogram\_dt=50\_nbins=10}, a window of \rvtWindow\,ms divided
into \rvtBins{} bins of \rvtBinWidth\,ms. Line \rvtSourceLine{} of
\texttt{scripts/genx/preprocess\_dataset.py} constructs the array
\texttt{ev\_repr\_timestamps\_us\_end} by counting backwards from a label
timestamp in steps of the window duration, and extends it between consecutive
label timestamps. Each representation is therefore indexed by the end of its
window, and those ends are placed on label times. A window attached to a label at
time $t$ holds the events of $[t-\rvtWindow\,\mathrm{ms},\,t]$, whose uniform
weighting centroid falls \rvtCentroidMag\,ms before the instant $t$ at which the
label asserts a state. We read both statements from the released source, and
the \rvtCentroidMag\,ms figure is a reference point instead of a property of
the trained network.

\subsection{The measured influence profile}
\label{sec:influence}

The newest-window ablation-sensitivity centroid is computed from the measured
bin-ablation sensitivities of a released checkpoint. We take \rvtInfluenceSamples{} samples of the released Gen1
validation split with the released RVT-\texttt{t} checkpoint, warm the recurrent
state for \rvtWarmSteps{} steps, and ablate each bin in turn. For sample $i$, let
$Y_i$ denote the emitted detection tensor from the unablated input and
$Y_i^{(-k)}$ the tensor obtained after zeroing temporal bin $k$ while keeping the
incoming recurrent state identical. We define the ablation sensitivity, or influence, of bin $k$ as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_k&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\frac{\left\|Y_i^{(-k)}-Y_i\right\|_2}{\left\|Y_i\right\|_2},\quad N=480,\\
c_s&=\frac{\sum_k s_k c_k}{\sum_k s_k},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $c_k$ is the center time of bin $k$ relative to the label instant. The
ablated forward pass receives the same incoming recurrent state as the
corresponding reference pass, and its returned state is discarded. The profile is
a property of the current window and not of a perturbed history.

The profile is balanced between the two halves of the window (Fig.~\ref{fig:predictor}a)
and yields an ablation-sensitivity centroid of \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms, compared
with \rvtCentroidUnif\,ms for uniform weighting. The released configuration
therefore places the measured centroid near the window reference without implying
uniform network sensitivity.
Across zero, mean, cross-sample replacement, and gradient instruments, the centroid spans
\occMean{} to \occGrad{} ms (Sec.~\ref{sup:influence}).
The reported centroid applies to the deterministic 12-sequence subset described in Sec.~\ref{sup:influence}.
This is the current window's window-ablation sensitivity profile with the recurrent
state held fixed, not a decomposition of the full temporal support.

\paragraph{Recurrent support beyond the newest window.}
Over a 1000\,ms horizon, the newest window accounts for 22.7\% of the summed
window-ablation sensitivity, while the remaining 77.3\% lies in preceding windows. The
horizon centroid shifts from
\histCentroidTen\,ms at \histHorizonA\,ms to \histCentroidTwenty\,ms at \histHorizonB\,ms,
so the measured range does not determine a converged full-history centroid. Sec.~\ref{sup:tail}
characterizes the tail and its fill controls.

\paragraph{Across the released checkpoints and a second architecture.}
RVT releases three Gen1 checkpoints differing only in embedding width, attention
head dimension and FPN depth. SSM-ViT~\cite{zubic2024ssm} releases two more in
which each stage's ConvLSTM is replaced by an S5 state space layer, at \ssmParamsS{}
and \ssmParamsB{} M parameters against RVT's \capParamsS{} and \capParamsB{}.
It distributes RVT's own preprocessed Gen1 and inherits its window construction. On the same sequences, and
with each driven as its own release specifies, all \archCkpts{} checkpoints put the
newest window's ablation-sensitivity centroid between \archHi{} and \archLo\,ms. None sits further than
\archUnifMax\,ms, or \archUnifPct\,\% of one bin, from the uniform-weight value.

The support within the newest window
is not fixed across capacities. Over \capSamples{} samples of the
three RVT capacities, the newest window's share of a \capHorizon\,ms horizon
falls from \capShareT\,\% to \capShareB\,\%, and the sensitivity centroid moves
from \capSuppT{} to \capSuppB\,ms.

\subsection{Regression-based temporal coefficient}
\label{sec:outputtime}

The influence profile is a statement about the network's input. Even a predictor
with uniform local sensitivity can emit a state describing a later instant,
and that quantity is measured from the same checkpoint's own detections.

A detector reporting the state an object occupied $\tau$ earlier incurs the
physical displacement $-\tau v$, where $v$ is the latent true velocity. We
observe only the finite-differenced label velocity $\tilde v=v+\eta$ and
therefore fit Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} using $\tilde v$. Thus
$\tau=-\tau_{P}$ on the axis of Sec.~\ref{sec:predictor}. With $\hat{c}$ the
predicted center, $c^{*}$ the label center, $A$ the area of the ground-truth
box, $\hat{u}=\tilde v/\lVert \tilde v\rVert$ and $\mathrm{R}$ a quarter turn,
$b$ is a two-dimensional intercept. That intercept is fitted globally or as
per-sequence intercepts in the reported specification.

\begin{equation}
\hat{c}-c^{*}=b-\tau\,\tilde v+\rho\,\mathrm{R}\tilde v+\gamma\sqrt{A}\,\hat{u}+\varepsilon
\label{eq:outreg}
\end{equation}

\noindent It is fitted by stacking both components of every match into one least
squares problem, with standard errors clustered by sequence. It is the temporal
calibration of~\cite{qin2018temporal} carried to a box center, taken as a slope
, not as the per-object ratio $-\langle
\hat{c}-c^{*},\hat{u}\rangle/\lVert \tilde v\rVert$, whose variance diverges as its
denominator approaches zero. For comparison, the per-object ratio evaluated on
the same rows has a mean of
$\ratioMean\,\pm\,\ratioMeanSE$\,ms, on the hypothesis evaluated in
Sec.~\ref{sec:extrapolation}, with an interquartile range of \ratioIQR\,ms.
Written this way, Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} fits the two-dimensional residual directly with $\tilde v$ as a
regressor. Unlike the per-object ratio, it does not first project the residual onto $\hat u$
and divide by $\|\tilde v\|$.
The reported full specification adds four coordinate/size regressors and
per-sequence intercepts to the terms displayed in Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg}.
The coefficient is a temporal offset only if no velocity-proportional spatial
bias remains after these controls. Such a bias is indistinguishable from $\tau$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg}.

$\mathrm{R}\tilde v$ is the label velocity rotated a quarter turn. Because a pure
temporal offset acts along $\tilde v$, not $\mathrm{R}\tilde v$, $\rho$ serves
as a negative-control coefficient. A nonzero value implies residual spatial
misspecification.
$\sqrt{A}\,\hat{u}$ absorbs a displacement along travel proportional to box side,
which is needed because that residual grows with box size and box size correlates
with label speed at \outSpeedSizeCorr{} on this split.

\paragraph{The finite difference used for the velocity.}
$\tilde v$ is finite differenced from labels. Label noise enters forward and backward
velocity differences with opposite shared-noise signs. The centered difference excludes the
current label center and removes this direct same-frame term. Secs.~\ref{sup:denominator} and \ref{sup:signflip} derive
and calibrate this effect, so the reported regression uses the centered estimator.

\subsection{Relating input-sensitivity timing to the regression-based temporal coefficient}
\label{sec:extrapolation}

The two descriptors are measured under different recurrent-state conditions. The centroid uses an eight-step warm-up with the incoming state held fixed during ablation; the regression uses detections from the validation stream with the released state carried across frames. They are distinct operational summaries of the same nominal timestamp. On the sign convention of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} the newest-window centroid corresponds to $\tau=+\rvtCentroidMagMeas$\,ms, and the reported coefficient is $\ladderFullTau\pm\ladderFullTauSE$\,ms, so the point estimates sit \outInterval\,ms apart. The spread of $\tau$ across specifications (Sec.~\ref{sup:sweeps}) is larger than the nominal standard error and is read alongside it.

The detector is trained against the ground truth at the label time on a window
ending there, so a measured offset near zero is consistent with the training
objective indexed at the label time. The objective
does not enforce the
interval, and the instant the output describes and the centroid of the evidence
used to produce it are different quantities that one benchmark timestamp stands
for. At the ninetieth percentile of label speed on this split, \outSpeedNinety\,px/s, the
\rvtCentroidMagMeas\,ms is \outPxNinety\,px of displacement, comparable to the
annotation dispersion of the labels themselves (\labelSigmaGenAnn\,px), and at the
ninety-ninth it is \outPxNinetynine\,px. That ablation-sensitivity centroid and
the regression-based temporal coefficient are measured from released artifacts alone.

This experiment is not a test of whether temporal support matters in general. It asks whether
Gen1's particular speed composition makes the measured RVT interval visible to its current mAP.
We translate each ground-truth box
center by $\delta\tilde v$, keeping its size and class fixed, a
first-order surrogate for temporal displacement under local constant velocity.
The released predictions remain unchanged, since a detection
has no velocity of its own. Over \mapFrames{} frames, \mapGTvelPct\,\% of the \mapGT{}

(Fig.~\ref{fig:predictor}b). Along the axis of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} that is
$\tau=\mapArgmaxTau$\,ms, and mAP varies by at most \mapCost{} points over the

value.

\mapCostPct\,\% relative to \mapZero{}). Average precision on the velocity-evaluable

\rankGapMinSE{}. A common displacement sweep reveals one localized

The near invariance therefore characterizes Gen1's metric resolution, not the absence of a
temporal discrepancy.

\section{The recurrent influence tail}
\label{sup:tail}

Sec.~\ref{sec:influence} reports that the window-ablation-sensitivity centroid tracks the horizon
instead of converging. Two horizons alone cannot distinguish a slowly converging support
from a divergent one, so the tail is characterized directly.

Let $I(L)$ be the summed sensitivity at lag $L$ windows. A weighted least-squares fit of
$\log I$ on $\log L$ over lags $1$--$9$ gives $I(L)\propto L^{-a}$ with $a=\aFitShort$, and
the convergent alternative $I(L)\propto e^{-L/\lambda}$ was fitted on the same lags. Neither
was fitted on lags $10$--$19$, which are held out. On the held-out lags, the power law gives
an RMS standardized residual of
\tailRmsPower{}, compared with \tailRmsExp{} for the exponential, a $\tailRmsRatio\times$
difference. Fitting all lags $1$--$19$ gives $a=\tailExp\pm\tailExpSE$, with a
bootstrap interval of [\tailExpLo,\,\tailExpHi] that no replicate carried to $a\geq1$. The
standard error and that interval come from a parametric resample of the per-lag means and so
describe the precision of the fit, not independent observations: all lags are computed from
the same \histSamples{} samples.

For a support whose first moment has converged, the centroid ratio approaches $1$ as the horizon
increases, and a
particular finite doubling need not give exactly $1$. The measured ratio is \tailRatio{}
$[\tailRatioLo,\tailRatioHi]$, an interval excluding $1$, and follows from
two measured centroids without a fit. Over the horizons measured here the centroid is
still moving with the horizon instead of settling. Conditionally, if the observed
$I(L)\propto L^{-a}$ with $a<1$ persisted asymptotically, then both $\sum_L I(L)$ and
$\sum_L L\,I(L)$ would diverge. The centroid over a horizon of $H$ windows of width $w$
would be $-wH(1-a)/(2-a)$, linear in $H$, and the first moment would then not exist. Either way
\tailOutside\,\% of the summed window-ablation sensitivity already lies outside the current window, which is
what the identifiability claim rests on.

A fill artifact could in principle produce persistent long-lag sensitivity, so the control
defined in Sec.~8 applies here. The zero and swap fills decay by factors of 10.3 and 2.2 over
the same lags, while substantial long-lag sensitivity remains detectable under both fills.

\section{The velocity regressor}
\label{sup:denominator}

The regression of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} uses the finite-differenced label velocity
$\tilde v=v+\eta$, where $v$ is latent true velocity and $\eta$ is label noise.
Three error mechanisms enter, and they behave differently. The shared-noise term
determines the estimator choice.

\paragraph{Classical dilution.} Let the label center carry noise of standard
deviation $\sigma_{c}$ per frame and let the speed be formed by a central
difference over $\pm\Delta$. The differenced speed then carries noise with variance

\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\eta}^{2}=\frac{2\sigma_{c}^{2}}{(\Delta_f+\Delta_b)^{2}},
\label{eq:dilution}
\end{equation}

and $\tau$ is attenuated toward zero by
$\lambda_{\mathrm{att}}=\mathrm{Var}(v)/(\mathrm{Var}(v)+\sigma_{\eta}^{2})$.
The noise is the label noise of the dataset the fit runs on, so it is measured
there. Sec.~\ref{sup:labelnoise} solves for $\sigma_{c}=\labelSigmaGen$\,px on
Gen1 itself, and Eq.~\ref{eq:dilution} is evaluated row by row instead of assuming a fixed cadence. On the
reported rows $\mathrm{Var}(\eta)$ is \eivVarNoise{} against a
$\mathrm{Var}(\tilde v)$ of \eivVarObs{} in px$^{2}$/s$^{2}$, so
$\lambda_{\mathrm{att}}=\eivLambda{}$. Because the reported Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} also includes geometric
controls and per-sequence effects, this marginal calculation is not the exact attenuation
factor for the final multivariable coefficient. We use it only as a scale diagnostic showing
that label-velocity noise is small relative to marginal velocity variation. Under the synthetic
setup of Sec.~10, the centered estimator recovers \synRecover{} of the injected lag against the
predicted \synLambda{}.

\paragraph{Smoothing bias.} On the uniform grid used for the label-noise
calibration, a central difference returns the mean velocity over $2\Delta$
instead of the instantaneous velocity. Expanding the trajectory about the
query instant,

\begin{equation}
\frac{x(t+\Delta)-x(t-\Delta)}{2\Delta}
=\dot{x}(t)+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{6}\dddot{x}(t)+O(\Delta^{4}),
\end{equation}

so the regressor carries a systematic term of order $\Delta^{2}\dddot{x}/6$. That
is a bias in the regressor, not noise in it, and no errors-in-variables
correction removes it. It is made small by making $\Delta$ small, which
Eq.~\ref{eq:dilution} says makes the dilution worse.
The reported regression rows use the local secant
$[x(t+\Delta_f)-x(t-\Delta_b)]/(\Delta_f+\Delta_b)$. Their median
$|\Delta_f-\Delta_b|$ is zero, so the first-order acceleration term is absent
in the typical row. The general expansion includes
$(\Delta_f-\Delta_b)\ddot{x}/2$ and is covered by the stated local-secant
interpretation.

\paragraph{The shared-noise term.} The first two mechanisms are properties of the
regressor alone. The third couples the regressor to the response. A forward
difference $(c^{*}_{k+1}-c^{*}_{k})/\Delta$ carries the label noise $\delta_{k}$ of
the current center with weight $-1/\Delta$, and the residual
$\hat{c}-c^{*}_{k}$ carries the same $\delta_{k}$ with weight $-1$. The two therefore covary.

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\hat{c}-c^{*}_{k},\,v_{f}\right)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\delta)}{\Delta}
\label{eq:shared}
\end{equation}

\noindent That holds even at $\tau=0$, and the induced slope is
$\operatorname{Var}(\delta)/\Delta\operatorname{Var}(v)$. A backward difference
carries $\delta_{k}$ with the opposite sign. The centered difference omits
$c^{*}_{k}$ and removes the direct same-frame shared-noise term. Residual bias
from temporally correlated annotation errors is not identified by this
construction, which is why the forward and backward fits are reported beside it
as a control and calibrated in Sec.~\ref{sup:signflip}.

\paragraph{Instrumental-variable alternative.} An instrumented
estimate would replace the current speed by a velocity finite differenced from a
strictly disjoint earlier label pair, on the argument that disjointness implies
independent errors. DSEC provides a counterexample to assuming that disjoint
label errors are generally independent. The third-difference kernel $(1,-3,3,-1)$ forces
$\rho(1)=\labelAcfTheory$ for white noise, and the same code returns
$\labelAcfWhite$ on white noise and $\labelAcfMeasured$ on the DSEC-Det labels.
Pooled-ratio bias, heteroscedastic segments and heavy tails each returned the
white-noise value through the identical pooling code, so the departure is a
property of the labels. That measurement does not identify what the correlation
structure is, beyond excluding a first-order autoregressive form. The paper does
not report an instrumental estimate.

\paragraph{The construction term.} A benchmark whose high-rate labels are
constructed between anchors adds a term to $e_{\parallel}$, where
$e_{\parallel}=\langle\hat c-c^{*},\hat u\rangle$, that has nothing to do
with the predictor. The chord of a curved image trajectory runs ahead of the
truth throughout the interval and meets it at the anchors, which produces a
signed, frame-locked, resetting residual for a predictor with no lag. Sec.~\ref{sec:labelclock} shows that exact adjacent-frame linear interpolation is inconsistent
with the observed DSEC-Det label trajectories as the sole construction mechanism,
and the Gen1 measurements of Sec.~\ref{sec:outputtime} are taken at label times.

\section{The influence profile}
\label{sup:influence}

Sec.~\ref{sec:influence} measures the window-ablation sensitivity profile of the released
RVT-\texttt{t} checkpoint. The stored input is a stacked histogram of
\rvtBins{} bins of \rvtBinWidth\,ms covering the \rvtWindow\,ms preceding the
query time. For each sample the recurrent state is advanced over
\rvtWarmSteps{} preceding steps so that the network is not read out of a zero
state. The reference output is recorded, and then each bin in turn is zeroed and
the output recorded again. A bin's influence is the magnitude of the change its
ablation produces in the emitted detection tensor, relative to its norm. That
magnitude is averaged over
\rvtInfluenceSamples{} samples drawn from the first twelve lexicographically
ordered released Gen1 validation sequences, using the first forty post-warm-up
samples from each sequence. This deterministic rule gives 480 samples. The
measured ablation-sensitivity centroid is the weighted average
$\sum_{k}s_{k}c_{k}/\sum_{k}s_{k}$ over the ten bin centers $c_{k}$, where
$s_k$ denotes the measured non-negative bin-ablation sensitivity. The ablated pass starts from the recurrent
state \emph{entering} the step, not from the state the reference pass returned. The two
passes therefore share one history and differ only in the ablated bin. If the
returned state is used instead, the passes are one step out of phase and the centroid
moves by \rvtCentroidDiff\,ms.

For the recurrent-history measurement, the same deterministic sampling rule uses
\histSamples{} samples, the first forty-eight post-warm-up samples from each of the
same twelve validation sequences. The current window and the preceding nine windows
form the \histHorizonA\,ms horizon, and the current window and preceding nineteen windows
form the \histHorizonB\,ms horizon. For each lag, the corresponding whole input window
is zeroed while the saved reference history before that lag is restored, and the state
is propagated through the intervening windows to the target step. The relative $L_{2}$
change in the target detection tensor is averaged over samples to give a non-negative
window sensitivity $q_{\ell}$. The horizon centroid is
\[
c_{q,H}=\frac{\sum_{\ell=0}^{L_H-1}q_{\ell}[-(25+50\ell)\,\mathrm{ms}]}
{\sum_{\ell=0}^{L_H-1}q_{\ell}},
\]
where $L_H$ is the number of windows in horizon $H$, and the newest-window share is
$q_{0}/\sum_{\ell}q_{\ell}$. The same saved reference
history is used for the reference and ablated passes at each lag.

Two fills define $q_{\ell}$, and the manuscript reports both. Under the \emph{zero fill}
the selected window is set to zero. Under the \emph{swap fill} it is replaced by the window
at the same within-chunk index drawn from an unrelated sample in a different validation
sequence. That fill preserves a real window's bin statistics and dynamic range while removing
that window's own content. Everything else is held identical across the two: the same lag,
the same restored reference history, the same propagation through the intervening windows,
and the same relative $L_{2}$ read-out. The two are interventions rather than ground truth
and they do not agree on scale, which is why both are reported. From lag $0$ to lag $19$ the
zero fill decays by a factor of \histDecayZero{} and the swap fill by \histDecaySwap{}. The
ratio of swap to zero sensitivity rises from \histSwapRatioLo{} at lag $0$ to
\histSwapRatioHi{} at lag $19$. Under the swap fill a window a second earlier still carries
\histTailSwapPct\,\% of the newest window's sensitivity. Substantial long-lag sensitivity
therefore persists under the on-manifold intervention, although its absolute magnitude remains
instrument-dependent.

Two properties of that measurement bound it. Ablation reports the sensitivity of
the output to a bin. The ablation profile is an operational input-sensitivity
measure and is not, in general, a decomposition of temporal weights. For a
nonlinear predictor it is a local instead of a global description. A single-bin ablation also leaves the remaining bins in place, so the measurement is a partial
derivative of the output, not a decomposition of it.

Because the profile is defined by an intervention, the centroid is reported under four
instruments instead of one. Zeroing a bin gives \occZero\,ms, setting it to its dataset
mean \occMean\,ms, and replacing it with the same bin of an unrelated sample \occSwap\,ms.
The gradient of the output norm with respect to the bin, which ablates nothing at all, gives
\occGrad\,ms. The four span \occSpread\,ms. Two of them remove content, one substitutes
foreign content and one perturbs nothing, so their agreement is not a property shared by
construction. The property the manuscript uses the centroid for, that it sits tens of milliseconds
before the label instant and well away from the fitted temporal coefficient, holds under
all four.

The sampling rule is deterministic, not random, so the sequences are the resampling
unit for its uncertainty. Resampling the \occSeqs{} sequences with replacement gives a
\bootCI\,\% interval of [\occSeqCiLo,\,\occSeqCiHi]\,ms for the zero-fill centroid, a standard
error of \occSeqSE\,ms, and no interval wider than \occSeqWidest\,ms under any of the four
instruments. Dropping any one sequence moves the estimate by at most \occSeqLoo\,ms, and the
individual sequence centroids run from \occPerSeqLo{} to \occPerSeqHi\,ms. The choice of
instrument therefore moves this centroid by \occSpread\,ms while the choice of sequences moves
it by well under one. The instrument spread is the binding uncertainty here, and the sampling error is
negligible beside it. Sequences outside the twelve are a separate
question, answered only by measuring them.

Masking the leading bins of the stored input is not a route to the same quantity
at a shorter support. For a fixed network of influence weights $a_{k}$ on bin
centers $c_{k}$, let a constant-velocity trajectory be
$p(t)=p_{0}+vt$. The conditional expectation after masking the leading $j$ bins
is $A_{j}p_{0}+C_{j}v$ with $A_{j}=\sum_{S}a_{k}$ and $C_{j}=\sum_{S}a_{k}c_{k}$
over the retained set $S$. Reading a time off that requires the normalization
$C_{j}/A_{j}$, which the network need not perform, and the result equals the
retained-mass centroid only for non-negative weights.

\section{The label-center noise of Gen1}
\label{sup:labelnoise}

The errors-in-variables treatment of Sec.~7 and the calibration of the velocity
estimator both need the dispersion of the Gen1 label center about a smooth
trajectory. Label noise depends on the annotation effort and the sensor, so it is solved for on Gen1 itself.

For a track center series $x_i=f(t_i)+e_i$ on a uniform grid, the $k$-th
difference has $\operatorname{Var}(D_k)=\binom{2k}{k}\sigma^{2}$ plus a motion
term of order $k$, so $\operatorname{Var}(D_k)/\binom{2k}{k}$ falls toward
$\sigma^{2}$ from above. Halving the sampling rate multiplies the motion term by
$4^{k}$ and leaves $\sigma^{2}$ untouched, giving a second equation in the same
two unknowns, $\sigma^{2}=(4^{k}V_{\text{full}}-V_{\text{half}})/(4^{k}-1)$.
Tracks are rebuilt by the same-class overlap linking the output-time
measurement uses. That gives \labelTracksGen{} tracks of at least twelve labels on
uniform annotation grids with cadences of 250, 500, or 1000\,ms, corresponding
to 4, 2, or 1\,Hz. The reported regression rows instead use the two adjacent
label neighbors with variable intervals. Their row-wise noise variance is
$2\sigma_c^2/(\Delta_f+\Delta_b)^2$, instead of a fixed-cadence approximation.
Variances are trimmed at \ladderTrimPct\,\%, because all four Gen1 box
coordinates are stored as integers and a median absolute deviation on
lattice-valued data locks onto discrete values.

The two-rate elimination agrees with the full-rate ladder to four decimals from
$k=5$ onward, which is the check that the motion term is negligible rather than
assumed away. The ladder extrapolates to $\sigma=\labelSigmaGen$\,px, of which
\labelSigmaGenQuant\,px is the quantization of a \sigmaClattice\,px center lattice, leaving
\labelSigmaGenAnn\,px of annotation dispersion.

\section{The sign-flip calibration}
\label{sup:signflip}

Equation~\ref{eq:shared} predicts an artifact whose size is set by the label noise
and the observed velocity variance, so it is calibrated before the estimator is
used. \synTracks{} synthetic tracks of fourteen labels on the Gen1 grid are
generated with quadratic motion. The synthetic experiment calibrates the
shared-noise artifact and attenuation under quadratic motion. The simulated detector reports the true position
at $t-\tau_{\text{true}}$ with its own independent error, and the label centers
are corrupted at \labelSigmaGen\,px.

With $\tau_{\text{true}}=0$ the forward estimator returns \synFwd\,ms and the
backward one \synBack\,ms, summing to \synSum\,ms, against the closed form
\synPred\,ms, the centered estimator returns \synCen\,ms. Setting the label
noise to zero returns all three to \synZero\,ms, and the placebo stays at its
null throughout, so it does not absorb the artifact and remains usable on real
rows. In the synthetic physical-lag experiment, the centered estimator recovers
\synRecover{} of the injected lag, consistent with the predicted attenuation of
\synLambda{} for this noise.

\section{The output-time specification sweeps}
\label{sup:sweeps}

\paragraph{Lag recovery under the full control set.} Adding
$-\tau_{\mathrm{inj}}\tilde v$ to the observed residual creates a known
perturbation along the fitted temporal regressor. The coefficient is recovered
at \injRecovery{} of its injected value at \injLevels\,ms, showing that the
geometric controls do not absorb this regressor. Injecting instead a purely
geometric displacement of \injGeoGamma{} px per px of box height moves the
minimal specification's $\tau$ by \injGeoMin\,ms and the reported one's by less
than \injGeoFull\,ms.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to axis pairing.} The reported specification
pairs box height and image row with $\hat{y}$ and box width and image column with
$\hat{x}$. Entering all four on both axes gives
$\satTau\,\pm\,\satTauSE$\,ms. Dropping each in turn moves $\tau$ by under
0.3\,ms except for the image column, whose removal returns the placebo to its
inflated value. Raising the overlap floor to \satTightIoU{} gives
$\satTight\,\pm\,\satTightSE$\,ms, the tightest of the sweep. Across every
specification carrying geometric controls, including speed floors from 5 to
20\,px/s, $\tau$ spans \satLo{} to \satHi\,ms, and none of them reaches the
ablation-sensitivity centroid.

\paragraph{Association independent of nominal-time overlap.} The rows are built by
matching a detection to a label at the label's nominal time. A gate on overlap could in
principle discard exactly those detections whose temporal offset is large and select $\tau$
toward zero. Raising the floor cannot detect that, because it discards more. The informative
direction is to open the gate or to remove it. The analysis rebuilds
the rows from the five-checkpoint detection dump of Sec.~3 under \assocRules{} association
rules and refits the reported specification under each. The rules are greedy and Hungarian
assignment at overlap floors from 0.05 to 0.50. One rule never tests overlap at all, admitting
a detection whose center falls within a fixed multiple of the label's box scale. That dump is
re-thresholded to the regression's confidence floor of 0.1, but its non-maximum suppression
was run at 0.65 against the 0.45 of the dump behind Tab.~\ref{tab:outtime}. Re-thresholding does not
undo the looser suppression. The rebuild's own $0.30$ gate therefore keeps \assocGateN{} rows
against the \outMatches{} of the reported table and returns
$\assocGate\,\pm\,\assocGateSE$\,ms against
$\ladderFullTau\,\pm\,\ladderFullTauSE$\,ms, a difference of \assocGateVsTable{} standard
errors. The sweep is read within the rebuild, as a comparison across association rules on one
detection set. Across all \assocRules{} rules $\tau$ spans \assocLo{} to \assocHi\,ms, a
spread of \assocSpread\,ms against a median standard error of \assocSE\,ms. Every rule leaves $\tau$ within two standard errors of zero and leaves the
ablation-sensitivity centroid more than two standard errors away. The overlap-free rule admits
\assocFreeN{} rows against \assocGateN{} for the rebuild's own gate and gives
$\assocFree\,\pm\,\assocFreeSE$\,ms against $\assocGate\,\pm\,\assocGateSE$\,ms, a difference of
\assocDelta{} standard errors. Under the minimal specification the same sweep spans \assocMinLo{}
to \assocMinHi\,ms, so neither specification's conclusion is an artifact of the matching rule.

\section{The per-object ratio}
\label{sup:ratio}

For comparison with the estimator in Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg}, we evaluate the per-object ratio
$-\langle\hat{\mathbf c}-\mathbf c^{*},\hat{\mathbf u}\rangle/\lVert \tilde{\mathbf v}\rVert$
on the same \outMatches{} rows. Across these rows, it has a sample standard deviation
of \ratioSD\,ms and an
interquartile range of \ratioIQR\,ms. Its mean over all rows is
$\ratioMean\,\pm\,\ratioMeanSE$\,ms, close to the $+\rvtCentroidMagMeas$\,ms coefficient
corresponding to the newest-window ablation-sensitivity centroid under the convention
of Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg}. Above a \ratioFloorSpeed\,px/s floor it gives
$\ratioFloor\,\pm\,\ratioFloorSE$\,ms. Because the ratio has no intercept, a
velocity-independent spatial bias can appear as an apparent temporal offset after division
by speed. Eq.~\ref{eq:outreg} includes an intercept.

\section{The port of the released checkpoint}
\label{sup:port}

The released RVT-\texttt{t} checkpoint loads into a model built from the
repository's own configuration files with \rvtMissingKeys{} missing and
\rvtUnexpectedKeys{} unexpected keys at \rvtParams\,M parameters. It runs

from. Both predictor measurements run on that port. A key count is a structural
check, so two numerical ones are reported alongside it.

\subsection{Exposure and labels}
\label{sec:labelclock}

The predictor-side analysis concerns the temporal support of released detector
inputs and outputs. DSEC provides a separate exposure-side analysis. Published
exposure intervals bound the interval within which event timing is observed.
Within-exposure event timing contains illumination-locked structure and therefore cannot be
uniquely attributed to object timing without separating that structure.
DSEC~\cite{gehrig2021dsec} publishes
per-frame exposure metadata, and the files resolve
for
\dsecSeqCount{} train sequences. The width varies by a factor of
\dsecExpRatio{} against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms, and within
one recording by up to a factor of \dsecWithinSeqRatio{}. The auto-exposure
ceiling of \dsecExpMax\,\micro s holds \dsecPinnedFrac\,\% of frames, in
\dsecCeilingSeqs{} of the \dsecSeqCount{} sequences, and the distribution is not
continuous over that range (Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure}). The gap separates the two
exposure regimes, and ceiling exposure forms a distinct stratum.

The released DSEC-Det labels comprise \ddBoxes{} boxes on \ddTracks{} tracks
across \ddSeqs{} sequences. Every label sits on the frame clock, and the median
spacing between consecutive label times is \ddSpacingMedian\,\micro s. Over
\ddTriples{} triples of consecutive labels on a track,
\ddInterpBelowThresh\,\% of middle box centers fall within a hundredth of a
pixel of their neighbors' linear interpolant. This rules out exact local linear
interpolation as a general description of the released trajectories without
identifying the underlying annotation process. DSEC-Det ships more than one annotation
version~\cite{beyondduality2026}, Sec.~\ref{sup:evidencetime} names the one measured here.
Median box-center image speed is \labelSpeedMedian\,px/s, with
\labelSpeedPninetynine\,px/s at the ninety-ninth percentile.

\subsection{Within-box temporal dispersion under that signature}
\label{sec:cost}

That signature limits attribution of within-exposure event timing to object timing. Within-box dispersion is \sigmaEvtNight\,\micro s against a null of
\sigmaEvtNullNight\,\micro s, with an excess of \sigmaEvtExcessNight\,\micro s.
A frame-level permutation preserving pooled temporal distributions and box counts gives a
null of \permNull\,\micro s, and the excess remains after the clean-pixel control.
Random-position boxes show that the dispersion is not specific to annotated object regions
(Sec.~\ref{sup:perm}), which supports reading the signature as an attribution limit and not as
object motion.

\paragraph{Data path.} For each labeled frame of a sequence, the frame's own
exposure interval $[a,b]$ is read from the dataset's published
\texttt{image\_exposure\_timestamps\_left} file. The event stream is sliced to
that interval through the release's \texttt{ms\_to\_idx} index, and the events
inside each released box are collected. The \texttt{ms\_to\_idx} index resolves to
whole milliseconds, so the slice it returns is filtered to $a\le t<b$ in
microseconds before any statistic is formed, and the analytic null is computed for
the same interval. Events and frames share one clock in this dataset, so no
calibration step enters. For the dispersion of Sec.~\ref{sec:cost} a box contributes
only if it carries at least \minEvents{} events in the window, and a frame only
if at least \minObjects{} boxes qualify. The reported statistics are medians over
frames of a within-frame quantity, so no frame is weighted by its object count.
For the per-box statistics of Tab.~\ref{tab:sixseq} a box contributes with at
least \sixMinEvents{} events and no frame-level requirement applies. The selected
qualitative frame is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}, and the paired
daytime control is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:daynight}.

For frame $f$, let $I_f$ be the qualifying boxes, let $N_{fi}$ be the event count
inside box $i$, and let $\bar t_{fi}=N_{fi}^{-1}\sum_{k\in i}t_k$ be its event-time
centroid relative to the frame's mid-exposure. The observed within-frame dispersion
and its frame-specific analytic null are
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_f&=\left[\frac{1}{|I_f|-1}\sum_{i\in I_f}
 (\bar t_{fi}-\bar t_f)^2\right]^{1/2},\\
n_f&=\left[\frac{1}{|I_f|}\sum_{i\in I_f}
 \frac{w_f^2}{12N_{fi}}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\bar t_f=|I_f|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_f}\bar t_{fi}$ and $w_f=b_f-a_f$.
The frame-level excess is $e_f=[\max(d_f^2-n_f^2,0)]^{1/2}$, and the reported
entries are the medians of $d_f$, $n_f$, and $e_f$ over eligible frames.

\paragraph{The stereo exposures.} The left and right exposures open at the same microsecond
in \dsecCotriggerPct\,\% of the \dsecCotriggerFrames{} frames checked and differ only in
when they close. The published image timestamp equals the mean of the two
mid-exposures to within \dsecTsResidual\,\micro s.

\paragraph{Archive version and track association.} DSEC-Det ships more than
one annotation version. The numbers in Sec.~\ref{sec:labelclock} describe the archive
\texttt{dsec-det\_left\_object\_detections.zip}, \ddArchiveBytes{} bytes. Whether those labels are tracker output on the RGB
frames, which the dataset's own description states, is not distinguishable from
human annotation by a second-difference residual. On the Gen1 side the
\texttt{track\_id} field is a per-frame index instead of a persistent identifier.
Association for the output-time fit is rebuilt by same-class overlap between
adjacent label times, at which \outLinkPct\,\% of boxes link.

\paragraph{Null.} For events uniform on $[a,b]$ the mean of $N_{i}$ times has
variance $w^{2}/12N_{i}$ with $w=b-a$. Each frame therefore carries its own null,
formed from the published width and the observed counts. The excess of
Sec.~\ref{sec:cost} is summarized over frames rather than formed from the medians of the two
summaries. That is why the excess column of Tab.~\ref{tab:controls} is not
$(\mathrm{sd}^{2}-\mathrm{null}^{2})^{1/2}$ of the two columns beside it.

\paragraph{Phase test.} The per-pixel test at frequency $f$ takes the event phases
$\phi_{k}=2\pi f t_{k} \bmod 2\pi$ of a pixel, forms the resultant length $R$ and
reports $Z=R^{2}/N$, which is $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ under uniform phase. Raw
modulation depth is not usable at these counts, because for $N$ events at random
phase the resultant is already of the size of the effect. Pixels enter with at
least \flickerMinEvents{} events over the tested slice, and every fourth event is
used to reduce sample size. The off-frequency and daytime controls remain close
to their nominal reference under the same procedure. Two arms hold the estimator to its null, an
off-frequency arm on the same events and the same frequency on daytime events.
The fraction of locked pixels is reported relative to live pixels in Sec.~\ref{sec:line} and
separately relative to the full sensor. The two denominators differ, and each statement
identifies its denominator.

\paragraph{The per-box statistics of Tab.~\ref{tab:sixseq}.} The same estimator is applied
to the events inside one released box during one published exposure window rather
than to the events of one pixel over a long slice. For box $i$ the depth is
$m=2|C|$ and the statistic is $Z=N_{i}|C|^{2}$ with
$\omega=2\pi f$ and $C=N_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k\in i}e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t_{k}}$, phases referred to that
frame's mid-exposure instant. A finite exposure does not produce uniform phase
at a tested frequency, so the pixel-level $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ null is not assumed
for these per-box values. The depth is reported descriptively, without a
random-phase floor. Boxes are matched to exposure windows by nearest mid-exposure
instant within half a frame period. The off-frequency arm at
\flickerCtrlHz\,Hz is run on exactly the same events. The per-box 137-Hz arm is used as a
same-event descriptive control, not as an independent spectral-resolution test. The
illumination attribution is supported primarily by the long-slice 100-Hz harmonic structure
and the daytime control.

\paragraph{Pixel exclusion and modulation strata.} The exclusion mask of
Table~\ref{tab:controls} is the set of pixels reaching $p<10^{-3}$ in the
per-pixel test. The
matched control draws a random pixel set of exactly the same size, so the two
arms remove the same number of pixels. The mask is built from a six-second slice
at every fourth event. The stratification uses no mask. The quartiles of $m$ are
taken over all \modBoxes{} qualifying boxes, and a frame contributes to a stratum
when at least \minObjects{} of its boxes fall inside it. The frame
counts of that block are therefore not a partition of the first row's.

\section{The permutation null}
\label{sup:perm}

The analytic null $w^{2}/12N_{i}$ for the Sec.~\ref{sec:cost} statistic is the variance of a mean of
$N_{i}$ draws uniform on the exposure. It assumes the events of a box arrive
independently and uniformly.

The permutation null does not assume independent or uniform event arrivals. Instead,
it conditions on the pooled frame-level event times and each box's event count while
randomizing box assignment. For each frame the events of all qualifying boxes are pooled
and reassigned to boxes at random, preserving each box's count. The exposure, the flicker and the scene's burstiness
all survive that reassignment, only which box an event belongs to is destroyed.
Over \permFrames{} frames and \permDraws{} draws each it returns
\permNull\,\micro s, \permRatio{} times the analytic null rather than above it,
and \permAbovePct\,\% of frames exceed their own permutation null. A burst common to the frame
displaces every $\bar{t}_{i}$ in the same direction and cancels in their
dispersion, which is why the statistic was already insensitive to it. For a separate
random-position draw evaluated against the permutation null, boxes of the same shapes give
\permRandObs\,\micro s against \permRandNull\,\micro s. Thus the dispersion is not specific
to annotated object regions, although object regions may still affect it.
