\title{Temporal-Support Identifiability\\
in Event-Camera Detection Evaluation Pipelines}
\author{Anonymous CVPR submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An event-camera detection benchmark identifies each scored sample by one nominal timestamp, yet a recurrent detector forms its prediction from a longer history whose length the benchmark never records. We state the missing requirement as \emph{temporal-support identifiability}: the variables a protocol records with a score must uniquely determine the temporal evidence behind every scored prediction. The released SSM-ViT evaluation pipeline is the case study: its reported score is not determined by the nominal timestamp alone, because it also depends on a temporal support that the benchmark does not specify. With frames, weights, labels, evaluator and evaluated timestamps fixed, moving only the chunk boundary of the streaming evaluation changes the recurrent history behind the same frames from 1--4 to 17--20 windows, and changes the SSM-ViT base checkpoint (S5-B) by \ssmPairedMapAllBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} mAP over all labeled boxes and by \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset; the released score pools histories of 1 to \ssmEvalChunk{} windows. The same intervention leaves RVT unchanged when its state is carried and moves it by \rvtBoundResetS--\rvtBoundResetB{} mAP when the state is reset, so the effect follows the reset policy of each architecture. Because temporal-support identifiability is a uniqueness property, one controlled pair with identical recorded variables and different scores is sufficient to show that the protocol under-specifies its score. Support-Conditioned AP specifies the support: every checkpoint is scored from the same reset state with the history fixed to $H$ windows. On five released Gen1 checkpoints, S5-B is third in the pooled score and first at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$, with every pairwise gap to it excluding zero, and Kendall's $\tau$ between the pooled and fixed-$H$ orderings is at most \fhTauHTwentyone{}. Predictor-side and label-side audits show that input timing and label-acquisition timing are left implicit by the same timestamp. We list five fields a benchmark can record with its labels so that the support behind a score is identifiable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Two recurrent detectors evaluated at the same nominal timestamp are not comparable in time unless the history each one actually used is also identified.

An event-camera detection benchmark defines a sample by a nominal timestamp. A prediction $\hat{s}$ is scored against the ground truth $s^{*}$ carried at query time $t$ through a function $m(\hat{s},s^{*})$ of position, shape and class. Beyond selecting the label at $t$, that function contains no variable for the temporal evidence behind either argument, so any temporal discrepancy enters the score disguised as displacement.

A recurrent detector does not read only the instant the benchmark names. It computes $\hat{s}$
from an input window and from state carried across earlier windows. The label it is
compared against comes from an exposure of finite width, an annotator or tracker working on the
exposed image, and a geometric transfer into another sensor's frame. The evidence on both sides
is an interval, while the protocol records an instant.

We use three terms. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant at which the protocol pairs a prediction with a label. The \emph{effective temporal support} is the past that can influence the prediction, including state carried by recurrence. The \emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired. The \emph{protocol record} is the set of variables a benchmark specifies and a paper reports with a score: frames, labels, weights, evaluator, input window, nominal timestamps and published acquisition metadata. A protocol is \emph{temporal-support identifiable} when its record determines the effective temporal support of every scored prediction uniquely. A quantity that can be reconstructed from released code or index files, but is absent from the record, does not meet this condition: two evaluations that agree on the entire record can still differ in it, and nothing reported with the score tells them apart.

We test it with a paired intervention on the released SSM-ViT evaluation pipeline, using its base and small checkpoints (S5-B, S5-S). In that
implementation the nominally carried state does not enter the outputs across evaluation chunks. The recurrent
history available to a prediction is a deterministic function of chunk position, and ranges from
1 to \ssmEvalChunk{} windows inside one pooled score. Moving every chunk boundary
\ssmPairedShift{} windows later re-scores the same frames under a longer history at the same
evaluated timestamp, with frames, weights, labels and evaluator fixed
(\ssmPairedN{} labeled frames scored twice, cluster bootstrap over \outSeqsScanned{} sequences; $\pm$ denotes one cluster-bootstrap standard error).

Effective temporal support alone changes S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} mAP over all labeled
boxes and \ssmPairedMapVelBase\,$\pm$\,\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable
subset (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). The second checkpoint of the same released pipeline, S5-S, moves by
\ssmPairedMapAllSmall{} and \ssmPairedMapVelSmall{} points. Since temporal-support identifiability is a uniqueness property, this controlled pair is sufficient to show that the nominal timestamp does not uniquely specify the temporal evidence represented by that score. The consequence is at the level of the protocol, not of one implementation: where the protocol does not record the support behind a score, the reported score is underdetermined. The same intervention on RVT leaves it unchanged while its state is carried and moves it by \rvtBoundResetS{} and \rvtBoundResetB{} points once the state is reset at the boundary, so the effect belongs to the reset policy.

The controlled pair has a constructive counterpart. Support-Conditioned AP scores a checkpoint with the recurrent history fixed to a stated number of windows $H$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:sc}). Applied to five released Gen1 checkpoints on \fhNCommon{} common frames, it orders them differently from the pooled score: S5-B is third in the pooled score and first at each of $H=1,5,10,21$, and for three pairs the pooled and $H=1$ gaps have intervals that exclude zero on opposite sides.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Temporal-support identifiability}: an evaluation property separating the nominal
timestamp a protocol records from the effective temporal support and label-acquisition support a
reported score represents.
\item A paired intervention that isolates recurrent support. With frames, weights, labels, evaluator and evaluated timestamp fixed, only the chunk boundary changes, and the S5-B score moves by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP overall and \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset. The same intervention on RVT follows the reset policy.
\item A protocol, Support-Conditioned AP, which fixes the recurrent history behind each score, applied to five released checkpoints, together with five fields a benchmark can record so that the support behind a score is identifiable.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Sensor and runtime latency.}
Work on event-camera timing takes the benchmark's temporal index as given and improves what is measured against it. Sensor latency is calibrated away~\cite{yang2024latency,liu2024nighttime}, compute latency is folded into average precision~\cite{li2020streaming,yang2022streamyolo,chu2026stare}, delay is scored separately~\cite{mao2019delay}, and temporal tolerance has been proposed~\cite{softed2024}. Each changes what the predictor does at a fixed benchmark index. The object measured here is that index itself and the support a score reported under it pools: we test whether a released benchmark specification uniquely determines the temporal quantities behind its score.

\paragraph{Temporal windows and event benchmarks.}
RVT and SSM-ViT redesign or parameterize temporal windows
\cite{gehrig2023rvt,zubic2024ssm}, and adaptive-window methods compare
alternative partitions \cite{sui2026astw}. Gen1, 1\,Mpx, DSEC-Det and Ev-3DOD use asynchronous measurements, rectified frames, or interpolated labels \cite{detournemire2020gen1,perot2020megapixel,gehrig2024lowlatency,cho2025ev3dod}, and the event count inside one labeled box can be too small to support detection~\cite{kugele2023howmany}. Event--RGB frequency mismatch and exposure duration have also been noted \cite{faod2024,sun2023refid,kim2022unknownexposure}. LEOD~\cite{wu2024leod} states that event detectors take only events triggered before the query time and builds a time-flip augmentation on that premise. It does not record where inside that window the evidence sits. Both released detector implementations leave a frame's position inside the evaluation chunk unspecified by the protocol. The recurrent state entering a chunk is the state saved at the end of the previous chunk, and is reset only at the first sample of a sequence. Under streaming sampling every timestep emits predictions, while otherwise only the last does (RVT \texttt{modules/detection.py}:232,\,243 and SSM-ViT :315,\,318).

\paragraph{Benchmark reliability and evaluation analysis.}
Prior work studies detector calibration, stratified model evaluation, label-induced benchmark
instability, and event-vision evaluation protocols
\cite{oncalibration2024,stratification2024,northcutt2021labelerrors,ercan2023evreal}.
These analyses examine uncertainty or error in reported performance, whereas our focus is the
temporal support and acquisition semantics that a nominal detection timestamp leaves implicit.

\paragraph{Label time and attribution.}
Waymo and annotation studies estimate per-object capture or annotation offsets
\cite{waymolabelproto,sun2020waymo,nunez2026annotations}. Event datasets expose
per-box time fields, but their degrees of freedom are not necessarily specified.
LET-3D-AP and TIDE/DETAD/HOTA decompose or bucket spatial error while taking the
label clock as given \cite{hung2022let,bolya2020tide,hoiem2012diagnosing,alwassel2018detad,luiten2021hota}.

\section{Primary evidence: the same-frame chunk-boundary intervention}
\label{sec:chunk}

The state the SSM-ViT release carries between evaluation chunks does not affect any of its
outputs. It enters as a multiplicative factor on the first coefficient of an associative scan
on which the outputs do not depend. Under the released streaming evaluation this bounds the
recurrent history available to a detection to the current \ssmEvalChunk{}-window chunk. That
history ranges from 1 window to \ssmEvalChunk{} with the detection's position in the chunk,
and the reported number averages over all \ssmEvalChunk{} positions. Chunk position is a
deterministic function of the release's own index files and is recorded nowhere in the
evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{The same-frame intervention.}
The intervention moves every chunk boundary \ssmPairedShift{} windows later and changes nothing
else: the same frames, the same weights, the same labels, the same evaluator, and the evaluated
timestamp of each frame unchanged. \ssmPairedN{} labeled frames are scored twice under this
shift, once with 1--4 windows of recurrent history and once with 17--20.
They are compared paired within frame with a cluster bootstrap over the \outSeqsScanned{}
sequences. The long-history arm gains
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} mAP points for S5-B and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{} for S5-S on the velocity-evaluable subset,
and over every labeled box the S5-B gain is
\ssmPairedMapAllBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapAllBaseSE{} points. Mean detection confidence, which uses
no labels, moves with it by \ssmPairedConfBase$\pm$\ssmPairedConfBaseSE{} and
\ssmPairedConfSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedConfSmallSE{}. Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos} (bottom) draws one such
pair. No quantity the protocol records distinguishes the two arms.

\paragraph{Graded response and what moves.}
The size of the boundary shift is a free parameter. Re-scoring the same \ssmDoseN{} frames after
shifting the boundary so that the starved block gains $+6$, $+11$ and $+16$ windows of history
changes the velocity-subset mAP of S5-B by \ssmDoseBaseSix$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseSixSE{},
\ssmDoseBaseEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseBaseElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelBase$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelBaseSE{} points, and that of S5-S by
\ssmDoseSmallSix$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallSixSE{}, \ssmDoseSmallEleven$\pm$\ssmDoseSmallElevenSE{} and
\ssmPairedMapVelSmall$\pm$\ssmPairedMapVelSmallSE{}. The zero-shift control, which re-runs the
release's own boundary placement, returns \ssmDoseNull{}. Confidence on detections matched to a
label rises by \ssmPairedConfTpBase{} and \ssmPairedConfTpSmall{}, and total output falls by
\ssmPairedDpfBase{} and \ssmPairedDpfSmall{} boxes per frame, so both terms of precision move.
Tab.~\ref{tab:evidence} lists all four amounts.

\paragraph{The observational contrast across chunk positions.}
Reconstructing chunk position over \mapFrames{} labeled frames of the released evaluation and
scoring every labeled box, S5-B reaches \chunkShortBase{} mAP on the frames given 1--4
windows and \chunkFullBase{} on those given 17--\ssmEvalChunk{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chunkpos}, top). This contrast compares different frames, so it is differenced
against the three RVT checkpoints, evaluated on the same frames with a state that crosses chunk
boundaries. That controls for frame-position effects shared with them. It leaves
\chunkDidBase$\pm$\chunkDidBaseSE{} points for S5-B ($z=\chunkDidBaseZ$) and
\chunkDidSmall$\pm$\chunkDidSmallSE{} for S5-S ($z=\chunkDidSmallZ$), against placebo
differences among the RVT checkpoints of at most \chunkPlaceboMax{} points
($z=\chunkPlaceboZ$). Permuting chunk position within each sequence puts both SSM values far outside their own null, whose standard deviation is about \chunkPermSd{} points, and every placebo inside
it. The two readings agree in sign and magnitude, and the paired intervention carries the claim
because it holds the frames fixed (Supplement Sec.~1).

\paragraph{RVT under the same intervention.}
That differencing treats the RVT checkpoints as a control because their state survives a chunk
boundary. The same-frame intervention applied to RVT measures that property. Driven on the SSM
chunk grid, RVT reproduces its stored per-window detections bit for bit at zero shift, and the
same \rvtBoundN{} frames are re-scored with every boundary moved \rvtBoundShift{} windows
later. With the released state carried across the boundary the paired change is
$\rvtBoundCarryS\pm\rvtBoundCarrySSE$ points for RVT-\texttt{s} ($z=\rvtBoundCarrySZ$) and
$\rvtBoundCarryB\pm\rvtBoundCarryBSE$ for RVT-\texttt{b} ($z=\rvtBoundCarryBZ$). Dropping
the state at every chunk start, the condition the SSM release is already in, moves the same
weights on the same frames by $\rvtBoundResetS\pm\rvtBoundResetSSE$ ($z=\rvtBoundResetSZ$)
and $\rvtBoundResetB\pm\rvtBoundResetBSE$ points ($z=\rvtBoundResetBZ$). Random-frame
nulls are centered within \rvtBoundPermMax{} points of zero. The boundary effect follows whether the
recurrent state survives the boundary, and the control the
difference-in-differences relies on holds as a measurement (Supplement Sec.~1).

\paragraph{What the pooled score reports.}
One released number pools detections whose recurrent history differs by a factor of \ssmEvalChunk{}. Restricting evaluation to the frames that receive 17--\ssmEvalChunk{} windows gives an S5-B score \chunkGapBase{} points above the pooled released score, and \chunkGapSmall{} points for S5-S. For scale, the frequency-generalization result that motivates the SSM-ViT design is a 3.31 mAP drop~\cite{zubic2024ssm}; an unrecorded chunk boundary moves the same model by a comparable amount at a fixed frequency.

\section{Supporting audits}
\label{sec:support}

Two audits delimit how the intervention of Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} is read. Each measures a
timing descriptor that the nominal timestamp does not encode, and neither is used as a recurrent-support estimator. Their derivations, figures and tables are in
Supplement Secs.~5 and 14.

\paragraph{Protocol record.}
Call a quantity \emph{protocol-identifiable} when the protocol record of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} determines it uniquely. The input window and the published exposure interval are in the record. The effective predictor timing, the recurrent history pooled in a score, and the attribution of event timing within an exposure are not.

\paragraph{Predictor side.}
The released RVT configuration~\cite{gehrig2023rvt} indexes each \rvtBins-bin, \rvtWindow\,ms input
window by its end, and places that end on a label time. Ablating each bin of the released RVT-\texttt{t} checkpoint over \rvtInfluenceSamples{} Gen1
validation samples puts the ablation-sensitivity
centroid at \rvtCentroidMeas\,ms. A regression of detection-center residuals on label velocity,
which carries the temporal calibration of~\cite{qin2018temporal} to a box center and is fitted on
nominal-time-matched detections, gives a coefficient of
\ladderFullTau\,$\pm$\,\ladderFullTauSE\,ms, compatible with zero. Over a 1000\,ms horizon the newest window accounts for 22.7\,\% of the
summed window-ablation sensitivity, so the newest-window centroid does not describe the full
recurrent support (Supplement Secs.~5, 6 and 8).

\paragraph{Label side.}
Across the \dsecSeqCount{} DSEC training sequences whose exposure files
resolve~\cite{gehrig2021dsec}, published exposure width varies by a factor of \dsecExpRatio{}
against a frame period fixed at \dsecFramePeriod\,ms. Within-box dispersion of event time inside one exposure is
\sigmaEvtNight\,\micro s against an analytic null of \sigmaEvtNullNight\,\micro s, so within-exposure event timing contains an illumination-locked component and cannot be
attributed to object timing without separating that component first (Supplement Secs.~14 and 16).
DSEC-Det ships more than one annotation version~\cite{beyondduality2026}, and Supplement Sec.~15
names the one measured here.

\section{Support-conditioned AP}
\label{sec:sc}

Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk} shows that one released protocol pools detections that received different histories. This section scores the \rankCkpts{} released Gen1 checkpoints at a stated history and compares the result with the pooled score.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
For a labeled frame scored at window $r$ and a history length $H$, the detector starts from a reset recurrent state at window $r-H+1$ and runs through window $r$, and the detections of window $r$ are scored with the labels, confidence threshold, NMS and evaluator of the released-protocol dumps. We call the resulting AP at fixed $H$ Support-Conditioned AP. $H$ counts the windows read up to and including the scored window; we write $H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ for this requested length. A frame with fewer than $H$ windows available receives $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=\min(H,r+1)$ windows instead. All checkpoints are scored on the same \fhNCommon{} frames, those with $H_{\mathrm{forced}}=H_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ at every $H\in\{1,5,10,21\}$. The other \fhNExcl{} of the \fhNAll{} labeled frames have fewer than \ssmEvalChunk{} windows of history and are excluded. The common frames lie in \fhNSeq{} sequences. The column \emph{pooled} is the released evaluation of each checkpoint, in which the history depends on the release and on the frame, restricted to the same common frames. The score of the released evaluation on all \fhNAll{} frames is listed separately. The access log of every run shows that the number of distinct windows read equals $H_{\mathrm{forced}}$ and that the first window read is $r-H_{\mathrm{forced}}+1$ in every cell of every checkpoint (Supplement Sec.~1.4).

\paragraph{Result.}
Table~\ref{tab:sc} lists the scores. On the common frames the pooled ordering is RVT-\texttt{b}, RVT-\texttt{s}, S5-B, RVT-\texttt{t}, S5-S. At $H=1$ the ordering is S5-B, S5-S, RVT-\texttt{b}, RVT-\texttt{s}, RVT-\texttt{t}, and at $H=21$ it is S5-B, RVT-\texttt{b}, RVT-\texttt{s}, S5-S, RVT-\texttt{t}. Kendall's $\tau$ between the pooled ordering and the orderings at $H=1$, 5, 10 and 21 is \fhTauHOne{}, \fhTauHFive{}, \fhTauHTen{} and \fhTauHTwentyone{}. At every $H$, each pairwise gap between S5-B and another checkpoint has a 95\,\% interval that excludes zero (Tab.~\ref{tab:scpairs}), so its first rank does not depend on the bootstrap draw. The pooled ordering, by contrast, is reproduced in \fhReproPool\,\% of draws on the common frames and in \rankHoldBox\,\% over all labeled boxes (Supplement Sec.~4).

Several pairwise gaps change sign. RVT-\texttt{t} minus S5-S is \fhgap{Rt}{Ss}{Pool} points in the pooled score and \fhgap{Rt}{Ss}{HOne} at $H=1$. RVT-\texttt{s} minus S5-S is \fhgap{Rs}{Ss}{Pool} pooled and \fhgap{Rs}{Ss}{HOne} at $H=1$. \paragraph{What Support-Conditioned AP equalizes.}
The access log confirms that exactly $H$ windows are read, and replacing the oldest admitted window changes the output in \fhProbeHit{} of \fhProbeN{} probed cells (\fhProbeHitT{} for RVT-\texttt{t}), so the full admitted history reaches the detection. Every checkpoint starts from the same reset state. The protocol therefore equalizes the two things the pooled score leaves free, the reset policy and the history length. Under the released evaluations these differ by family: the RVT release carries its state across a sequence, while the SSM-ViT release scores each chunk from a reset state (Sec.~\ref{sec:chunk}). This is why the pooled RVT scores sit above their $H=\ssmEvalChunk{}$ scores (RVT-\texttt{b}: \fhMapRbPool{} against \fhMapRbHTwentyone) and the pooled S5 scores sit below theirs (S5-B: \fhMapSbPool{} against \fhMapSbHTwentyone). The pooled column compares checkpoints that were given different support. Table~\ref{tab:sc} compares them at the same support, which is the comparison a timing benchmark is taken to make. The ordering change is a result for these five checkpoints under this evaluation and does not rank the architectures.

\paragraph{What a benchmark should record.}
A nominal timestamp does not determine the effective temporal support or the label-acquisition support, and the intervention above shows a case in which the unrecorded support changes the score. We propose that a benchmark record, for each scored sample, five fields. The \emph{nominal timestamp} is the instant at which the sample is scored. The \emph{input window} is what the detector reads at that timestamp. The \emph{state-reset policy} states where the recurrent state is cleared, including chunk boundaries and sequence starts. The \emph{effective history length} is the number of windows read, as the access log of the evaluation run records it. The \emph{label-acquisition support} is the interval over which the label's evidence was acquired. Together, the reset policy and the history length determine the effective temporal support. Tab.~\ref{tab:fields} fills in these fields for the two releases examined here. A score is then reported at a declared history length, as in Tab.~\ref{tab:sc}, together with the distribution of history lengths that the pooled score contains. These fields do not equalize the support across checkpoints. They record it, so that scores can be compared at a history that both detectors received.

\section{Conclusion}

With frames, weights, labels, evaluator and evaluated timestamps fixed, moving only the chunk boundary shifts S5-B by \ssmPairedMapAllBase{} mAP over all labeled validation boxes and by \ssmPairedMapVelBase{} mAP on the velocity-evaluable subset. The chunk boundary is a degree of freedom the protocol does not record. It changes the recurrent support behind a reported score while every recorded quantity stays fixed, which is an instance where temporal-support identifiability does not hold. RVT shows the same effect as soon as its state is reset at the boundary, so the effect belongs to the reset policy and not to one architecture.

Support-Conditioned AP removes this degree of freedom by fixing the reset state and the history behind every score. On five released Gen1 checkpoints, S5-B moves from third in the pooled score to first at every $H$, Kendall's $\tau$ between the pooled and fixed-$H$ orderings stays at or below \fhTauHTwentyone{}, and three pairwise gaps reverse sign with intervals that exclude zero on both sides.

Two scores at the same nominal timestamp are comparable in time only when the support behind them is the same. A benchmark can make this checkable by recording five fields for each scored sample (nominal timestamp, input window, state-reset policy, effective history length, label-acquisition support) and by reporting scores at a declared history length.
