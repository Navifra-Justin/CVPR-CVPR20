### The five most consequential FAILs

**1. Item 162 / 93 / G11 — the anisotropic estimator is fitted on magnitudes but its
calibration is derived for signed residuals, and the reported lag does not follow.**
`src/e24_order.py:141–142` fits `lsq(X, np.abs(epar))` and `lsq(X, np.abs(eperp))`.
`supplement.tex:145–148` justifies the reading with *"A displacement of the prediction by
$d$ along the track adds $d$ to $e_{\parallel}$ and nothing to $e_{\perp}$"* — true for the
signed residual, false for its magnitude. For $e_\parallel = d + \varepsilon$ with
$\varepsilon$ symmetric, $\partial\,\mathbb{E}|e_\parallel|/\partial d = 1-2F_\varepsilon(-d)$,
which is **zero at $d=0$**; the leading term is $f_\varepsilon(0)\,d^{2}$. The paper's own
numbers put this deep in the small-$d$ regime: $d=\tau\lVert v\rVert = 0.32$ px at the median
speed (`\pxAniGen`, 424) against an intercept of $-3.427$ px and $-0.695$ px (Table 1). So
the speed-row difference of $+0.0166$ s cannot be read off as $\tau$, and
*"Read as a zeroth-order lag, that difference is +16.6 ms"* (419–420) is not derivable from
the construction stated in `supplement.tex:137–150`. Corroborating signal that the model is
misspecified: **both** along- and cross-track speed coefficients are negative
($-0.0146$, $-0.0312$ s) and **both** intercepts are negative ($-3.427$, $-0.695$ px) for a
response that cannot be negative. The paper prints all four values in Table 1 and never
addresses either fact in prose. This is the load-bearing number of the lead contribution and
it appears six times (55, 100, 131, 420, 447, 778).

**2. Item 106 / 107 / G8 — the influence centroid measures the current input window only,
while the network is recurrent, and the paper never says so.**
`src/e17_bin_influence.py:57–67` warms the recurrent state, records `ref`, then re-runs
`mdl.forward(xm, previous_states=states)` with the **same** `states` and one bin zeroed. The
occlusion therefore perturbs only the current 50 ms step; every earlier window survives in
the carried hidden state and contributes evidence the measurement cannot see. The centroid
$-24.94$ ms is consequently an upper bound on how recent the network's evidence is, not the
evidence centroid. The paper states it flatly as the latter — abstract 46–49, Sec. 3.6
*"Its input evidence is centered 24.94 ms before the label time"* (441–442), conclusion
769–771 — and the two bounding properties it does list (307–309, 753–755, `supplement.tex:118–123`)
are the linear-weight caveat and the partial-derivative caveat, not this one.

**3. Item 100 / 101 / G5 — "evidence time" names two unrelated quantities.**
The title and abstract use it for the predictor's influence-weighted centroid on Gen1
(*"Evidence Time and Output Time of a Released Event Detector"*, 32; 46–49). Equation 2 at
586–591 defines *"The **evidence time** of the box"* as $\bar t_i$, the mean event time of a
DSEC box inside one exposure window minus the mid-exposure instant. These are different
sensors, different datasets, different units of aggregation, and different magnitudes
(24.94 ms vs 183.6 μs). Fig. 3's y-axis and Fig. 4(b)'s x-axis both read
"evidence time $\bar t_i$", so a reader arriving from the title reads the second meaning off
the first figure that shows it.

**4. Items 109 / 111 / 158 / 185 / G9 — the figure budget inverts the paper's own stated
priority, and the one figure of the lead result draws it as the smallest bar.**
The abstract leads with the predictor side. Figure inventory: Fig. 1 (`fig1_dsec_exposure.pdf`,
**full width**, p. 3) → benchmark premise; Fig. 2 (`fig_predictor.pdf`, **single column**,
p. 4) → predictor, the only one; Fig. 3 (`fig3_day_night.pdf`, single column, p. 6) → left
panel is the demoted dispersion, right panel the per-pixel flicker; Fig. 4
(`fig5_qualitative.pdf`, **full width**, p. 7) → panels (a) and (b) are the demoted
dispersion, only (c) is the benchmark claim. So the lead contribution holds one of four
figures and roughly one column-width of about six, while the quantity the paper explicitly
declines to claim (*"It is not evidence that the objects of one frame are captured at
different times"*, 715–716) holds a full-width panel pair plus half of Fig. 3. Worse, inside
Fig. 2(b) the lead result — the label-speed row at 3.34 SE — is drawn as **the shortest of
four bars**, beside 16.94 and 26.37. A reviewer reconstructing the paper from its figures
reconstructs the mains result and misses the predictor result.

**5. Item 104 / G7 / 124 — the checkpoint every predictor number depends on is never shown
to reproduce its published score, and one printed number matches no artifact on disk.**
Line 363 says *"The port both predictor measurements run on is verified"* and then offers
only key-loading evidence (0 missing, 0 unexpected, 4.41 M params). `grep -rn "mAP"` over
`experiments/` returns exactly one hit, `e04_rvt_port_risk/README.md:96`:
*"Still not established: that the loaded model **reproduces the published mAP**. Weights
loading cleanly is necessary, not sufficient."* No mAP is reported anywhere in the
repository. Separately, `\flickerPeakRatioDay` = `6.6` (`numbers.tex:246`) matches neither
`experiments/e10_flicker/README.md:14` (`6.4`) nor `e10_flicker/result.json`
(`line_ratio_100Hz: 6.385`); and `\flickerPeakRatio` = `10 841` exists only in the
hand-written README table at line 13, while the machine artifact holds `48694.204`.
